## Ranked papers

### 1. **Posterior Matching Distillation**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow distillation and distribution-aware supervision. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26629), Hidir Yesiltepe, Julius Berner, Arash Vahdat, and Pinar Yanardag; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26629), [project](https://pmd-distillation.github.io/).
- **Mechanism:** For Gaussian corruption, exponentially tilting the clean posterior by exp(lambda-transpose x) is equivalent to shifting the noisy teacher input by sigma-squared/alpha times lambda. Match softmax-weighted means of stochastic student outputs against those shifted teacher denoisings. A family of tilted means can identify a distribution, unlike a single conditional mean. The implementation reuses four student draws across four teacher tilt queries, applies jackknife/cross-fit finite-sample correction, and adds a caption-conditioned GAN loss.
- **Why it matters:** A cheap posterior probe can reveal information that ordinary mean regression discards. This is a transferable supervision design, not merely a shorter sampler. Its strongest lesson is also negative: matching an unguided teacher posterior and producing desirable guided-looking samples are different objectives.
- **Evidence:** At four steps, SDXL FID is 19.14 versus DMD2's 19.20 on COCO-10k, while GenEval is lower, 0.5539 versus 0.5882. On 1,632 PartiPrompts with eight complete generations each, SDXL Vendi is 4.4621 versus DMD2's 2.5178. FLUX.2 [klein] 4B reaches Vendi 3.3708 versus the base four-step model's 2.3237. Same-stochastic-architecture controls show PMD-only Vendi 5.112 but poor FID 56.13; stochastic DMD has Vendi 2.572 and FID 28.85. Posterior coverage and visual quality are visibly distinct.
- **Catch:** Exact posterior recovery requires an exact mean-denoising teacher, population expectations, and sufficiently rich tilt coverage; four draws and coarse spatial probes do not establish that guarantee. The GAN deliberately changes the posterior-matching optimum. Diversity metrics vary whole-generation seeds, not just the extra student randomness at a fixed noisy state. A 0.06 FID difference is not demonstrated statistical superiority. Training uses eight L40 GPUs, 2,000 student updates and 10,000 discriminator updates; one teacher query per tilt is not one query for the whole update.
- **Read scope:** Main identity, identification argument, objective, sampling, and image results; Appendix A.2 identification proof and C implementation/finite-sample correction; selected D/E ablations, F human-study protocol, and H video summary. Other appendix interpretations were selectively inspected, not exhaustively audited. PDF-based note; no implementation reproduction. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/posterior_matching_distillation.md).

### 2. **Direct3D-V3: Latent-Free Diffusion Transformers in the Native Voxel Domain**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant 3D generative modeling and explicit geometric interfaces. **Verdict:** Useful architecture and supervision recipe; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26511), Shuang Wu and colleagues; ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH Asia 2026 according to the manuscript; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26511), [project](https://dreamtechai.github.io/Direct3D-V3).
- **Mechanism:** Diffuse raw sparse truncated-SDF values rather than learned VAE latents. A patch-level DiT handles global structure; a lightweight voxel-level branch restores detail. Add a neighboring-voxel Lipschitz hinge, differentiable mesh-normal supervision, and an auxiliary camera predictor. For reconstruction, refine the camera with a silhouette and interleave visible-normal-guided SDF updates with sampling. A separate coarse generator still chooses the sparse surface support.
- **Why it matters:** Removing a learned compression stage makes geometric losses act directly on the generated state. The useful decomposition is global support, local SDF detail, and an explicit observation/camera interface—not a claim that representation engineering or multi-stage generation disappeared.
- **Evidence:** Public-data training uses 320K Objaverse-XL meshes; the separately marked larger variant adds about 200K private meshes. In default 256-cubed ablations, Normal-FID improves from 92.96 for patch-only to 75.80 with the voxel branch and 68.97 with all listed geometry losses. At 1024-cubed, the regularizer reduces average boundary-edge count from 25,353 to 898. On GSO reconstruction, CD is 0.0131 versus TRELLIS2's 0.0147, but volume IoU is worse, 0.3815 versus 0.3884.
- **Catch:** Latent-free describes the high-resolution stage; the default first stage is a VecSet generator. Missed support cannot simply be restored by optimizing SDF values on existing patches, and normal guidance can distort thin structures when support is wrong. The regularizer reduces holes, not a topology guarantee; residual holes still use post-processing. Image-to-shape embedding scores on 102 inputs are not geometric ground truth. Training uses 16 A800 GPUs, and higher resolution remains expensive. This is object geometry generation/reconstruction, not a dynamics world model.
- **Read scope:** Main representation, architecture, losses, reconstruction loop, data/evaluation tables, component and first-stage ablations, limitations, and selected computational-cost appendix. No code execution or full fairness audit. No detailed note today.

### 3. **Neural Conditional Gaussian Model: Efficient Data Assimilation with Sequential Uncertainty Quantification for Nonlinear Dynamics**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant scientific world models, explicit belief state, and uncertainty-aware inference. **Verdict:** Useful constrained-model design; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26463](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26463), Chuanqi Chen and Nan Chen; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26463), [announced code/data](https://github.com/Chuanqi-Chen/NeuralCGM).
- **Mechanism:** Encode hidden physical state into a latent state whose transition and observation equations are affine in that latent, with nonlinear coefficients depending on observed variables. This permits analytical Gaussian filtering. A separate distribution-aware decoder consumes both latent posterior mean and covariance, using Jacobian moment propagation plus learned mean/covariance corrections. Train reconstruction, forecasting, and Gaussian data-assimilation likelihood together; online inference needs only observed variables.
- **Why it matters:** Decoding a posterior mean is not decoding a posterior distribution. The interface makes sequential uncertainty operational rather than appending a residual-error regressor after prediction. Conditional linearity buys tractable inference without requiring physical-state dynamics themselves to be linear.
- **Evidence:** Lorenz-63, Lorenz-96, and Kuramoto-Sivashinsky simulations. On KS, DA RMSE is 0.3846 versus predecessor CGKN's 0.4222, and diagonal-Gaussian NLL improves from 166.11 to 54.67. The particle filter has slightly better NLL, 52.42, but takes 706.22 seconds versus 1.83 for Neural CGM on the reported M2 inference setup. FNO still forecasts better: RMSE 0.0448 versus 0.0554.
- **Catch:** Offline training requires paired full-state trajectories, including variables hidden online. Exact Gaussian filtering holds for the learned conditional model, not necessarily the real nonlinear system; the nonlinear physical pushforward is only approximated as Gaussian. Physical likelihood and process-noise estimates use diagonal covariance, so joint uncertainty is not validated. Long test trajectories are temporally correlated and do not measure training-seed variability. The speed comparison excludes offline learning and depends on classical-filter settings; likelihood gains are not a general calibration certificate.
- **Read scope:** Main latent dynamics/filter, posterior decoder, training objectives, benchmark table/protocols, selected KS analysis, and conclusions/limitations. No artifact execution or independent coverage analysis. No detailed note today.

### 4. **The Stability Gap in Continual Reinforcement Learning**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant non-agent RL, continual adaptation, and evaluation design. **Verdict:** Useful diagnostic, not a new optimizer; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26252), Sebastian Pusch, Matthia Sabatelli, and Gido M. van de Ven; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26252), [announced benchmark/code](https://github.com/sebapusch/stability-gap-drl).
- **Mechanism:** Give otherwise identical control tasks different orthogonal transforms of vector observations and append a task ID. Introduce tasks sequentially while continuing to train on every encountered task with balanced replay. Evaluate past-task return every 15 environment steps near transitions, and compare the actual optimization trajectory with parameter interpolation between successful checkpoints.
- **Why it matters:** Final retention can conceal dangerous temporary failures. Full access to old tasks does not guarantee stable old-task behavior during adaptation; replay availability and the optimization path are separate design obligations.
- **Evidence:** DQN and discrete SAC on CartPole, DDPG and SAC on InvertedPendulum; learning-rate tuning uses 10 seeds and final experiments use 50 new seeds with bootstrapped intervals. All four algorithms show a substantial transient drop after at least one transition. Controls retain replay/optimizer state, reset exploration, or use IQM aggregation without removing the pattern. SAC variants can have high-return linear paths between checkpoints despite poor intermediate training return.
- **Catch:** Only two simple environments, three representation-shift tasks each, and privileged task IDs. This is not evidence for arbitrary reward/dynamics changes or high-dimensional lifelong robotics. Parameter interpolation is an offline diagnostic, not an available safe online update rule. No proposed method closes the gap, and endpoint-tuned learning rates need not optimize transient safety.
- **Read scope:** Entire short main paper, benchmark construction, protocol, controls, interpolation experiment, and limitations. No code reproduction. No detailed note today.

### 5. **C-LiTE: A Lightweight Foundation Model for Cellular feature learning in Histopathology**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical representation learning; adjacent inspiration for scale-matched tokenization. **Verdict:** Useful efficiency/data contribution; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26316](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26316), Esha Sadia Nasir, Jiaqi Lv, Gozde N. Gunesli, and Shan E Ahmed Raza; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26316), [public code](https://github.com/eshasadia/C-LITE), [checkpoint repository](https://huggingface.co/eshasadia/C-LITE). GitHub reports a nonempty repository and Hugging Face lists three checkpoint files; neither was executed or downloaded for reproduction.
- **Mechanism:** A roughly 22.5M-parameter ViT-5-S processes native 64-by-64 cell crops using 4-by-4 patches, keeping a 16-by-16 spatial token grid. Pseudoinverse resizing initializes the smaller patch projection from pretrained weights. DINO/iBOT teacher-student learning uses two full-field cell views on 60M TCGA cell-centered crops; optional variants start from distilled pathology teachers. Small patches matter more than the initialization trick.
- **Why it matters:** Upsampling a tiny cell crop does not create morphology, but retaining a coarse patch size after shrinking the input destroys its token grid. Match token granularity to the physical unit being represented before assuming a much larger foundation model is needed.
- **Evidence:** Seven frozen-feature cell-classification benchmarks; mean macro-F1 is 0.670 versus CytoFormer's 0.682. At the largest fitting batches on one A100, reported throughput is 1,039 versus 49 cells/second. CoNIC's controlled patch-size comparison improves macro-F1 from 0.567 for Patch16/Crop64 to 0.701 for Patch4/Crop64, then 0.718 with pseudoinverse initialization. The latter is a useful smaller increment, not the main effect.
- **Catch:** Some TCGA-derived evaluation images may overlap pretraining slides; the paper separately includes four non-TCGA benchmarks. Linear probes on centroid-defined crops are not end-to-end clinical detection or segmentation. Throughput uses different maximum-fitting batch sizes, not matched single-cell latency. No data-corpus ablation isolates the 60M-crop contribution, and detection noise, other stains, molecular phenotype, and fine-tuning remain incompletely tested. The weight metadata declares a noncommercial license.
- **Read scope:** Data/crop construction, native tokenization and patch-transfer derivation, DINO/iBOT training, split/linear-probe protocol, aggregate/per-class results, efficiency, patch ablations, and stated limitations. Long tables and appendices selectively inspected, not every cell audited. No detailed note today.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Posterior Matching Distillation.** Use a family of tractable conditional probes when the desired object is a distribution, not a point estimate. Preserve the tilt/shift identity and the stochastic-architecture controls, while treating exact posterior recovery, useful diversity, and visual quality as separate claims. The study is most useful as a supervision recipe and a baseline design, not as a blanket best-distiller announcement.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Posterior supervision:** Compare zero-tilt mean matching, stochastic DMD, PMD-only, GAN-only, and full PMD with matched stochastic inputs and query/compute budgets. Distinguish guided sampling from an exact posterior-mean teacher. Measure posterior variation at a fixed noisy state as well as whole-generation diversity.
- **Native 3D generation:** Separate support-generation errors from fine-SDF errors. Match data budgets, rendering losses, and reconstruction-time optimization before crediting latent removal alone. Report raw boundary defects and post-processed mesh validity separately.
- **Scientific beliefs:** Compare point decoding, distribution decoding, and likelihood training separately. Charge offline full-state supervision to the method, report coverage under changed dynamics/observation patterns, and distinguish marginal uncertainty from joint covariance.
- **Continual RL:** Add minimum old-task return, recovery time, and accumulated performance deficit around task switches to final return/forgetting. Retain buffer/optimizer controls. A benign line between checkpoints does not certify the stochastic training trajectory.
- **Cell representations:** Match physical field of view and token scale, not just nominal pixel resolution or parameter count. Use non-overlapping slide/cohort tests, smaller-patch controls, and explicit segmentation/detection costs alongside encoder throughput.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Each paper catches information lost at an interface. A mean does not preserve a multimodal posterior; a latent decoder can erase geometry; a point-state decoder discards belief uncertainty; final return hides adaptation failures; and a coarse token grid can destroy cellular detail. The useful response is specific structure and a matched diagnostic—not indiscriminate model enlargement. Keep the corresponding boundaries equally explicit: finite posterior probes are not an exact-distribution certificate, sparse support is still a bottleneck, Gaussian beliefs are approximations, safe endpoints do not imply safe learning, and strong cell probes are not clinical validation.
