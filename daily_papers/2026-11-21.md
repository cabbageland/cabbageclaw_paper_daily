## Ranked papers

### 1. **RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow post-training, controllability, and mechanistic evaluation. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26627](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26627), Tuna Han Salih Meral and colleagues; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26627), [project](https://rl-is-steering.github.io/).
- **Mechanism:** Freeze the base generator and add token-shared vectors at transformer-block outputs, indexed only by block, stream, guidance pass, and two fixed timestep bins. Fit the vectors by squared-error matching to an RL-tuned checkpoint's velocity predictions on re-noised teacher samples. The objective uses no reward, although checkpoint selection uses validation reward. A separate experiment trains only these vectors with Flow-GRPO from zero. Constant interventions can produce prompt-dependent effects because the frozen nonlinear network still performs the computation.
- **Why it matters:** Before attributing a reward gain to new input-dependent transformations, test a much smaller intervention at an expressive internal interface. Distillation sufficiency, direct learnability, and causal routing are separate questions, and this paper tests all three rather than relying on activation visualization alone.
- **Evidence:** On SD3.5-M, 288,768 vector parameters versus an 18.8M-parameter RL LoRA recover 90.8% of PickScore, 94.4% of OCR, and 95.9% of GenEval reward gains. Across six image checkpoints, recovery is 90.0–96.3%; two selected positive-gain video checkpoints give 111.9% and 89.8%. Direct vector-only RL reaches 103.6% and 104.6% of the reference PickScore/OCR gains. Replacing image-to-text attention reads with the unsteered base's text keys/values leaves only 10.5% of the fitted PickScore gain and 20.5% of its OCR gain.
- **Catch:** Sufficiency is not proof that RL actually learned only constants or never adds capabilities. All eight headline checkpoints use policy-gradient RL; only two of six screened video models had clearly positive evaluated gains and entered the main comparison. Matching a reward does not match an output distribution: the vectors also reproduce reduced diversity and color variation. The 25-rater/50-prompt human comparison finds no significant difference from RL-tuned images, not statistical equivalence. Small parameter count is not negligible training compute: direct vector RL uses 123/183 B200 GPU-hours. Supervised identity learning is a useful counterexample, where same-size LoRA preserves details better.
- **Read scope:** Full main paper; selected appendix fitting/initialization, compute/layout, attention identity, supervised counterexample, checkpoint inventory, and video-selection/prompt-pair controls. Long qualitative galleries and all appendix derivations were not exhaustively audited. PDF-based note; no code reproduction. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/rl_posttraining_diffusion_activation_steering.md).

### 2. **Active Feature Acquisition by Distilling an Oracle Teacher**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant explicit supervision and decision-making under partial observation; adjacent inspiration for adaptive sensing. **Verdict:** Useful mechanism; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26598), Yang Meng, Zixin Ding, Robert L. Grossman, and Yuxin Chen; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26598).
- **Mechanism:** Hold a mask-aware downstream predictor fixed. During training, reveal each candidate feature and the true label to compute its post-acquisition Brier score; softmax the negative scores into a teacher distribution. Train a small masked-input MLP with soft-target cross-entropy plus an auxiliary classification loss. At deployment, the student sees only acquired values and their mask, selects the highest-ranked affordable feature, and repeats. Random-mask training covers different subset sizes.
- **Why it matters:** An explicit privileged teacher makes acquisition decisions inspectable, and exposes an information gap that a larger student cannot necessarily close. The useful object is a teacher ranking and its deployment-visible recoverability, not another opaque utility network.
- **Evidence:** Six datasets spanning TCGA-Glioma, clinical/tabular benchmarks, synthetic data, and ICU measurements; five seeds, with additional five-fold CV on medical datasets. The reported Glioma curve recovers 90% of the full-acquisition accuracy improvement for under one quarter of total acquisition cost. In the teacher-quality ablation, mean-across-budget Glioma accuracy is 0.810 with Brier supervision, 0.800 with label-blind entropy supervision, and 0.767 with a random teacher.
- **Catch:** The oracle is myopic and conditional on the chosen predictor, not an optimal long-horizon acquisition oracle. Costs constrain affordability but do not enter its Brier ranking as value per dollar. Training assumes full candidate values and labels are available; feature-level benchmark actions need not correspond to orderable clinical tests. The kNN recoverability estimate of 0.30–0.53 is an empirical local-continuity diagnostic, not a proven universal ceiling. No STOP action or pooled patient budget is modeled.
- **Read scope:** Problem setup, teacher/student objectives, training/deployment algorithms, datasets/protocols, main results, teacher-quality ablation, recoverability analysis, and discussion. Detailed runtime and all appendix split/architecture tables were not audited. No detailed note today.

### 3. **One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant video generative models, bottlenecks, and action-conditioned prediction; the digest's single robotics-adjacent recommendation. **Verdict:** Useful information-access design; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26617](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617), Yuxin Jiang and colleagues; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26617), [project](https://showlab.github.io/o2laf).
- **Mechanism:** Initialize separate inverse and forward dynamics from the same Wan2.2 video prior. Read-only transition queries infer a 32-D Gaussian action bottleneck. A frame-causal forward model predicts jointly noised future frames using the initial frame and inferred actions. Emphasize high target noise to reduce the shortcut of copying visible target/history information; optimize flow matching plus KL regularization. For higher-quality generation, freeze the action encoder and train a separate bidirectional forward model.
- **Why it matters:** A nominal bottleneck does little if the predictor can bypass it. Attention masks, clean-history access, and target-noise schedules decide whether the action channel carries useful information. Strong action probing alone also fails to detect appearance leakage.
- **Evidence:** MiraData-to-MIND navigation and EgoDex-human-to-GR-1 probing. Reported GR-1 end-effector action R-squared is 0.277 versus DreamDojo's 0.149. On 840 camera-transfer generations, first-person translation NRMSE is 1.289 versus Olaf-World's 1.627, but temporal consistency is slightly worse, 0.900 versus 0.910. A V-JEPA2 variant probes strongly yet leaks source appearance. Training configurations use 16 GPUs for each stage.
- **Catch:** A latent inferred from observed transitions is not an executable motor command or an identified causal action. Human/robot transfer uses generated videos, probes, and cross-cycle reconstruction; these do not establish physical execution success. High-quality transfer uses additional second-stage training, not just the joint causal model. Learned coordinates remain hard to interpret, and the appendix explicitly distinguishes visual prediction from efficient decision-making dynamics. A qualitative simulator/video policy-verification application is not validated real2sim2real transfer.
- **Read scope:** Main architecture/objective, noise/access rationale, probing and transfer protocols/results, prior/noise and two-stage analyses; appendix training configuration and stated limitations. Appendix application figures were selectively inspected, not benchmark-reproduced. No detailed note today.

### 4. **When Foundation Models Disagree: Representational Heterogeneity for Calibrated Uncertainty in Whole-Slide Image Classification**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical/neuro AI, selective prediction, and uncertainty evaluation. **Verdict:** Useful confidence signal with heuristic boundaries; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26159](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26159), the CaliFuse framework; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26159).
- **Mechanism:** Train a separate nnMIL classifier on each frozen pathology encoder. Combine an entropy-based confidence from consensus-conditioned slide prediction pooling with one minus normalized generalized Jensen–Shannon divergence over all model/chunk predictions. Use equal weights for the two confidence branches. The fusion stage is training-free; the per-encoder classifiers and feature extraction are not free.
- **Why it matters:** Averaging predictions can conceal uncertainty induced by choosing a representation. Keep evidence inconsistency as an explicit signal instead of assuming one encoder's internally stable confidence reflects all plausible views.
- **Evidence:** Seven WSI tasks, including glioma subtyping and two label granularities of EBRAINS. With five encoders, EBRAINS-30 AURC is 0.110 versus naive averaging's 0.174, while balanced accuracy is 0.753 versus 0.752. The paper reports best error-ranking AUROC/AUPR/AURC on all seven tasks in this setting, but naive averaging has better Kidney ECE. Five TCGA tasks use patient-separated folds; the two EBRAINS tasks share one cohort and official split.
- **Catch:** The JS score mixes within-slide tissue heterogeneity with cross-encoder disagreement; it is not a pure epistemic measurement. Scalar confidence is not automatically a calibrated class probability. Consensus pooling is heuristic, including choosing the most confident model under unanimity. Main runs use one training seed, each encoder count tests only one subset, and EBRAINS results lack fold variance. Five full encoder/classifier branches cost substantially more than one; prospective clinical utility and shift robustness are not established.
- **Read scope:** Main motivation, complete confidence/pooling definitions, dataset and patient-split protocol, metric definitions, selected main/combination tables, lambda analysis, and limitations. Not every table cell or preprocessing artifact audited. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Grid Cells as Spectral Successor Features: A Reusable Basis for Continual Reinforcement Learning**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant non-agent continual RL; adjacent inspiration for operator-aligned representations. **Verdict:** Useful derivation, limited evidence; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26504](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26504), Elodie Cote-Gauthier, Doina Precup, and Isabeau Premont-Schwarz; NeurReps/WiML workshops at NeurIPS 2026 according to the manuscript; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26504).
- **Mechanism:** Choose state-action features spanning an invariant subspace of a fixed reference-policy transition operator. For an eigenfeature with eigenvalue lambda, its successor feature is simply the same feature divided by 1 minus gamma times lambda. Learn only linear reward weights and act greedily on reference-policy values. In practice, low-frequency eigenvectors of a symmetrized transition graph supply the basis.
- **Why it matters:** Pick a representation in which a needed long-horizon operation becomes algebraic, then isolate what adaptation really requires. State-action structure matters: invariance under an action-averaged state operator does not imply invariance under each action operator.
- **Evidence:** One 12-by-12 deterministic gridworld with 84 free cells and 336 state-action nodes, two recurring goals, 250K steps per task, and five seeds. Both spectral variants show near-maximal return with brief switch dips, while pixel DQN and SimpleSF drop substantially. These are informative curves, not broad continual-RL superiority.
- **Catch:** The offline spectral model has full graph coverage plus supervised encoder pretraining; the online version gets cell IDs instead of baseline pixels. The paper acknowledges unequal information. Symmetrization makes the closed form exact for a surrogate walk, not necessarily the original dynamics. One greedy improvement over a fixed reference policy is not generally optimal; negative-reward lava was removed, and the paper gives a corridor counterexample where the greedy policy avoids the goal. Grid-cell naming does not add neuroscientific validation.
- **Read scope:** Full main paper, invariant-subspace/eigenfeature derivation, state-action argument, graph approximation, experimental setup, curves, and limitations. Detailed appendix hyperparameters and proofs not exhaustively audited. No detailed note today.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**RL Post-Training in Diffusion Transformers Is Activation Steering.** Keep the restricted-interface baseline, not the universal-sounding title. A tiny trainable intervention can exploit substantial existing computation; test sufficiency, direct optimization, routing, and counterexamples before deciding what a weight update learned.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Post-training mechanisms:** Compare mean activation shifts, velocity-fitted shifts, same-size input-dependent adapters, and direct vector RL. Separate parameter count, teacher-cache/fitting cost, rollout cost, reward gain, diversity, and human preference. A successful compressed surrogate does not identify the unique internal algorithm.
- **Acquisition:** Hold the downstream predictor fixed. Compare label-blind and privileged teachers; test cost-sensitive and multi-step teachers on the same visible states. Distinguish a noisy kNN ambiguity estimate from an information-theoretic impossibility result.
- **Latent actions:** Cross action probing with appearance probing, target-context preservation, and executed control. Charge two-stage training explicitly. Change masks and noise while matching data/prior capacity; otherwise the bottleneck claim is underdetermined.
- **Clinical confidence:** Separate classification, error ranking, absolute calibration, and net clinical benefit. Compare heterogeneous-view signals with calibrated ensemble baselines, matched compute, and external shifted cohorts. Do not count EBRAINS label granularities as independent clinical populations.
- **Continual RL:** Match graph access, observation modality, representation pretraining, and exploration before crediting spectral structure. Include signed rewards, directed/stochastic dynamics, and changing geometry. Report the residual from symmetrization and basis truncation.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The common lesson is to constrain an interface before enlarging the learner. Fixed internal shifts test how much computation is already available; explicit acquisition teachers expose missing information; causal/noise design makes an action bottleneck earn its role; heterogeneous pathology representations reveal disagreements hidden by averaging; spectral features turn some long-horizon prediction into rescaling. The boundaries are the interesting part: reward recovery is not mechanism identity, privileged rankings may be unrecoverable, video transfer is not motor control, confidence ranking is not clinical calibration, and a closed-form surrogate is not an optimal policy.
