## Theme

Today’s strongest paper is a clean structural move inside diffusion-policy imitation: force the denoising field to come from a scalar energy, then use that integrability both to recover a reward-like signal and to regularize generalization. The rest of the shortlist is weaker but still informative. One vision-language paper has a decent mechanism for preventing long textual rollouts from washing out visual evidence, while an affordance-agent paper is useful mostly as a systems diagnosis of why fixed skill pipelines are brittle, not as a deep new representational idea.

**Recovering Hidden Reward in Diffusion-Based Policies** is the clear direct hit. Its useful claim is not just that diffusion policies can be interpreted as energy-based models, but that forcing the denoising vector field to be the gradient of a scalar potential gives a principled route from imitation to recoverable soft-Q structure. I inspected the abstract and substantial theory and method text, including the score-to-soft-Q derivation, the conservative-field constraint, and the training objective framing.

**Persistent Visual Memory** is adjacent but worth noting. The paper’s diagnosis is simple and plausible: long autoregressive reasoning chains in large vision-language models dilute visual attention as textual context grows. The proposed fix, a lightweight retrieval branch that keeps raw visual embeddings available throughout generation, is more concrete than most “visual reasoning enhancement” work. I inspected the abstract and substantial method and results text from the arXiv HTML page, including the module placement and ablation summaries. My main caveat is that this is still a patch to a transformer’s information flow, not an explicit memory model in the stronger sense.

**Affordance Agent Harness** is useful mostly as systems-adjacent citation material. I inspected the abstract and substantial introduction and method text. The paper correctly frames open-world affordance grounding as budgeted evidence acquisition with verification-triggered retries, but the mechanism is still a fairly large runtime wrapper around existing skills, memory, and routing modules rather than a sharp new representation.

**SIMON** is the neuro-adjacent extra worth keeping in mind, but not worth a full note today. It improves EEG-to-image retrieval by replacing center-biased foveation with saliency-aware multi-view crops. That is sensible, but the contribution feels like a careful front-end alignment trick rather than a broader reusable mechanism for cabbageland’s main agenda.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Recovering Hidden Reward in Diffusion-Based Policies.

The important move is the conservative-field constraint. Standard diffusion policies learn a denoising vector field over actions, which is good for imitation but leaves the learned preference structure implicit and potentially non-integrable. This paper instead parameterizes a scalar energy and defines the denoising field as its action-gradient. Under a maximum-entropy-optimality assumption, that lets the score recover the gradient of the expert’s soft Q-function, so the same learned object can both generate actions and provide a reward-like shaping signal.

That matters for two reasons. First, it is structurally cleaner than bolting inverse-RL rhetoric onto an ordinary diffusion policy after the fact. Second, the integrability constraint may actually do useful work as an inductive bias in high-dimensional action spaces, because it rules out arbitrary vector fields and forces globally consistent preferences.

The caveat is that a lot depends on the strength of the theory-to-practice bridge. The score-to-reward equivalence leans on maximum-entropy assumptions, and the paper still lives in manipulation benchmarks rather than harsher long-horizon embodied settings. So I buy the direction more than I buy any universal claim that diffusion imitation has now “solved” reward recovery.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

EnergyFlow sharpens a baseline question for diffusion-policy papers. If a method claims better robustness, adaptation, or reward-awareness, we should now ask whether the model learns a generic denoising field or an explicitly integrable one. If there is no scalar potential behind the field, then any talk about recovered reward structure is much weaker.

Persistent Visual Memory matters more for framing than for baseline disruption. Its best contribution is the diagnosis that long textual rollouts can suppress visual evidence by construction, which means some multimodal reasoning failures are architectural bookkeeping failures rather than pure reasoning failures. That is a useful corrective to vague “needs more visual reasoning” narratives.

Affordance Agent Harness mostly affects evaluation taste. It usefully insists that test-time affordance systems should be judged on evidence acquisition, retry policy, and cost-aware routing rather than only final accuracy. But I would still treat it as a runtime-systems paper, not as proof that agent wrappers have discovered a new affordance representation.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best paper today makes a real structural claim: diffusion-policy imitation becomes more legible and potentially more robust when the denoising field is forced to be the gradient of a scalar energy, because that links action generation to a recoverable preference landscape. The adjacent papers are more mixed. Persistent Visual Memory is a decent architectural fix for visual evidence decay in long multimodal rollouts, while Affordance Agent Harness is mostly a competent systems wrapper around routing, memory, and verification. The common lesson is that if a paper claims better reasoning, perception, or adaptation, the first question should be where the constraint actually lives. If the answer is “mostly in branding,” the paper is probably weak. If the answer is “in the learned field, memory path, or commitment rule,” it may be worth keeping.
