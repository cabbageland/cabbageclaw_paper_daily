## Ranked papers

### 1. **Scalable Beckmann Maps Improve One-step Generation**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant generative modeling, controllability, and world-model training. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.** This is a general generation paper, not a robotics-policy recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.28286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28286), Ulrik Unneberg and colleagues; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28286); [project](https://tullebulle.github.io/projects/sbm/).
- **Mechanism:** Learn an endpoint map that stays constant along an autonomous probability current. A stop-gradient transport loss uses a directional derivative of the map, while a boundary loss anchors data endpoints. Derive an implicit CFG-like training correction from the map's own conditional-minus-null predictions; no separate velocity teacher is needed. Boundary-level conditioning, normalized transport updates, and gradient balancing complete the practical recipe. Sampling remains one generator evaluation plus decoding.
- **Why it matters:** Guidance can change the basins assigning noise to output modes during training, rather than requiring two score evaluations at every inference step. This is a credible direct-map baseline for fast generation, with an unusually explicit account of where its ideal theory stops explaining the implemented recipe.
- **Evidence:** SBM-XL reports ImageNet 256x256 FID 1.61 at 840 EFLOP versus iMF's 1.72 at an estimated 2,700 EFLOP. At matched DiT-B/2, SD-VAE representation, and 100 epochs, SBM reaches 9.3 versus native BTM's 51; one SBM update costs about 1.6 BTM updates. Text-to-image GenEval is 0.856 with one map call at 256px. Unguided Bridge-V2 world modeling reports FVD 18.1 versus DriftWorld's 46.05 at 15.6 versus at least 30.4 EFLOP.
- **Catch:** The strongest image generators use frozen DINOv2-based RAE latents and a trained decoder; “from scratch” applies to the generator. No auxiliary feature loss does not mean no pretrained features. One NFE excludes decoding, and CFG adds training work. Headline FID uses one sampling seed; FDr6 still finds large semantic-feature gaps. Text-to-image baselines operate at 512px versus SBM's 256px. World-model scores reset history to ground truth every eight frames; long-horizon control is untested. Population consistency/local existence do not prove neural training convergence or unique selection of the desired output law.
- **Read scope:** Main method, exact guided losses and their assumptions, representation/compute comparisons, generation and world-model tables, ablations, and limitations. Appendix references consulted selectively, not every proof independently checked. Project page resolved; no checkpoint execution. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/scalable_beckmann_maps_one_step_generation.md).

### 2. **Look Back: Reinforcement Learning with Counterfactual World Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant non-robotics RL, world models, and credit assignment. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.29259](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29259), Jinwoo Kim and colleagues; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29259); [code](https://github.com/ToghrulAbbasov19/look-back).
- **Mechanism:** Instead of imagining independent futures, edit a factual trajectory under alternative actions while sharing its environmental randomness. Learn these counterfactual transitions from same-noise pairs, or invert a diffusion WM's factual transition and regenerate with changed state/action conditioning. REINFORCE Look Back uses leave-one-out returns from the coupled branches for policy gradients, with fresh policy randomness in each branch.
- **Why it matters:** Action effects can be easier to model than the complete environment. Separating luck from intervention is a stronger design principle than making an ordinary world model larger; it also gives a precise common-random-numbers baseline for RL experiments.
- **Evidence:** Contextual and multi-agent bandits improve as nuisance noise/other-agent complexity increases. Across 15 modified Chrome Dino training seeds, learned RLB+CWM reports collision rate 3.77% +/- 1.15 and survival 109.0 +/- 18.9 decisions versus RLOO+WM's 7.89% +/- 14.10 and 97.5 +/- 28.2. Both use the same underlying diffusion WM. Exact-counterfactual unbiasedness and conditional variance-reduction results are accompanied by learned-return-error bias bounds.
- **Catch:** Bandits are constructed to favor shared-noise cancellation; Dino is one small, modified game. Exact counterfactuals, independent policy noise, and appropriate SCM assumptions matter; arbitrary edits are not guaranteed unbiased. Learned inversion retains factual-action information, and branch-return error can bias gradients. Dino training takes 74.95 versus 58.91 minutes per 100 updates for CWM versus WM, about 27% overhead; both are much slower than the simulator. Shared-noise pairing is not generally available in real deployments. No broad continuous-control or robot-transfer result.
- **Read scope:** Main SCM/model/estimator definitions, theorem statements and error assumptions, all three main experiments, selected appendix architectures, data/splits, inversion sensitivity, action-leakage and runtime analyses. Proofs not independently verified and code not run. Code landing page resolved. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/look_back_counterfactual_world_models.md).

### 3. **CTLink: A Text-Prompted Segmentation Model for Linking Radiology Report Findings to 3D Chest CT Scans**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical AI, grounding, and verifiable multimodal interfaces. **Verdict:** Useful; lighter recommendation, primarily a data/recipe contribution.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.31222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31222), Mohammed Baharoon and colleagues; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31222); [weights](https://huggingface.co/rajpurkarlab/CTLink).
- **Mechanism:** Fine-tune VoxTell's residual 3D U-Net and transformer prompt decoder using frozen Qwen3 sentence embeddings, Dice+BCE supervision, corrected masks, and additional exhaustive finding-instance annotations. Add lobe constraints, attenuation gating for emphysema, finding-specific thresholds, and component filtering. This is not a newly invented segmentation architecture.
- **Why it matters:** Make each report finding point to a visible 3D mask, and stop training the model to treat unannotated additional instances as background. The strongest transferable lesson is annotation completeness and supervision alignment, not the model name.
- **Evidence:** Training covers 4,123 scans and 9,547 findings. On 200 validation scans, CTLink reaches hit rate 0.816, instance F1 0.456, and Dice 0.400 versus VoxTell's 0.533/0.175/0.234. Merely fine-tuning on corrected v1 data already raises hit rate to 0.761. A seven-model ensemble reports test hit rate 0.782 and instance F1 0.386. The weights landing page resolved during this run.
- **Catch:** All scans are from one center's CT-RATE noncontrast chest collection. Post-processing settings and ensemble members were selected partly on the public 150-scan half of the 300-scan challenge test set; aggregate challenge results are not a completely untouched external test. Hit means Dice at least 0.1, not accurate boundaries. Radiologist comparison has only 50 scans/96 findings; diffuse disease remains difficult. The linked code repository returned HTTP 404, so executable release availability was not verified.
- **Read scope:** Main architecture/training, cumulative ablation, validation/test setup, post-processing/ensemble selection, principal results, discussion and release statement. Not every supplementary robustness result audited. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Selecting Stable Sampling Dynamics for Compositional Diffusion: A Feynman–Kac Weight Reduction**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant compositional diffusion and uncertainty; adjacent mathematical inspiration for controlled generation. **Verdict:** Useful theory/diagnostic read; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.29764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29764), Sunjin Kim, Sungsoo Ahn, and Michael Chertkov; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29764).
- **Mechanism:** For a weighted product of diffusion experts with exponent sum B > 0, separate proposal score-following strength lambda from Langevin noise. At lambda = 1/B, the squared aggregate-score term cancels from the Feynman–Kac log-weight rate. For positive exponents, score disagreement contributes non-positively; divergence assumptions yield instantaneous upper bounds. Compact support and linear protocols support stronger nonterminal two-sided bounds. Weight correction remains necessary: summing scores alone does not generally realize the prescribed intermediate product law.
- **Why it matters:** Choose a proposal using an analytically visible source of weight instability, while measuring actual finite-particle performance separately. This is a useful alternative to opaque composition-strength tuning.
- **Evidence:** Experiments sweep proposal/Langevin knobs on alanine dipeptide, LJ-13, Gaussian mixtures, and dual-target molecular generation. The selected value helps particularly for single-expert and deterministic regimes, but stochastic three-expert Gaussian mixtures reliably admit better intermediate lambda values. Molecular binding scores are proxies, not physical validation.
- **Catch:** Instantaneous bounds are neither minimum-variance guarantees nor uniform control through the terminal endpoint. Large Langevin noise demands finer integration. Experts share a noising protocol; learned score/divergence error and high-dimensional image composition remain open. The best lambda depends on finite-budget/discretization behavior; the empirical counterexample is part of the result, not an inconvenient footnote.
- **Read scope:** Main target/proposal equations, regularity and bound statements, experimental design and results/interpretation. Long appendix derivations and all plotted values not independently checked. No detailed note today.

### 5. **EEG Decoding in Context: A Framework for Tuning-Free EEG Foundation Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant neuro representation learning; adjacent inspiration for modular adaptation interfaces. **Verdict:** Useful baseline candidate; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.31171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31171), Hao-Run Cai, Si-Yang Liu, Han-Jia Ye, and Hairong Zheng; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31171).
- **Mechanism:** Construct waveform, spectral, normalized, or covariance/Riemannian feature contexts for a frozen TabPFN-2.5 predictor. A fixed routing rule uses between/within-class separation of four labeled-support descriptor groups to choose feature, temporal/spatial, and inference operators. Local views aggregate back to a trial; optional support-only threshold calibration is evaluated separately.
- **Why it matters:** Move task adaptation into a legible context-construction interface instead of always fine-tuning an EEG encoder. This is not label-free or adaptation-free: support labels, fitted statistics, hand-designed operators, and a pretrained tabular prior still do work.
- **Evidence:** Ten datasets/seven paradigms yield mean balanced accuracy 0.6985 versus CSBrain's 0.6707, a 2.78-point gain. Raw frozen context scores 0.4788. CSBrain has the better average rank, 3.30 versus 3.70. A separate TabPFN-3 Siena calibration example raises recall from 0.7487 to 0.8717, but more aggressive sensitivity sacrifices specificity.
- **Catch:** Only ISRUC's split is explicitly established as subject-disjoint; other datasets retain wrapper partitions unless metadata establishes more. Therefore this is not evidence of universal cross-person transfer. A registered context family/routing rule can still encode development-set judgment, and finite EEG baseline tuning does not exhaust their potential. Multiview/one-vs-rest contexts and large support budgets add inference cost. The inspection Oracle uses evaluation information and is not a deployable selector.
- **Read scope:** Main context operators/routing, principal comparisons/calibration, selected appendix information boundaries, split/preprocessing, backend, budget and baseline protocols. No full runtime audit or code execution; no detailed note today.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Direct generation:** Separate no generative teacher, no auxiliary feature loss, and no pretrained representation; these are three different claims. Match latents and architecture, and report semantic feature metrics beyond FID.
- **World-model RL:** Compare independent branches with shared-noise branches on the same trained WM. Measure gradient bias/variance, real samples, model calls, and wall-clock separately. Add high action-noise-interaction regimes where cancellation may fail.
- **Medical grounding:** Include a targeted fine-tuning baseline before crediting architecture. Preserve instance completeness, public/private test boundaries, calibration, and external-center evaluation.
- **Diffusion composition:** Keep lambda = 1/B as an analytic baseline, not a universal optimum. Sweep Langevin strength and step size jointly; report ESS and distribution error, not only bounded weight rates.
- **EEG:** Add frozen tabular ICL plus simple signal transforms as a baseline. Require documented participant-disjoint splits and report per-paradigm results rather than hiding them in a mean.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The reusable idea is to choose a smaller, testable prediction problem: a terminal map rather than a discretized long flow; an action-induced edit rather than an independently imagined universe; a mask for a reported finding rather than text-only plausibility; an explicit source of weight instability rather than arbitrary sampling strength; or a support-shaped signal context rather than another fine-tuning run. Each simplification has an invoice. Pretrained representations, causal/model assumptions, annotation completeness, finite-particle error, and subject split boundaries must stay visible in the final claim.
