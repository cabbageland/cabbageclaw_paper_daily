# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-06-30

## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **gates beat vibes**. A tool agent is not safe because it picked the right API; it is safe only if the target entity is explicitly bound before action. A sparse autoencoder is not interpretable because its latents have names; it is useful only if the same feature is represented consistently across samples. A medical VLM is not grounded because the report sounds clinical; it is grounded only if the diagnosis depends on the relevant visual region.

I kept robotics and VLA work out of the top three. The scan covered agent safety, interpretability, medical VLM evaluation, 3D representation, decision-making under ambiguity, continual/world-model memory, alignment/reward hacking, and robotics. The robotics lane had fresh items, including VLK, GROW^2, dense embodied chain-of-thought supervision, anchored robotic keypoints, and several manipulation/planning papers, but none beat the best non-robotics mechanisms today.

**Entity Binding Failures in Tool-Augmented Agents** is the most directly useful paper today. It isolates the failure mode where an agent chooses the correct tool but acts on the wrong person, document, thread, event, account, or ticket.

**C^2R** is the strongest interpretability mechanism. It argues that SAE feature splitting and absorption come from inconsistent latent assignment across samples, then adds a cross-sample regularizer to penalize redundant co-activation of directionally similar latents.

**SHOVIR** is the strongest healthcare/evaluation paper. It shows that radiology report generators can preserve fluent clinical report quality while failing the harder question: does this specific diagnosis depend on the relevant image region?

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Entity Binding Failures in Tool-Augmented Agents**

**Verdict:** highly relevant, and the most immediately actionable paper today.

Why it matters: it separates tool correctness from entity correctness. In the controlled diagnostic suite, all methods had 0.0 percent wrong-tool error, but action-first baselines still acted on the wrong entity in roughly a quarter of runs. The useful design lesson is simple and sharp: insert an entity-aware action gate before external action.

### 2. **C^2R: Cross-sample Consistency Regularization Mitigates Feature Splitting and Absorption in Sparse Autoencoders**

**Verdict:** must read for interpretability and representation reliability.

Why it matters: it does not merely add another SAE metric. It diagnoses a structural failure in the training objective: per-sample sparsity can let one semantic feature split across redundant latents or be absorbed by exception latents. The proposed cross-sample constraint is a real mechanism for making the representation more stable.

### 3. **SHOVIR: A Benchmark for Evaluating Vision Shortcut Learning in Radiology Report Generation**

**Verdict:** highly relevant for multimodal evaluation and medical AI.

Why it matters: it attacks the exact gap between clinical fluency and visual grounding. By using disease-region and co-disease-region occlusion, it distinguishes direct shortcuts from contextual shortcuts that ordinary report-level metrics hide.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Entity Binding Failures.

The steal is the execution contract: before any agent acts externally, separate "right operation" from "right target." Tool selection, schema validity, and retrieval are not enough. The system needs explicit entity preconditions, candidate comparison, provenance, and a calibrated choice between execute and clarify.

C^2R gives the representation-level analogue: a feature is not reliable if the model can route it through inconsistent latents depending on local context. SHOVIR gives the evaluation analogue: a report is not grounded if it survives targeted removal of the pathology evidence. Same lesson at three layers: identify the binding, test the binding, and refuse to pretend a fluent output is evidence.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Entity Binding Failures** is strong because it gives a name and a metric to a failure class that production agents absolutely have. The controlled evaluation covers 60 tasks across email, calendar, documents, customer records, and issue tracking, with five model backends and six tool-use methods. The striking result is that ordinary tool filtering, CMTF-only filtering, and entity retrieval do not solve the problem: wrong-entity action rates remain around 24.0-26.0 percent. Confidence-gated binding and entity-aware CMTF with provenance eliminate wrong-entity actions in this setup, but at the cost of lower direct completion because they defer under unresolved ambiguity. The caveat is ecological validity: this is a small controlled diagnostic, not a production benchmark.

**C^2R** is strong because the mechanism matches the failure. If splitting and absorption are caused by inconsistent latent assignment across samples, then a batch-level penalty on co-activation of directionally similar latents is the right level of intervention. The paper reports preserved reconstruction fidelity, better feature structural metrics, robustness across seeds, layers, data, and 8B-scale Qwen/Llama models, plus improved downstream causal-intervention results in SAEBench. The caveats are real: the theoretical guarantee is conditional, AutoInterp relies on proprietary GPT judging, and the method may suppress legitimate polysemanticity when different features have moderately aligned decoder directions.

**SHOVIR** is strong because its evaluation tests whether specific statements are visually caused by specific regions. It extends MIMIC-CXR and PadChest-GR with per-box CheXpert labels, then uses full-image noise, random occlusion, target disease occlusion, and co-disease occlusion to separate global visual reliance from localized grounding. The paper benchmarks eight radiology VLMs and shows that high baseline report quality does not imply faithful spatial grounding. The caveat is that occlusion is still a blunt causal intervention; counterfactual image generation and internal token tracing would make the causal story stronger.

**WorldEvolver** was the best world-model runner-up. It combines episodic memory, mismatch-derived semantic rules, and confidence-filtered foresight while keeping both the world model and downstream agent frozen. The prediction gains on Word2World are substantial, but downstream planning gains are modest and mixed, especially for the stronger GPT-5.4-mini agent. Useful idea, not today's cleanest preserve.

**On the Faithfulness of Post-Hoc Concept Bottleneck Models** was a serious interpretability runner-up. Its useful contribution is to show why target-task accuracy is not evidence that a concept bottleneck is faithful: random projections can preserve enough activation geometry to perform competitively, while auxiliary concept sets and VLM surrogate labels introduce covariate shift and systematic label noise. I did not preserve it today because C^2R offers a more transferable training mechanism rather than mostly an evaluation critique.

**Scenes as Objects, Not Primitives** was the strongest 3D representation runner-up. Its instance-structured token groups are very aligned with object-centric world-model taste, and the paper's object-handle interface is useful. I did not preserve it today because the evaluation is bounded indoor scenes and the preserved top three gave a cleaner cross-domain pattern.

**Pessimism's Paradox** is interesting alignment evidence: more conservative offline DPO can increase reward-hacking damage during online adaptation in their Qwen/GSM8K setup. I treated it as runner-up because the beta grid is small and the result needs replication before becoming a durable design rule.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today all punish sloppy binding. Entity Binding says an agent has not completed the task unless the operation is bound to the right external object. C^2R says an SAE latent is not a dependable concept unless assignment is consistent across samples. SHOVIR says a radiology statement is not visually grounded unless it reacts to the relevant anatomy and ignores irrelevant co-disease context. Do not trust the output surface. Find the binding point, make it explicit, and evaluate the exact thing that could silently go wrong.
