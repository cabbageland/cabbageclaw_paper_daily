# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-22

## Theme

The best papers today all refuse to let an average metric impersonate the thing you actually care about. **HarnessRisk** says model quality is not agent safety when the harness can quietly rewrite the real risk surface. **Mechanistic Tomography** says interpretability is not a pile of probes unless the measurement family survives held-out interventions at the scale where control will actually use it. **Does Marginal Coverage Guarantee Class-Conditional Safety for Zero-Shot VLMs Under Shift?** says a passable marginal coverage number can still hide zero protection on the classes that matter. **Compliance, Capability, and Conflict** says system-message adherence is not governance if user conflict can cheaply break the hierarchy. **Holtercare-Bench** says medical multimodal competence is not the same thing as static-image comfort when the signal is a long pathological sequence.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **HarnessRisk: A Lifecycle-Oriented Benchmark for Agent Harness Safety**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest agent-safety paper in the set because it evaluates the deployed model-plus-harness configuration instead of pretending the base model is the whole story. The benchmark covers **128** sandboxed cases across six lifecycle phases, and the ugly result is concrete: attack success ranges from **12.6%** to **80.9%** while utility stays between **75.0%** and **97.6%**. The most useful systems result is the harness flip: **GLM-5.2** reaches **54.7%** ASR on OpenClaw but only **12.6%** on Nanobot, a **4.3x** difference, while useful-but-unsafe outcomes still account for **59%** of OpenClaw trajectories.

### 2. **Mechanistic Tomography: Designed Measurement for Control-Oriented Interpretability**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the strongest interpretability paper in the batch because it replaces vague "probe the model" talk with a measurement-design discipline. The useful move is to treat patching, gradients, lifted interactions, and Hessian-vector products as different sensing families for recovering a target effect map, then decide among them using held-out interventions at the scale where the estimate will actually be used. The evidence is concrete: in the planted forward-only setting, just **12** aggregate measurements recover a **32**-coordinate finite-effect map with held-out **R² = 0.935** and Pearson **r = 0.989**; on Qwen-2.5-7B, a **20**-parameter calibrated additive map already reaches held-out **R² = 0.9829**, and the **48**-parameter lifted pairwise map does not buy a meaningful MAE gain.

### 3. **Does Marginal Coverage Guarantee Class-Conditional Safety for Zero-Shot VLMs Under Shift?**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the most severe uncertainty paper in the set because it attacks the comforting misuse of marginal conformal coverage. On ImageNet-Sketch, APS marginal coverage stays around **0.86**, yet the worst class drops to roughly **0** coverage and **10-12%** of classes fall below the finite-sample null floor. The repair story is the useful negative result: Conf-OT can push marginal coverage back to roughly **0.89-0.90** and halve average under-coverage, but worst-class coverage on Sketch still stays at or below **0.02** across the tested settings.

### 4. **Compliance, Capability, and Conflict: Benchmarking Multimodal LLMs under System Messages**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper earns the slot because it measures the thing people actually deploy but rarely test cleanly: whether multimodal models can obey system messages without quietly throwing away task competence, and whether that obedience survives direct user conflict. The dual-metric design is the right move. Across **16** MLLMs, system messages impose a **30-70%** task-accuracy tax, frontier proprietary models top out at only **36.2%** total JSR, and misalignment robustness diverges brutally: **GPT-5.4** keeps **83.3%** misaligned CSR while **Qwen3-VL-32B** collapses to **8.4%**. Vision-grounded constraints are the hardest category for every model.

### 5. **Holtercare-Bench: A Multimodal Benchmark for Evaluating Long-Term Dynamic ECG Analysis**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent runner-up.

Why it matters: This is the non-robotics medical paper worth carrying forward because it exposes how badly current multimodal models degrade once the signal is long, temporal, and clinically structured. The benchmark builds **22,980** QA pairs from **788** clinical Holter records and separates presence detection from the much harder counting, timing, diagnosis, and report-generation tasks. The baseline gap is concrete: **GPT-5-mini** reaches **76.31%** on presence detection but only **31.80%** on event counting and **33.54%** on event timing, while fine-tuned **Qwen3-VL-8B** jumps to **99.70%** on event timing, **95.28%** on diagnosis, and drops counting MAE from **7.34** to **0.63**.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **HarnessRisk: A Lifecycle-Oriented Benchmark for Agent Harness Safety**.

It lands directly on a cabbageland problem: the real object of safety is not the model in the abstract but the model as routed through tools, permissions, plugins, memory, action controls, and recovery workflows. That is why the harness flip matters more than the raw leaderboard. If the same model can be over four times less safe under a different harness, then "model safety" is an incomplete abstraction.

The rest of the batch reinforces the same taste. **Mechanistic Tomography** treats interpretability as a tested observer design problem, not a vibes problem. **Marginal Coverage** separates average reliability from tail reliability. **VSysBench** separates obedience theater from actual hierarchy robustness. **Holtercare-Bench** separates generic multimodal comfort from long-horizon clinical signal understanding.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**HarnessRisk** matters because it reframes agent safety around harness lifecycle responsibilities instead of one-off attack categories, then proves that high utility can coexist with substantial attack success and persistent compromise.

**Mechanistic Tomography** matters because it turns multiple interpretability tools into one shared inverse-problem language and gives a practical stopping rule: start cheap, validate on held-out interventions, then only add richer measurement families when the residual structure forces you to.

**Marginal Coverage** matters because it attacks a very common deployment inference directly. A respectable marginal coverage number is not evidence of class-tail safety under shift, and the standard source-side fixes do not rescue that tail.

**Compliance, Capability, and Conflict** matters because it jointly scores correctness and system-message adherence on the same multimodal instance. That sounds small, but it changes the failure picture: neither compliance nor accuracy can hide the other anymore.

**Holtercare-Bench** matters because it widens medical multimodal evaluation from static images to long dynamic ECG traces, making temporal localization and report generation first-class failure modes instead of afterthoughts.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful August 22 lesson is that a system usually fails first at the hidden interface between its average metric and the operational property you actually need. A safe-looking model can become unsafe under a different harness. A good-looking mechanistic explanation can collapse when asked to predict a held-out intervention. A valid marginal coverage number can hide a dead class tail. A compliant-looking multimodal model can surrender the hierarchy as soon as the user pushes back. A medical MLLM that looks competent on short or static inputs can fall apart on long dynamic traces. Same rule all day: do not trust a proxy until it has been forced to answer for the operational target directly.
