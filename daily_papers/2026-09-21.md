# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-09-21

## Theme

Today is a failure-carrier batch. The best papers do not just add another module, schedule, gate, or evaluation table. They name the failure, identify the variable that carries it, and test whether controlling that variable changes the outcome.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Benchmarking World Models for Continual Learning on Compositional Tasks**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model benchmark.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest cabbageland paper today because it separates "adapt fast" from "reuse what was already learned." The benchmark trains primitive manipulation tasks first, then ends each curriculum with a composition task that recombines prior primitives along action, perception, or both. That makes forward transfer a reuse test rather than a generic adaptation score. The strongest design lesson is also blunt: explicit modular dynamics nearly eliminate forgetting under a frozen privileged encoder, with BWT 3.85 and FWT 36.18 versus TD-MPC2's BWT 34.97 and FWT 38.11, but the advantage disappears without that encoder. Modularity helps only if the representation feeding it stays reusable.

### 2. **lambda-Controlled GRPO: Turning Flow-Matching Ratio Instability into a Budgeted Resource**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct generative-model RL paper.

Why it matters: This paper turns a messy Flow-GRPO stabilization problem into a measurable resource: per-step path variance. The finite-grid Gaussian transition kernel predicts the log-ratio mean and variance, so late-step ratio drift, dispersion, clipping imbalance, and loss of usable samples become symptoms of one scalar rather than separate hacks. On SD3.5 hard-OCR, the lambda-controlled method improves OCR reward from 0.5632 to 0.5831, exact match from 12.99% to 14.44%, and substring match from 19.29% to 22.83% over RatioNorm on 762 held-out prompts. On PickScore, it gains +0.0075 with a bootstrap CI excluding zero. The caveat is scope: FLUX task-level gains are not uniform, so the durable contribution is the transition-law budget, not a universal win claim.

### 3. **Abstention and Noise Filtering: Two Missing Primitives of Softmax Attention**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct architecture and interpretability paper.

Why it matters: This paper takes the vague observation that value gates help attention and splits it into two separate primitives. A learned sink logit gives a head a true zero option, while value gates filter interference in the read vector. The scale trend is the useful part: the zero-option gain falls from 0.0185 nats at 10M to 0.0013 at 350M because larger baselines build their own attention sinks, while filtering gains rise and dominate at larger scale. The combined sink-logit plus projection-gate arm has the best validation loss at every tested tier. This is a good example of replacing "gating adds nonlinearity" with a falsifiable decomposition.

### 4. **Available Guardrails: Certifying Selective Prediction across ML Systems**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct uncertainty and deployment paper.

Why it matters: The paper draws a sharp line between a valid selective predictor and one that finite calibration data can actually certify at the reporting granularity a deployment wants. It uses exact binomial inversion to compute certificate availability, then treats reporting partitions as a traffic-coverage planning problem. The support lesson is memorable: at target precision 0.90 and true error 0.05, one group needs 179 served calibration examples for 80% availability; fifty groups need 450 each. But if true error rises to 0.09, fifty groups need 13,407 examples. The caveat is equally important: held-out partition selection recovers some coverage, but no planner dominates strong controls. Availability is the mechanism, not the marketing claim.

### 5. **Beyond Benchmark Scores: Auditing Medical Vision-Language Models for Chest X-Ray Tuberculosis Screening**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent medical evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This is a strong evaluation-specification paper. It shows that "medical VLM X gets good AUROC" is not a portable claim unless the prompt, cohort, negative class, prevalence assumption, threshold, and reliability metric are named. Across 12,200 records and 244,000 model-image-prompt scores, prompt-family changes alter AUROC in 21 of 48 controlled comparisons. Replacing healthy controls with sick non-TB controls drops AUROC by 0.075 to 0.306. TBX11K thresholds chosen for 95% sensitivity retain that point-estimate constraint in only four of sixteen transports. A supervised source model reaches 0.999 AUROC on TBX11K validation but only 0.629 on Shenzhen and Montgomery. The paper is less about TB than about not confusing a checkpoint with an evaluation specification.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Benchmarking World Models for Continual Learning on Compositional Tasks**

It matters because cabbageland cares about reusable structure, not just better downstream scores. This benchmark makes reuse observable by ending each curriculum with a recombination of earlier primitives and by separating action composition from perception composition. The paper's central warning is valuable: explicit reusable experts help only when the latent representation is stable enough for reuse to mean something.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**World-model benchmark** matters because it isolates knowledge reuse from raw adaptation speed and shows that perception shifts are the harder bottleneck.

**lambda-Controlled GRPO** matters because it replaces empirical ratio patching with a transition-law scalar that predicts and budgets Flow-GRPO instability.

**Attention abstention/filtering** matters because it separates the zero option from value-pathway interference suppression and shows the scale crossover.

**Available Guardrails** matters because it makes finite-data certifiability a first-class deployment resource, distinct from validity.

**Medical VLM audit** matters because it turns benchmark portability into a named evaluation-specification problem.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 21 pattern is that useful structure has to name its carrier. World-model reuse is carried by compositional curricula and reusable experts, but only through stable representations. Flow-GRPO instability is carried by path variance. Attention's missing operation splits into zero output and filtering of superposed value reads. Selective-prediction safety is limited by certificate availability, not just validity. Medical screening claims are carried by the full evaluation specification, not by a checkpoint name. The shared lesson is severe and useful: if you cannot name the carrier, you probably cannot trust the claimed mechanism.
