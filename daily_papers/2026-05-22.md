# Daily Paper Digest — 2026-05-22

## Theme

Today’s strongest signal is **explicit intermediate structure that actually constrains generation or control**. The best paper is **DiLA: Disentangled Latent Action World Models** because it turns the vague goal of “abstract yet predictive latent actions” into a concrete architectural split between structure and content, then tests whether that split buys transfer and planning. **SceneCode** is the most interesting adjacent systems paper because it treats indoor scenes as executable programs with articulated object structure rather than static mesh soup. **Pelican-Unify 1.0** is ambitious and worth watching, but right now it reads more like a giant integration claim than a sharply isolating mechanism paper.

## Short overview

Brave Search and AlphaXiv were unavailable in this run, so discovery fell back to direct inspection of recent arXiv category listings and full-text arXiv HTML reads where accessible. I fully inspected arXiv HTML for **DiLA**, **SceneCode**, and **Pelican-Unify 1.0**. Confidence is good on their central mechanisms, task setup, and headline numbers, and weaker on appendix-level implementation details I did not independently reconstruct.

**DiLA** is the most relevant paper today. Its core move is to stop asking one latent pathway to carry both controllable dynamics and high-frequency appearance. Instead, it predicts future structural state through a bottlenecked latent action pathway while maintaining a separate content-memory pathway for visual detail, then fuses them for reconstruction. That is a much cleaner story than the usual LAM pattern where abstraction is purchased by wrecking generation quality or by discarding the learned forward model and handing rendering to a separate pretrained video model.

**SceneCode** is adjacent but genuinely useful. It compiles scene prompts into a room plan plus per-object executable Blender programs, then validates and repairs those programs before exporting simulation-ready assets with articulation metadata. The paper matters because it replaces opaque retrieved meshes with editable object programs and a persistent scene-state registry. The part I am less certain about is how far this scales beyond the relatively curated prompt set and the dominant hinged or sliding articulation patterns it targets.

**Pelican-Unify 1.0** makes the broadest claim, namely that understanding, reasoning, imagination, and action should be trained as one shared loop. I buy the direction more than the current evidence. The paper clearly has scale, and the shared latent interface between reasoning and joint video-plus-action generation is a real design choice, but the mechanism is not isolated as cleanly as DiLA’s and much of the story is still benchmark aggregation.

## Ranked papers

**Verdict:** highly relevant, and the most relevant paper today.

Why it matters: it proposes an explicit structure-content split that seems to do real work for action abstraction, generation fidelity, and planning instead of treating those goals as magically compatible.

### 2. **SceneCode: Executable World Programs for Editable Indoor Scenes with Articulated Objects**

**Verdict:** useful adjacent inspiration.

Why it matters: it treats scene generation as editable world construction with articulated object programs and persistent scene state, which is much closer to reusable simulation structure than static asset retrieval.

### 3. **Pelican-Unify 1.0: A Unified Embodied Intelligence Model for Understanding, Reasoning, Imagination and Action**

**Verdict:** interesting, but mostly framing pressure for now.

Why it matters: it is a serious large-scale attempt to put reasoning, future video generation, and action generation into one shared loop, even if the paper currently proves breadth more clearly than mechanism.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** DiLA: Disentangled Latent Action World Models.

The useful part of DiLA is not just that it says “disentanglement.” The useful part is the specific contract it imposes: latent actions are only allowed to model structural dynamics, while appearance and static detail are maintained in a separate content pathway with memory. That means the model is not forced to choose between an abstract action bottleneck and a visually competent forward model in the same way older LAMs often are.

This matters for cabbageland because it is exactly the kind of paper that tries to replace latent mush with **typed internal structure that changes what different parts of the model are allowed to represent**. The evaluation is also more aligned with the claim than usual. The paper checks not just reconstruction metrics, but action transfer, latent manifold behavior, and model-predictive visual planning. I am still somewhat uncertain how much of the gain comes from the disentanglement idea itself versus the entire decoder-and-memory package around it, but the architecture is at least making a falsifiable representation claim.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

The framing update from **DiLA** is that latent action papers should probably be judged by whether they cleanly separate *what must stay controllable* from *what must stay reconstructable*. A lot of recent latent action work quietly escapes this problem by either weakening the forward model target or outsourcing generation to a separate pretrained model. DiLA is more interesting because it keeps a single-stage story and tries to solve the trade-off inside the model.

**SceneCode** sharpens a different point. If a generated world is supposed to support interaction, then editable object structure and motion metadata should be first-class outputs, not afterthought annotations on top of static meshes. That is a good novelty lens for future 3D and simulation papers: ask whether the representation really exposes state, parts, and mechanisms.

**Pelican-Unify 1.0** mostly affects framing rather than immediate design choice. It is a reminder that shared latent interfaces between reasoning, imagination, and action are becoming a live target. But the paper does not isolate enough ablations yet to tell which parts of the unification claim are necessary rather than merely co-present.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best paper today is **DiLA** because it offers a concrete, legible answer to a real world-model problem: how to keep latent actions abstract without trashing generation quality. **SceneCode** is the best adjacent paper because it treats 3D scene synthesis as executable world construction with articulated structure and editable state, which is much closer to the kinds of reusable representations embodied work actually wants. **Pelican-Unify 1.0** is ambitious and maybe important, but I would currently treat it more as evidence that unified embodied foundation-model training is being seriously attempted than as a clean mechanism paper. The shared lesson is that explicit structural separation helps most when it meaningfully constrains what a subsystem is allowed to represent or output.
