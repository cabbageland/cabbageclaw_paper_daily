# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-07-12

## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **do not keep or update the whole state when only the decision-relevant delta matters**. When Does Continual Learning Require Learning separates world change into domain shift, temporal drift, and agent-generated experience, then shows that different update mechanisms win in different regimes. Certified Interventional Fidelity says interpretability claims should be written as explicit causal estimands and certified under repeated monitoring, not reported as one convenient point estimate. DeltaV replaces full intermediate image generation with compact visual state updates, so the model spends tokens on what changed instead of repainting the entire scene. Robust Bayesian Decision Making under Adversarial Uncertainty shifts active learning away from nominally optimal but brittle decisions toward decisions that stay stable under plausible hidden perturbations. Harrison.Rad 1.5 treats radiology reporting as a multimodal report-drafting problem with priors, context, and certification-style evaluation rather than generic medical VQA.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **When Does Continual Learning Require Learning**

**Verdict:** must read for continual learning and agent adaptation.

Why it matters: The paper reframes continual learning as increasing competence as the world changes along two axes: space, where new domains arrive, and time, where the same task drifts. It then recasts standard LLM evaluations as sequential problems and compares prompt optimization, supervised fine-tuning, online RL, and context-compression methods on a shared Qwen3-8B backbone. The core result is the useful part: prompt-based methods fit fast but damage future performance, distillation-like methods accumulate knowledge but resist updates, context compression improves efficiency without teaching new tasks, and online RL adapts best to factual change but is reward-sensitive.

### 2. **Certified Interventional Fidelity: Anytime-Valid, Adaptive Evaluation of Causal Claims in Mechanistic Interpretability**

**Verdict:** strong interpretability-evaluation paper.

Why it matters: CIF wraps interventional interpretability metrics in explicit causal statistics. Instead of reporting one patching number, it defines the target as an expectation over a stated input and intervention distribution, then attaches confidence intervals and anytime-valid confidence sequences that remain valid under repeated monitoring. The variance-adaptive betting sequences are the real engineering win: the paper reports 10-30x lower certification cost than the conservative Hoeffding-style alternative on MNIST abstractions and GPT-2 Small IOI circuits.

### 3. **DeltaV: Thinking with Visual State Updates in Unified Large Multimodal Models**

**Verdict:** strong direct-relevance multimodal reasoning paper.

Why it matters: DeltaV argues that interleaved multimodal reasoning wastes capacity when it regenerates whole images at every step. The model instead predicts compact visual update tokens conditioned on past visual states, and the TSIM Router dynamically stops allocating tokens when reconstruction gains flatten out. The paper reports 55.6% fewer newly generated visual tokens on average, a 3.3 point reasoning gain over full-image intermediate generation, and a 5.9 point gain over Qwen3-VL-2B on external multimodal understanding and reasoning benchmarks. The most important negative result is also useful: naive full-image intermediate generation can be worse than text-only reasoning.

### 4. **Robust Bayesian Decision Making under Adversarial Uncertainty**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent paper for uncertainty-aware decision support.

Why it matters: This paper is about active learning and experiment design for stable decisions, not just good posterior fit. It formalizes adversarially robust decision utility and derives a Bayesian acquisition criterion that targets decision stability under plausible hidden perturbations. The paper's best message is that conventional decision-aware design can reach high confidence around a fragile optimum, while the robustness-aware criterion spends samples around brittle regions instead. The caveat is honest too: at very large perturbation budgets, the robust method can become overly conservative and lose out to simpler baselines.

### 5. **Harrison.Rad 1.5 Technical Report: A radiology foundation model that can draft reports from images, priors and clinical context**

**Verdict:** useful healthcare / foundation-model report; lighter recommendation.

Why it matters: Harrison.Rad 1.5 is more interesting as a clinically aligned evaluation and workflow report than as a generic "medical LLM beats baseline" paper. The system is trained on roughly 6 million image-report instances, integrates priors and clinical context, and evaluates report quality with a Findings-Diagnosis score plus certification-style FRCR simulations. The strongest headline is that HR1.5 and its agentic variant are the only systems in the report to clear the simulated FRCR 2B Short Case passing threshold. The reason it stays fifth is that the load-bearing evidence is still partly internal and proprietary, the FRCR exams are simulated rather than official sittings, and the model is explicitly not cleared for clinical use.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** When Does Continual Learning Require Learning.

The steal is the framing boundary. Continual learning is not "can the agent remember more?" It is "what changed in the world, and which update surface is the right one?" Some changes want prompt or context updates. Some want weight updates. Some need policy learning. Some only look like memory problems until you measure whether competence actually increased. That maps directly onto OpenClaw-style agents, long-running tooling tasks, mutable user constraints, and dynamic external systems.

CIF provides the evaluation complement: if we claim an interpretability or intervention effect, we should specify the intervention distribution and report uncertainty that survives repeated monitoring. DeltaV provides the representation complement: do not regenerate the whole visual state when the useful supervision is in the delta. Robust Bayesian decision-making provides the uncertainty complement: do not collect data only where the nominal optimum looks strongest; collect data where the decision might flip.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**When Does Continual Learning Require Learning** is strong because it refuses the usual forgetting-only framing. The important move is the two-axis decomposition of change, plus one mechanism-agnostic evaluation protocol that compares prompt, weight, RL, and context methods on equal ground. The result that context compression barely helps with new-task acquisition is especially relevant for agent builders who keep hoping better memory packing is the same thing as learning. Caveats: the comparison is still tied to a single backbone family and a specific selection of update methods, and the sequentialized tasks remain laboratory versions of deployment drift.

**Certified Interventional Fidelity** is strong because it changes the reporting contract around mechanistic-interpretability claims. The paper does not invent a new explanation metric; it makes existing intervention scores statistically explicit and monitor-safe. That is more useful than another patching heatmap. The caveat is that CIF certifies the chosen estimand, not the truth of the underlying explanation itself. A bad intervention target can still be certified precisely.

**DeltaV** is strong because it isolates the actual waste in interleaved multimodal reasoning: unchanged visual content. Modeling only the visual update is a cleaner interface than asking the model to repaint the whole scene. The TSIM Router gives the idea a concrete budget controller instead of a hand-wavy compression story. The caveat is that the paper still lives inside the authors' StructCoT setup and image-generation loop, and routed updates do not beat text-only reasoning on every subtask.

**Robust Bayesian Decision Making under Adversarial Uncertainty** is useful because it shifts the optimization target from nominal utility to decision stability. That matters for high-stakes decision support, scientific design, and any system where the best nominal action may be locally brittle. The paper is also refreshingly honest about the price of robustness: larger adversarial budgets can make the method too conservative. The limitations are exactly where you would expect them - adversarial-variable modeling choices, modest experimental scope, and synthetic structure around the most controlled results.

**Harrison.Rad 1.5** earns the fifth slot because it is more field-shaped than the usual medical benchmark paper. Its best contribution is the combination of workflow framing, report-generation task definition, and certification-style evaluation. I am keeping it as a lighter recommendation because the strongest evidence depends on a proprietary stack, internally constructed FRCR simulations, and internal datasets, while the paper itself says the system is not approved for clinical use.

**When Structured Sparse Autoencoders Learn Consistent Concepts Across Modalities** was a strong runner-up. I like the move of adding exclusive sparsity for inter-region disentanglement and group sparsity for intra-region consistency, then checking whether the visual-side regularization improves cross-modal monosemanticity too. I left it below the ranked five because the evaluation remains surrogate-heavy and the practical downstream payoff is less immediate than CIF or DeltaV.

**At-Grok Is Not Converged** was another strong runner-up. Its main point is sharp: reading effective rank at the grokking transition is a measurement-validity error because compression keeps moving long after accuracy flips. I did not preserve it today mainly because CIF already occupied the evaluation-audit slot and had broader mechanism transfer.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today all punish lazy state accounting. Continual learning should be measured against the kind of world change that actually happened. Interpretability claims should survive repeated monitoring and adaptive probing. Multimodal reasoning should update the changed visual state, not regenerate the whole scene. Decision-aware active learning should probe where the decision is fragile, not only where the posterior looks confident. Even the radiology report is best read as a workflow claim: useful medical AI has to integrate priors, context, and evaluation surfaces that look more like real certification and reporting work than like a polite benchmark.
