## Ranked papers

### 1. **Stochastic World Models for Verifying Vision-Based Neural Feedback Systems**

**Verdict:** must read direct verification/world-model paper.

Why it matters: The paper attacks a precise bottleneck in vision-based safety verification: the controller acts on camera observations, but verification needs a compact, boundable surrogate for what the camera can show. Instead of using a large GAN with ungrounded random latents, the authors train a small renderer-like stochastic world model whose latents correspond to physical appearance factors such as lighting. The surrogate uses verifier-friendly operations and FiLM modulation, then plugs into a reach-avoid verifier combining falsification, forward analysis, adaptive refinement, symbolic analysis, and backward analysis. The result is unusually concrete: on a grayscale emergency-braking benchmark it resolves all 10,000 cells, including the 38% left unresolved by prior work; on the RGB benchmark it resolves over 80% of the state space with a higher-fidelity world model surrogate. The main caveat is also important: the safety guarantee is for the surrogate system, and no formal bridge yet proves the surrogate covers the real camera.

### 2. **Imagine3D-LLM: Teaching MLLMs to Imagine 3D Scenes Before Answering**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct 3D-reasoning paper.

Why it matters: This is a clean example of forcing an MLLM to build an intermediate spatial state instead of asking it to hallucinate 3D reasoning from image tokens alone. Imagine3D-LLM appends Gaussian summary tokens after multi-view image tokens, decodes middle-layer hidden states from those tokens into compact 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives, and trains them with photometric reconstruction plus language modeling and teacher distillation. The useful idea is the bottleneck: fewer Gaussian tokens than image tokens encourage object-level cross-view grouping rather than patch-by-patch copying. On seven 3D reasoning and scene understanding benchmarks, it beats a controlled baseline with the same backbone, data, and schedule, with a large gain on SPAR-Bench. The caveat is that the method leans on indoor ScanNet-style data and a fixed Gaussian budget, so the abstraction may not scale cleanly to large outdoor scenes.

### 3. **Honeycomb: Constant-Size Scene Memory Representation for Video World Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct video-world-model memory paper.

Why it matters: Honeycomb addresses the part of video world models that usually turns into a growing cache. Its HexMemory stores scene features in six fixed-size spatial and spatiotemporal planes, writes each new generated chunk with a feed-forward writer, warps old planes when bounds expand, and fuses old and new features with confidence-weighted pooling plus a learned residual correction. A reader retrieves latents from this memory to condition a camera-controlled video diffusion backbone. The mechanism is attractive because memory size and write cost stay bounded while the rollout grows. On RealEstate10K, Honeycomb improves novel-view PSNR to 18.45 dB versus 17.46 for LSM-World and 15.58 for Spatia; on closed-loop revisit evaluation it improves PSNR to 17.22 and reduces flow error to 3.00 pixels. The red flag is dependence on estimated depth and camera geometry, which means the memory is only as stable as that reconstruction pipeline.

### 4. **Multi-Agent Flow Matching with Decoupled Generative Guidance**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct flow-matching/control paper.

Why it matters: DeGG-Flow asks how a generative process can satisfy hard coupled requirements when each agent computes its own guidance input. The paper frames the flow-matching sampler as a control-affine dynamical system and adds decoupled guidance for two settings: shared requirements involving multiple agents and private requirements whose satisfaction depends on neighbors. The technical contribution is not just "use guidance"; it derives feasibility conditions, finite-horizon convergence guarantees, and a Wasserstein bound on how much guidance distorts the nominal distribution. In experiments, guided generation reaches 250/250 success on both a multi-robot bridge-building task and a multi-object scene arrangement task, including team sizes unseen during training. The scope is still stylized, but the design lesson is useful: constraints can be made part of the sampling dynamics without retraining or post-generation repair.

### 5. **FORM: Robot Manipulation through Direct Material Law Identification**

**Verdict:** highly relevant robotics paper that clears the higher bar.

Why it matters: FORM is the one robotics paper today that earns preservation because it replaces an implicit dynamics latent with an explicit constitutive law. From a single robot probing interaction, it reconstructs material motion and contact loads, writes weak-form momentum balance equations, and solves a linear least-squares problem for material coefficients. The recovered law is used directly inside the same MPM simulator for planning new actions and geometries. The payoff is practical: identification drops from roughly 10-25 minutes for iterative baselines to 2-5 seconds while staying competitive on elastic, granular, elastoplastic, and fluid materials; in manipulation tests, matched identified models succeed where swapped material models fail badly. The caveat is that the method still depends on motion reconstruction assumptions and a chosen library of analytic or learned constitutive bases.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Stochastic World Models** matters because it turns the perception surrogate into a physically grounded, verifier-friendly object instead of an unstructured image generator.

**Imagine3D-LLM** matters because it shows that an MLLM can benefit from being forced to build a compact reconstructive 3D state before answering.

**Honeycomb** matters because it makes persistent video memory constant-size and recurrent rather than a growing cache of points or latents.

**DeGG-Flow** matters because it turns hard coupled constraints into finite-horizon guidance inside the flow sampler, without retraining.

**FORM** matters because it recovers explicit material laws quickly enough to condition robot planning on one physical probe.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 30 pattern is accountable intermediate state. The verifier needs camera variation expressed as bounded physical latents. The 3D reasoner needs a compact scene reconstruction, not just more visual tokens. The video world model needs a memory representation that can be revisited without unbounded growth. The flow sampler needs constraints that shape generation before the sample is final. The robot needs a material law it can put into a simulator. The shared lesson is compact and severe: if a representation is supposed to support planning, verification, memory, or control, it has to be built for that job.
