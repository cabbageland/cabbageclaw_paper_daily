## Ranked papers

### 1. **Control-Geometry Straightening for Sampling-Based Latent Planning**

**Verdict:** must read direct latent-world-model planning paper.

Why it matters: The paper targets a failure mode that ordinary next-latent prediction misses: a world model can predict local transitions while arranging its latent space so action search is badly conditioned. Control-Geometry Straightening adds a pairwise similarity loss that makes latent transition differences preserve action-space geometry. The claimed payoff is practical, not decorative: with the same LeWM-style predictive setup, CGS gives sampling-based planners smoother curvature, finite-budget guarantees for MPPI, local contraction arguments for CEM, and up to 20 percentage-point success gains over LeWM on evaluated control tasks. The strongest transferable idea is that representation learning for world models should optimize the geometry the planner will actually search, not just the prediction target.

### 2. **Manifold-Stable Flow Matching**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct generative-model/control paper.

Why it matters: This is a clean mechanism paper for flow matching on constrained data. Standard flow matching can have low velocity regression error while its trajectories drift off the valid data manifold. MSFM separates learned tangential transport from prescribed normal contraction, using analytic projectors for known manifolds and local PCA proxies for unknown manifolds. The useful bit is not just "manifold awareness"; it is a dynamical guarantee that samples converge transversely to the manifold from an ambient prior. In Push-T it raises disturbed success from 37/50 to 41/50 while reducing proxy error by orders of magnitude, and in Robomimic Square it improves success from 30/50 to 36/50 while cutting rotation-manifold deviation from roughly 1e-2 to 1e-7. This is a good example of hard-coding the part of geometry the learner should not have to rediscover.

### 3. **VideoPhysEdit: Physical Counterfactual Video Editing via Rigid-Body Physical Scene Reconstruction**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct physical-video paper.

Why it matters: VideoPhysEdit treats video editing as a physical intervention problem rather than a visual inpainting problem. It reconstructs an executable rigid-body scene whose simulation reproduces observed motion and contacts, applies an insertion, removal, or parameter-change intervention, rolls the simulated counterfactual forward, and uses those trajectories to guide video generation. The paper also introduces PCVE-RigidBench with paired factual and counterfactual videos plus physical ground truth, and a Physical Edit Score that measures whether an edit moves trajectories toward the counterfactual target rather than away from the source. Its PES is 0.376, the only positive score among compared systems. The scope is still narrow, but the framing is right: if an edit changes physical causes, the downstream motion should come from a reconstructed state transition, not from a prompt hoping the model remembers physics.

### 4. **DynaTokens: Teaching Dynamics to Camera-Controlled Video Models at Test Time**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct video-world-model adaptation paper.

Why it matters: DynaTokens separates two motions that camera-controlled video models tend to conflate: global camera motion and local object or scene dynamics. Instead of LoRA-adapting a frozen camera-controlled model and disturbing the global camera interface, it adds scene-specific tokens read through cross-attention, trains only those tokens on a few example trajectories, and lets the base model stay fixed. The empirical story is pointed: DynaTokens outperforms LoRA, block finetuning, and TTT layers on VBench2 dynamic categories and WorldScore, while token attention localizes to moving regions. It is not a general dynamics learner yet, but it is a useful interface lesson: put the trainable adapter where the causal variation lives.

### 5. **Weighting Schedules Govern What and When Score-Based Generative Models Learn from Multimodal Data**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion-theory paper.

Why it matters: This paper explains a training-design issue that is usually tuned by folklore. In multimodal score-based models, the denoising trajectory commits to modes around a narrow speciation time. The paper shows that high-SNR regions can learn mode directions without learning relative mode weights, while learning around the speciation scale exposes both directions and weights on feature-dependent timescales. Recasting diffusion and flow-matching objectives through effective SNR weightings explains why different schedules learn different distributional structure, and the experiments carry the pattern beyond Gaussian mixtures to tinted MNIST and human haplotype generation. The future-useful point is direct: a noise weighting is not just an optimization detail; it selects which aspects of the data distribution become learnable and when.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 29 pattern is interface discipline. A latent world model should be shaped for action search. A flow model should not be trusted to learn validity constraints that can be imposed geometrically. A physical video edit should rebuild state, intervene, and roll forward, not just repaint pixels. A camera-controlled video adapter should touch local dynamics without trampling the camera path. A diffusion schedule should be treated as an information-allocation mechanism, not a harmless training knob. The shared lesson is compact: the representation is not done until the next computation can use it.
