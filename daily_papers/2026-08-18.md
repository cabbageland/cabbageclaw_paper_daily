# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-18

## Theme

The strongest papers today all reject fake sufficiency. A compliance detector is not rule-aware just because it flags bad-looking scenarios. A knowledge store is not governed just because it keeps a trace. A video world model is not physically competent just because single samples look plausible. A pathology foundation model is not really scalable if it still depends on hand-labeled cells. A long-horizon robot stack is not compositional if it cannot represent the handoff state between subtasks.

## Short overview

This Tuesday, August 18, 2026 run used live arXiv HTML full-text inspection across compliance auditing, governed knowledge stores, stochastic world-model evaluation, histopathology foundation models, and long-horizon robotics. I inspected the arXiv HTML full text for all five ranked papers below.

Four papers earn preserved notes. **Don't Drop the Baton** is the one robotics paper worth carrying today, but it stays just below the preserve bar because the mechanism is sharper than the evidence ecology: the benchmark is still self-contained, the stack is highly specific, and broader transfer remains unproven. **Most relevant today:** **Quipu**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **What Do Compliance Detectors Read? An Audit of Activation Probes and Guard Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest detector-audit paper in the batch because it asks the right embarrassing question. The issue is not just whether a guard model scores well. It is whether it reads the supplied rule at all. The paper introduces a training-free Internal Compliance Score (ICS), then uses it to audit probes, guards, and public benchmarks. The main result is ugly and useful: deleting, permuting, or swapping the rule causes **0/20** significant drops across their robustness checks, while **4 of 7** public compliance benchmarks are so lexically degenerate that a policy-blind bag-of-words model can solve them. ICS still has practical value as a cheap selector, raising IFEval verified pass by **5.2** points, but the paper is strongest precisely because it refuses to confuse utility with solved understanding.

### 2. **Quipu: A Governed Bitemporal Knowledge Graph Store**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the most cabbageland paper of the day. It treats agent-written knowledge as something that should be governed at write time, not cleaned up later by dashboards and good intentions. Quipu gates writes against the pending post-state, keeps signed verdict facts even for denied writes, makes data, trust labels, verdicts, and rules bitemporal, and composes graph authority under a non-widening lattice. On the seeded Census lifecycle it ends with **0 of 6** planted defects versus **6 of 6** for the ungated control, re-derives **50 of 50** satisfied verdicts as of their decision instant, and gets **PSA 1.0** with zero overclaim on its exported DEMM-style evidence readout.

### 3. **CaliBench: Are the Stochastic Dynamics of Video World Models Physically Calibrated?**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the right benchmark for a world-model field that keeps grading itself on single-sample aesthetics. CaliBench evaluates stochastic physical scenes in closed-form discrete outcome spaces such as Galton boards, dice, cards, roulette, and lottery draws, so the reference distribution is known exactly. Across six frontier video models and nine environments, the paper shows that realistic-looking rollouts still collapse the output distribution. The best aggregate calibration error in the suite is still a poor **0.39** mean normalized TV distance, and the best headline compliance rate is only **63.3%**. This is a real benchmark contribution because it isolates distributional calibration from mere sample plausibility.

### 4. **CytoFormer: A Molecularly Supervised Cell Foundation Model for Histopathology Cell Classification**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent direct paper.

Why it matters: This is a strong example of using a paired modality to replace expensive annotation rather than merely decorate it. CytoFormer uses paired Xenium spatial transcriptomics and H&E to obtain molecularly grounded cell labels for **15.4 million** cells across **81** sections and **16** organs, then trains a ViT-giant cell model without manual image labeling. On spatially held-out tissue it reaches **0.846** accuracy and **0.779** macro-F1, transfers well to four public cell-classification benchmarks, and in an interactive setting reaches **0.82** F1 for separating normal epithelium from look-alike tumor using only a small number of annotations, beating the strongest baseline by **0.13** F1.

### 5. **Don't Drop the Baton: Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Agentic Subtask Exploration and Transition-Aware Memory**

**Verdict:** adjacent inspiration, not yet preserve-worthy.

Why it matters: This is the only robotics paper today that genuinely earns attention, because it identifies the right unit of failure. Long-horizon manipulation does not just need better subtask skills; it needs explicit representation of the transition state that one subtask hands to the next. Baton explores at the subtask level and stores invocation, handoff, and lookahead transitions in language memory, improving RoboMemArena by **11.6** points in task success and **14.9** points in cumulative success. The reason it stays below the preserve bar is not weak mechanism. It is ecology: the main evidence is still tied to the paper's own benchmark and a very specific LLM-plus-frozen-VLA setup.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Quipu**.

Its core claim is exactly the kind of architecture rule that tends to survive contact with reality: if agents are allowed to write knowledge, governance cannot remain an after-the-fact wrapper. It has to live inside the write path, inside the time model, and inside the authority composition logic. The same general lesson shows up everywhere else in the digest. **Compliance Detectors** says the rule has to stay operational at decision time, not just appear in the prompt. **CaliBench** says the distribution has to be evaluated directly, not inferred from attractive samples. **Baton** says a composite task needs explicit transition state, not just stage competence. Same taste across domains: stop treating the container as proof that the content is usable.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Compliance Detectors** matters because it turns guard-model evaluation inside out. A high detector score is not enough if rule ablations leave behavior unchanged. The benchmark audit is part of the contribution, not an appendix afterthought.

**Quipu** matters because it makes policy, trace, and verdict first-class facts in the same store they govern. The useful novelty is not "AI-native knowledge graph" branding. It is the combination of post-state gating, bitemporal governance, and non-widening graph composition.

**CaliBench** matters because it measures the thing world-model papers keep implying but rarely test: whether multiple samples from the same macro-state form the right physical distribution.

**CytoFormer** matters because it uses paired spatial transcriptomics as supervision for morphology, which is a much better answer than simply asking pathologists to annotate more nuclei.

**Baton** matters because it reframes long-horizon robotics around transition representation and additive exploration cost. It just has not yet proved that the idea survives outside its current stack.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 18 lesson is that explicit operational units matter more than generic artifacts. If the unit of compliance is the rule-scenario relation, measure that rather than scenario vibes. If the unit of governance is the write decision, keep the verdict and the rule snapshot in the same auditable substrate. If the unit of world-model competence is a stochastic outcome distribution, test that directly instead of admiring plausible videos. If the unit of pathology supervision is the cell, use a modality that actually knows what cell it is. If the unit of long-horizon control is the transition, store that transition rather than hoping stage success will compose. Same severe moral all day: stop letting proxy artifacts pretend to be the thing itself.
