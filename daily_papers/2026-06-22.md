## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **do not average away the variable that carries the risk**. The strongest papers I found all attack a different kind of hidden aggregation failure: an agent monitor that collapses uncertain predicates into local thresholds, a calibration theorem that previously needed prediction-time randomness, and a federated medical calibration protocol where pooled guarantees hide vulnerable hospitals.

I deliberately kept robotics and VLA work out of the top three. The scan covered agent verification, calibration theory, medical uncertainty, MLLM bias, continual adaptation, EEG foundation-model evaluation, Bayesian in-context learning, and navigation uncertainty. **GroundControl** was the best robotics/navigation candidate, but it did not beat the strongest non-robotics papers on mechanism or transfer value today.

**Efficient and Sound Probabilistic Verification for AI Agents** is the most relevant paper today. It treats agent monitoring as probabilistic inference over an execution trace instead of local thresholding of noisy predicates. The useful move is the distributionally robust bound: the monitor does not get to assume independence just because independence would make the action look safe.

**Optimal Deterministic Multicalibration and Omniprediction** is the strongest theory paper in the scan. It resolves whether prediction-time randomization is statistically necessary for sample-optimal multicalibration and extends the same derandomization idea to outcome indistinguishability and omniprediction.

**When Calibration Fails the Vulnerable Hospital** is the strongest medical/healthcare paper today. It shows pooled federated conformal risk control can satisfy an average guarantee while failing 40 percent of hospitals in a real brain-tumor segmentation setting, then proposes a shrinkage risk-curve protocol that trades coverage and efficiency more sanely than local calibration.

Several runner-ups were useful but stayed below the note line. **StylisticBias** is a good MLLM bias benchmark: holding synthetic identity fixed while changing one visual cue reveals that roughly 15 appearance attributes account for nearly 80 percent of aggregate bias. I kept it as a runner-up because the setup is synthetic and the mechanism is more audit design than modeling primitive. **GroundControl** is a clean robotics/navigation uncertainty paper: distance-to-goal dynamics plus risk-coverage evaluation beats entropy-style uncertainty on EB-Navigation, but it stayed out of the top three under the topic-balance guardrail. **FlowEdit** has an attractive external-memory pronunciation adaptation mechanism for frozen TTS, but the paper is short and narrower than the preserved agent-verification and medical-calibration papers.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Efficient and Sound Probabilistic Verification for AI Agents**

**Verdict:** must read, and the most relevant paper today.

Why it matters: it gives agent runtime monitors a way to reason about uncertain policy predicates without collapsing them into brittle local binary decisions. The Datalog derivation graph plus distributionally robust SDP relaxation is a concrete template for enforcing global risk bounds under ambiguity.

### 2. **Optimal Deterministic Multicalibration and Omniprediction**

**Verdict:** highly relevant as calibration and evaluation theory.

Why it matters: it removes prediction-time randomness from sample-optimal multicalibration and omniprediction. That matters because randomization makes auditing, reproducibility, and downstream decisions messier exactly where calibration is supposed to improve trust.

### 3. **When Calibration Fails the Vulnerable Hospital: Federated Conformal Risk Control via Risk-Curve Shrinkage**

**Verdict:** highly relevant as medical deployment calibration.

Why it matters: it makes the marginal-versus-site failure visible. A pooled conformal guarantee can look fine while specific hospitals miss tumor voxels above the target false-negative rate. The paper's useful object is the per-site empirical risk curve, not another aggregate metric.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Efficient and Sound Probabilistic Verification for AI Agents.

The steal is the monitor shape. If an agent is about to mutate or transmit state, the monitor should not ask a sequence of isolated yes/no questions and then pretend the resulting trace is safe. It should preserve uncertainty across the trace, compile the dependency structure, and compute a global risk bound for the candidate action.

The multicalibration paper contributes a complementary lesson: if a trust property depends on random coins at prediction time, it is worth asking whether those coins are essential or just an artifact of the proof. Here they were not essential. The paper's adaptive interval-plus-rounding construction is not a plug-and-play engineering recipe, but the framing is valuable.

The federated CRC paper contributes the deployment lesson: average calibration is not enough when the harmed unit is a hospital, client, group, or environment. If the deployment unit matters, the calibration object has to keep that unit visible.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Efficient and Sound Probabilistic Verification for AI Agents** raises the bar for agent monitor papers. It models tool-call trajectories as Datalog derivation graphs, formulates exact worst-case policy-violation risk as an LP over joint measures, and uses a polynomial SDP relaxation to get a sound upper bound without assuming predicate independence. On Intercode, ATBench, and Praline side-channel tasks, the SDP monitor preserves security while improving utility and avoiding the long solver tails of exact SMT-style refinement. The caveats are serious: the guarantee is only as good as the tool semantics, predicate marginals, and policy specification, and long traces can make robust bounds collapse toward 1.

**Optimal Deterministic Multicalibration and Omniprediction** is a clean theoretical advance. Earlier sample-optimal multicalibration algorithms output randomized predictors; deterministic predictors had worse epsilon dependence. This paper handles the atom problem by learning context-dependent confidence intervals, producing a randomized predictor whose support respects those intervals, partitioning the context space into rounding cells, and fixing one sampler seed per cell. The main theorem gives deterministic ECE multicalibration at the minimax randomized rate, roughly Otilde(epsilon^-3) in polynomial group regimes. The limitation is practical distance: this is theory, with finite or covered test classes and nontrivial implementation machinery.

**When Calibration Fails the Vulnerable Hospital** is valuable because the failure mode is concrete. On FeTS-2022 brain tumor segmentation, naive pooled CRC violates the alpha = 0.10 target at 8 of 20 institutions while looking marginally calibrated; local per-site CRC mostly restores coverage but blows stretch up to 83x. The shrinkage protocol sends only G-scalar risk curves from each site and uses n0 as a coverage-efficiency dial; the LOSO-selected n0 = 19 gets 2.7 violations at 2.0x stretch. The caveats: the practical per-site thresholds are empirically validated rather than formally site-conditional, and the result needs replication beyond one dataset/backbone.

**StylisticBias** is the strongest runner-up for multimodal bias. It is useful because it isolates attribute-level shifts by editing one visual cue while holding synthetic identity fixed. The main result is not that MLLMs are biased, which is unsurprising, but that the bias mass is concentrated in a small set of cues and amplified when the queried judgment is appearance-aligned. The limitation is also clear: synthetic faces reduce privacy problems but can import generator stereotypes and may not fully represent real deployment inputs.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today all refuse an easy average. **Efficient and Sound Probabilistic Verification for AI Agents** keeps uncertain predicates alive until the whole agent trace can be bounded. **Optimal Deterministic Multicalibration and Omniprediction** removes prediction-time coins from calibration guarantees without losing the optimal sample rate. **When Calibration Fails the Vulnerable Hospital** keeps hospital identity visible instead of hiding failures inside a pooled guarantee. Different domains, same standard: if the risk lives in a dependency, a group, or a randomization channel, do not summarize it away before testing the claim.
