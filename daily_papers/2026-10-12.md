## Ranked papers

### 1. **GLIMPSE: Global-Local Indexing with Multi-Precision Spectral Encoding for Explorable Video World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct video-world-model memory paper.

Why it matters: GLIMPSE isolates a subtle failure mode in camera-controlled video world models. If a model stores old features and later renders them into a target view, imperfect pose and depth can turn memory into ghosting. GLIMPSE instead transports addresses, not content, and deliberately makes those addresses multi-precision and geometry-conditioned so inaccurate projections do not enter attention with false certainty. On Y-trajectory revisit tests, it raises the ten-component WorldScore mean to 66.552 versus 63.994 for ReWorld and 63.415 for UCM, and improves inlier counts and local memory ablations without increasing the local read budget. The steal-worthy idea is that memory is not only what you store; it is how precisely you let geometry address it.

### 2. **On the Design Space of Continuous Diffusion Language Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct diffusion-language-model design paper.

Why it matters: This paper turns a messy CDLM design space into two concrete mechanisms: train/inference trajectory mismatch and token identifiability under large Gaussian noise. DoubleCat recovers much of self-conditioning's benefit without the extra forward pass, raising TinyGSM accuracy from 4.42% to 16.00%. SemiCat adds stochastic discrete reveals and reaches 49.94% on TinyGSM with tuned sampling; at 1.7B scale it beats discrete diffusion baselines on four of six likelihood-style tasks and dominates one-hot flows on unconditional generation. The useful lesson is not "continuous diffusion works now"; it is that language diffusion needs the state representation to make the endpoint identifiable.

### 3. **Prediction Is Not a Sufficient Memory State**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct memory / learning-state theory paper.

Why it matters: The paper gives a crisp mathematical version of a problem cabbageland keeps circling: a state can answer today's queries while being unable to learn correctly tomorrow. For ridge regression, the predictor costs dp coordinates, but the exact learning state also needs the design matrix, adding d(d+1)/2 hidden coordinates. More generally, smooth convex residual losses have fixed-dimensional exact recurrent state only for polynomial losses; nonpolynomial losses have unbounded requirements even among histories with the same current predictor. The design lesson is brutal and useful: preserve continuation-identifiable evidence, not just the current readout.

### 4. **The Predictability Bias of Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning in Medical Imaging**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent medical representation-learning paper.

Why it matters: This is a strong warning about predictive self-supervision. TAP-JEPA is a serious ViT-B/3D CT encoder trained on 113,611 volumes and competitive with state-of-the-art CT encoders, but when the authors replay the exact JEPA teacher/predictor computation, prediction preserves anatomy much better than sporadic pathology. Pathology Skill drops from 0.434 to 0.298 in ATLAS and 0.341 to 0.243 in ReX, while anatomy drops much less. In several scan-level tasks, deleting all final-layer pathology-overlapping tokens changes the decision margin by at most 0.45%. The point generalizes: context-predictable structure is not the same thing as task-critical information.

### 5. **VideoAWR: Reinforcing Video Flow Models with Decoupled-Timestep Advantage-Weighted Rectification**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent video-flow RL paper.

Why it matters: VideoAWR fixes a practical failure in regression-style RL for video flow models. Unguided rollouts are theoretically aligned with the flow objective but fall into low-quality regions where rewards stop being meaningful; guided rollouts restore useful exploration but training on the CFG-guided field causes a geometric guidance blow-up. VideoAWR separates the two: an EMA policy samples guided rollouts, while the trainable policy is rectified against the unguided conditional anchor. At a 600 GPU-hour budget on Wan2.1-1.3B, it reaches VBench aesthetic quality 69.18, subject consistency 98.48, and object class 92.53, leading the reproduced baselines on three of four reported general-quality metrics. The reusable idea is behavior-anchored optimization: use a helpful behavior policy without baking its bias into the target field.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **GLIMPSE: Global-Local Indexing with Multi-Precision Spectral Encoding for Explorable Video World Models**.

GLIMPSE matters because it treats memory addressing as a first-class modeling problem. Cabbageland keeps wanting world models that can leave, return, and still know what was there. The paper's key move is not another memory bank; it is a way to let uncertain geometry enter attention with the right precision instead of pretending every projected address deserves full trust.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**GLIMPSE** matters because it decomposes video-world-model memory into global recurrent context, local pointer memory, and precision-aware positional addressing.

**CDLM design space** matters because it explains self-conditioning as exposure to inference-like states and token identifiability, then offers DoubleCat and SemiCat as cheaper targeted fixes.

**Prediction Is Not a Sufficient Memory State** matters because it separates the current predictor from the evidence needed for future updates.

**Predictability Bias in Medical JEPA** matters because it audits the pretraining computation itself and shows that clinically decisive residual variation can be attenuated by context-prediction objectives.

**VideoAWR** matters because it separates guided behavior sampling from the unguided optimization anchor, making reward post-training less likely to confuse exploration quality with target-field bias.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 12 pattern is bottleneck honesty. Do not let geometry address memory more precisely than it deserves. Do not let diffusion language models train on states unlike the ones they sample from. Do not mistake a present prediction for a sufficient learning state. Do not let predictable anatomy stand in for unpredictable pathology. Do not train a video flow model on the same guided field you used only to get better rollouts. In all five papers, the mechanism is useful because it says what evidence is allowed through, where it is allowed through, and what gets lost if the boundary is lazy.
