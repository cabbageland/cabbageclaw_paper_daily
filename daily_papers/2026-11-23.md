## Ranked papers

### 1. **Beyond Visual Fidelity: Comparing Categorical and Continuous World Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant world-model evaluation, generative modeling, and uncertainty. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.27870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27870), Sarah Parisot, Melanie Fernandez Pradier, David Bignell, Sergio Valcarcel Macua, and Katja Hofmann; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27870).
- **Mechanism:** Cross categorical versus continuous prediction with sequential patch generation versus iterative whole-frame generation. Lossless observation encoding in a small stochastic Pac-Man environment removes tokenizer reconstruction error. Check generated transitions against the simulator's permissible dynamics, propagate hidden-state consistency over rollouts, and separately compare predicted outcome frequencies with exact conditional probabilities. A simple 3D maze adds partial observability and lossy-tokenizer comparisons.
- **Why it matters:** A valid future need not match one sampled reference, and a stream of valid futures can still assign the wrong probabilities. This factorial comparison and separate validity/calibration readouts are more reusable than another visual-quality leaderboard.
- **Evidence:** Greedy categorical next-token prediction retains approximately 86% fully valid 64-step Pac-Man rollouts after 2 PFLOPs of training. At 10 PFLOPs, empirical ghost-distribution TV is 0.30 for sampled categorical next-token prediction, 0.67 for sampled iterative categorical prediction, and 0.33/0.34 for continuous next-token/iterative models. Thus continuous models beat iterative categorical prediction on this calibration metric despite weaker validity. Lossy categorical maze models remain competitive on semantic accuracy and FVD despite worse exact RGB accuracy.
- **Catch:** Two deliberately simple worlds, small transformers, three Pac-Man training seeds and only one maze seed. The 3D evaluation has no exact dynamics-validity oracle. Compute is estimated with 6ND and excludes encoder training; inference costs differ. Thirty-two samples per context impose a finite-sample TV floor. Palette projection and specialized lossless vocabularies are useful controls, not evidence that categorical prediction universally beats large continuous world models. Categorical image tokens are not symbolic physical state.
- **Read scope:** Full main text, model losses, training/evaluation appendix, inference-step analysis, and selected component/ghost-policy results. No code execution or independent reproduction. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/categorical_continuous_world_models_validity.md).

### 2. **EchoAlign: Echocardiography foundation model with multilevel report alignment**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant clinical foundation models, structured supervision, and representation learning. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.27517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27517), Ahmed Aly and colleagues; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27517).
- **Mechanism:** Give each video and study a global embedding plus 12 learned anatomical embeddings. Four CLIP-style losses align videos, structures, and studies with corresponding report text. An expert view-by-structure visibility matrix selects eligible supervision and samples view-appropriate captions. A study transformer pools multiple videos; downstream probes use 832 PCA-compressed features from the 13 study embeddings.
- **Why it matters:** Match supervision to observable evidence instead of attaching every report finding to every clip. The transferable mechanism is structured eligibility for contrastive supervision, not merely adding anatomical names to attention queries.
- **Evidence:** The detailed recipe uses 2,651,674 training videos/stills from 60,782 studies and 150 centers, plus 217,195 validation videos from nine other centers; the headline 2.87M includes validation. Across 68 classification task-source pairs, linear-probe mean AUROC is 0.890 versus EchoPrime's 0.880; mean severity QWK is 0.530 versus 0.505. At 30K identical pretraining studies, the EchoAlign and reproduced EchoPrime recipes reach 0.869 and 0.803 AUROC. EchoAlign is not the best measurement or segmentation model.
- **Catch:** Visibility gates supervision and some pooling, not all internal information flow: video queries attend to all patches, study self-attention mixes all tokens, and inference uses no view labels. This is not guaranteed anatomical localization or shortcut elimination. Retrospective report-derived labels, unequal feature/architecture choices, unadjusted multiple comparisons, small external subsets, and private pretraining data remain material. Aggregate advantages do not establish superiority on every external dataset. The manuscript advertises code/weights, but its linked GitHub repository returned HTTP 404 during this run; release availability was not verified.
- **Read scope:** Main results/discussion; architecture, all four losses, visibility/caption rules, pretraining counts, probe construction, split and statistical protocols; selected extended-data analyses. Not every extended-data cell audited. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/echoalign_visibility_gated_report_alignment.md).

### 3. **A Plan or an Adversarial Example? A Round-Trip Check for Latent World Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant model-based control, planning evaluation, and verification. **Verdict:** Useful; strong follow-up, lighter recommendation today.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.27124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27124), Huiyuan Tian, Chih-Yung Wen, and Shijian Li; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27124).
- **Mechanism:** Execute the current plan after each search iteration to measure how often optimization solves a problem and then loses it; this is an evaluation diagnostic, not free online supervision. For the actual repair, train a small inverse model on existing transition data to recover the sum of each five-action block from consecutive latent states. Penalize disagreement with that identifiable block sum during CEM/MPPI, normalized by candidate-wise cost and residual standard deviations.
- **Why it matters:** Stronger search can exploit a predictor rather than improve control. Check action-to-prediction-to-action consistency, but predict only what the transition can identify; reconstructing an entire aliased action sequence adds an irreducible penalty even to genuine plans.
- **Evidence:** With DINO-WM on Wall, open-loop CEM success rises from 0.39 to 0.76 and the solved-then-lost rate falls from 52% to 10%. With JEPA-WM, MPPI rises from 0.56 to 0.94. LeWM Cube MPC rises from 0.70 to 0.81. Reported extra planning time is 0.2%-3.1%. Six already-low-failure planner settings show no significant gain, an important negative result.
- **Catch:** The largest Wall failure is partly traced to inconsistent released position labels; correcting those labels reaches 0.82-0.85 without the check. Additive block sums are especially suitable for these tasks, not arbitrary torque/contact dynamics. The residual is a ranking aid, not a sound safety certificate, and a sufficiently strong optimizer can also exploit a learned inverse model. No real-robot transfer is established.
- **Read scope:** Main setup, diagnostic, identifiability argument, exact loss/penalty, selected CEM/MPPI/MPC comparisons, negative results and label-correction analysis. Supplementary implementation details not exhaustively audited. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Mind World Models: Joint Learning of Motor EEG Representations and Action-Conditioned World Dynamics**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant neuro representation learning; adjacent inspiration for learned action interfaces. **Verdict:** Useful, with a narrow world-model claim; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.27029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27029), Weikang Qiu, Zheng Huang, Tinglin Huang, Yujun Yan, and Rex Ying; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27029).
- **Mechanism:** Encode past EEG into a 128-dimensional modulation interface for predicting future frozen DINOv2 features. Combine masked EEG reconstruction, EEG/video InfoNCE, and feature L1 prediction. A lateralization-aware transformer splits learned bilateral/lateral features and uses paired mirror passes to impose different symmetry readouts; paired video mirroring supplies cross-modal augmentation.
- **Why it matters:** Teach a neural representation to explain a subsequent sensory change rather than only reconstruct itself. The useful experiment freezes the EEG backbone and separately asks whether a matched movement proxy actually helps the frozen visual predictor.
- **Evidence:** Pretraining uses 63.28 hours from 40 EgoBrain participants. With participant-disjoint downstream splits and three probe seeds, task-averaged balanced accuracy improves 5.9% relatively and regression correlation 16.2% relatively over the strongest family-average baselines. On held-out subjects, feature L1 is 0.4675 versus 0.4753 for action-free pretraining. The matched-versus-shuffled IMU gap is 0.0071 versus 0.0021; a target-frame-limited IMU control gives similar results.
- **Catch:** These percentages are relative, not percentage-point gains. EEG-to-video predictability does not identify neural intention or an executable causal action; movement artifacts and shared behavioral correlates require stronger controls. The main IMU protocol includes a window after the prediction target to absorb clock offsets, although an appendix control removes it. The test is 0.2-second feature prediction, not long-horizon planning or demonstrated BCI/robot deployment. Fixed probe recipes do not exhaust each baseline's tuning potential.
- **Read scope:** Method/losses, pretraining and frozen-probe protocol, selected downstream tables, lateralization/ablation discussion, IMU transfer and its timing control. Not all dataset preprocessing or supplementary code audited. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Preserving Pretrained CFG Structure in RL for Diffusion Models with Conditional Contrast Regularization**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow RL post-training and controllability. **Verdict:** Useful narrower regularizer; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.27288](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27288), Yunfei Zheng and colleagues; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27288).
- **Mechanism:** From the current policy's clean estimate, renoise locally and query the frozen reference conditionally and unconditionally. Normalize their clean-space contrast and regress the current estimate toward a stop-gradient shifted target. Add this directional update to DiffusionNFT-style reward training and KL regularization. Track projection of the learned correction onto the pretrained CFG correction as a diagnostic, not an objective to maximize.
- **Why it matters:** Preserve a useful conditional direction instead of only shrinking total model drift. The local re-noising construction is a concrete baseline for preserving pretrained structure during reward optimization; it is not another instruction to turn CFG up at inference.
- **Evidence:** On Z-Image's GenEval setting, CCR versus default KL gives reward 0.960 versus 0.938, DPG 83.1 versus 79.8, and MUSIQ 74.7 versus 67.6. On Qwen-Image, GenEval falls from 0.947 to 0.933 while MUSIQ rises from 66.0 to 72.3. Four reward settings, reference/KL baselines, local-target ablation, weight sweeps, and a weaker-benefit SD3.5 comparison support a trade-off claim rather than universal dominance.
- **Catch:** Sharpness and learned image-quality scores are proxies, not a blinded human-preference study. Frozen-reference conditional/unconditional queries add training work. Backbone dependence, regularizer sensitivity, and missing uncertainty in headline tables limit confidence. Alignment with a CFG direction is not a complete theory of quality or a solution to reward hacking; too much CCR can hurt both reward and prompt alignment.
- **Read scope:** Main geometric diagnostic/rotation intervention, complete CCR objective, principal reward/backbone tables and target/weight ablations; selected appendix interpretation. No reproduction, full cost audit, or detailed note today.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **World models:** Cross representation with generation procedure, then report validity, calibration, perceptual quality, training compute, and inference cost separately. A low FVD is not a dynamics certificate; greedy validity is not distribution learning.
- **Clinical alignment:** Compare matched data and probe capacity; isolate visibility gating from caption augmentation, anatomical queries, and study pooling. Add external shifted cohorts, calibration and prospective utility before claiming clinical trust.
- **Planning:** Report solved-then-lost rates over optimization iterations and matched open-loop/MPC protocols. Include corrected-data, early-stopping, action-clipping, and per-action inverse-model baselines. Round-trip consistency cannot certify behavior outside the models' shared support.
- **Neuro/action representations:** Match preprocessing/probe tuning, remove post-target conditioning, audit neural versus motion/artifact information, and test closed-loop behavior. A predictive action interface is not yet causal motor control.
- **Diffusion RL:** Compare local contrast, direct guided-target distillation, KL, and inference CFG at matched training cost. Keep independent quality measures and reference-direction counterexamples visible.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Structure is useful when it changes which claims can be tested. Known transition rules distinguish impossible futures from merely different ones; visibility rules stop some report labels from supervising unseen anatomy; identifiable action summaries avoid penalizing ambiguity; EEG-grounded prediction probes information beyond reconstruction; local conditional contrast preserves a pretrained direction during reward tuning. None of these boundaries should disappear in the headline: valid is not calibrated, anatomically named is not localized, self-consistent is not safe, predictive is not causal, and sharper is not necessarily preferred.
