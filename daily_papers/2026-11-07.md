## Ranked papers

### 1. **PlayGen2: Learning Real-Time Playable Worlds with Explicit 3D State**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.07867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07867), November 5, 2026. [Code](https://github.com/MajorDavidZhang/PlayGen-2).
- **Mechanism:** A learned transition model updates an occupancy grid, a known/unknown mask, and identity-indexed entity records. A separate diffusion renderer receives projected depth/semantics plus RGB history. Generated pixels never enter state prediction. Geometry is committed only to unknown voxels, making revisits a memory lookup rather than an act of visual improvisation.
- **Evidence:** Trained on roughly 500 hours from one Doom map; evaluated on that map and seven unseen layouts. At ten simulated minutes, severe-penetration contact error is 5.2–6.8%, and RGB action accuracy is 82.1–85.6%. The explicit state also permits multiplayer composition and programmed interventions without retraining.
- **Catch:** Entities outside the active crop are stored but paused, not globally simulated. Structured engine supervision is privileged; this is not learning a 3D simulator from video alone. The abstract's approximately 160 ms claim should not replace Section 4.4's measured 200.6 ms serial latency / 4.98 FPS; overlapped execution reaches 7.16 FPS but 275.7 ms latency. Local-generation FID/FVD do not establish correct geometry.
- **Read scope:** Main text, state/memory/loss appendix, renderer objective, evaluation details, and public demo README. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/playgen2_explicit_3d_state_playable_worlds.md).

### 2. **FewStepRL: Fine-tuning Few-step Diffusion Models with Reinforcement Learning**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/RL. **Verdict:** Useful; priority candidate for a deeper technical note, not a completed canonical read.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.08240](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08240), November 5, 2026.
- **Mechanism:** Existing RL objectives often require instantaneous velocity, while few-step generators predict average interval velocity. FewStepRL rewrites objectives in prediction space, splits their gradients into reference regression and reward augmentation, then constructs appropriate anchors through MeanFlow identities or auxiliary velocity networks. Seven trainers share rollout/reward/evaluation infrastructure.
- **Evidence:** For SD3.5-Medium/AnyFlow at four steps, the mixed-reward image experiment reports iteration-400 GenEval2 of 0.463 for FewStepOPSD and 0.452 for ReFL versus 0.363 at initialization. But video does not inherit that ordering: DMDNFT has the highest training reward and the lowest VBench overall among the seven tuned models, 83.38.
- **Catch:** Each trainer has a single run. Video reward-gradient methods use 48 GPUs and 1.5 times the rollout count of the 32-GPU alternatives, and only part of the reward is differentiable. More inference steps do not systematically help. Do not turn this into a universal optimizer leaderboard.
- **Read scope:** Introduction, prediction-space gradient decomposition and selected adaptation equations, experimental setup/results, test-time-scaling discussion, and limitations from the 86-page full text; not all derivations or implementation appendix.

### 3. **Scaling Neural Motor Decoding via Decoupled Behavioral Pretraining**

- **Classification:** Direct neuro/representation relevance; adjacent inspiration for modular world models. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.08263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08263), November 5, 2026. [BEEMO code](https://github.com/nerdslab/beemo).
- **Mechanism:** Learn a movement decoder from cheap, unpaired behavioral trajectories; inject expensive neural recordings through optional cross-attention. The same squared-error objective supports behavior-only and paired training. Behavioral-context dropout prevents the decoder from relying on information absent during neural-only evaluation.
- **Evidence:** With about 6.5 additional hours of behavior, BEEMO-POYO improves random-target R² from 0.7498 to 0.7609 and isometric-wrist R² from 0.7237 to 0.7361. The scaling experiment matches full-budget backbone performance using 32 rather than 105 neural sessions. Those are experiment-specific data-efficiency results, not a general threefold law.
- **Catch:** Held-out animals still receive paired target-session fine-tuning. This is neither zero-shot neural decoding nor human closed-loop BCI validation. One matched-backbone wrist gain is not statistically significant; task-matched behavior helps more than indiscriminate extra trajectories.
- **Read scope:** Main methods/results, discussion, architecture/training details, and public code README. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/beemo_decoupled_behavioral_pretraining_neural_decoding.md).

### 4. **Multimodal Hierarchical Mixture-of-Experts for 3D Medical Vision-Language Modeling**

- **Classification:** Adjacent inspiration, medical representation learning. **Verdict:** Useful lighter recommendation; no preserved note today.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.08185](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08185), November 5, 2026; accepted WACV 2027.
- **Mechanism:** MedHiVE uses two-level soft expert routing to connect anatomy-conditioned and global image/report tokens. Segmentation supervision and anatomy/global contrastive losses make the hierarchy do measurable work. Reports and anatomy masks are required during pretraining, not downstream image-only inference.
- **Evidence:** Pretrained on 25,494 Merlin CT scans from 18,321 patients. Table 1 reports segmentation mDSC of 78.7/87.8/66.7/87.7 on PANORAMA/AMOS/KiTS/GEPAR3D. Routing, text placement, and loss ablations are more informative than the all-benchmarks headline.
- **Catch:** The full model is 177.9M parameters and 20.8 million voxels/s, not the efficiency winner. Supervised anatomy and reports are extra information. Top-k routing actually beats soft routing on KiTS and narrowly on AMOS; the average gain is not universal. Qualitative kidney-lesion/report agreement lacks voxel-level ground truth for that cohort.
- **Read scope:** Architecture/routing, training objectives, main tables and ablations, plus selected data/evaluation appendix passages; not the entire supplement.

### 5. **Monotone Viability Learning for Offline Safe Reinforcement Learning**

- **Classification:** Direct RL/control mechanism; adjacent safety-estimation inspiration. **Verdict:** Useful, with a sharp theory-to-deployment qualification; no preserved note today.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.07616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07616), November 5, 2026.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of letting discounted long-horizon safety values attenuate, MoViL recursively learns shrinking finite-horizon viable sets with binary membership targets. Calibrated thresholds and observed safe-trajectory witnesses protect the recursion; action-conditioned classifiers screen a behavior-cloned policy.
- **Evidence:** Dubins-car viable-set IoU is 99.34 ± 0.25%, with a 0.42 ± 0.28% false-positive rate, versus FISOR's 94.48% IoU and zero reported false positives. Across 17 simulated safe-RL tasks, the authors report satisfying the average cost limit on each task.
- **Catch:** The behavior-supported recovery theorem assumes deterministic transitions, adequate behavior support, and uniform probability-estimation error bounds. Those conditions are not certified for neural deployment. More importantly, Algorithm 1's fallback minimizes a learned cost over candidate actions without requiring the selected fallback to pass the viability test. Average-cost compliance is not proof of zero trajectory violations.
- **Read scope:** Formulation, theorem statements/proof arguments, losses, deployment algorithm, numerical validation, main benchmark results, and policy ablation.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**PlayGen2.** It makes the strongest architectural commitment of the batch: appearance is an observation of the modeled world, not the authority that determines its state. The known/unknown mask, write-once geometry, and identity table are concrete mechanisms. The equally important boundary is that persistent storage is not continuous global dynamics, and an explicit state interface does not make learned transitions physically correct.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **World models:** Compare PlayGen2 against both pixel-recurrent predictors and structured-state simulators. Match access to engine state; separately measure revisit geometry, entity identity, collision errors, and off-crop evolution. Do not credit privileged supervision to architecture alone.
- **Diffusion/RL:** FewStepRL makes average-versus-instantaneous velocity a required implementation check. Compare held-out quality, GPU-hours, rollout counts, and reward-gradient availability—not only training reward or iteration count.
- **Neural decoding:** BEEMO makes behavior-only pretraining and simple dynamical smoothing necessary baselines for claims that bigger neural pretraining datasets create better representations. Keep target-session fine-tuning budgets fixed.
- **Medical hierarchy:** MedHiVE supports testing anatomically grounded routing rather than generic expert branding. Separate the effect of masks/reports, routing, contrastive supervision, and compute.
- **Safety:** MoViL separates long-horizon set semantics from discounted score fitting, but its deployment fallback needs its own safety argument.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful decomposition is not merely two modules with new names. PlayGen2 blocks rendered-image errors from rewriting world state; BEEMO lets behavioral structure scale without scaling invasive recordings; FewStepRL forces the optimizer to respect what its network actually predicts. MedHiVE and MoViL add a second lesson: explicit structure earns attention when its training targets and ablations support it, but the operational claim still has to stop at the evidence. A stored entity can be frozen, a higher reward can hide worse video, and a safety classifier can hand control to an uncertified fallback.
