# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-23

## Theme

The strongest papers today all refuse to let a local heuristic impersonate the real object being optimized. **Phantom Gains** says transition-level self-improvement claims need measured nulls, not noisy gain-loss folklore. **Optimal Skill Selection** says skill routing is a budgeted set optimization problem, not top-k relevance. **Harness Continual Learning** says agent adaptation lives in a mutable harness that can forget even when the model is frozen. **Forgetting, plasticity, and co-observation** says retention and plasticity still do not recover joint-training generalization when the data is never seen together. **Levy Attention** says uncertainty should come out of the attention operator itself rather than being bolted on with extra heads or extra passes.

## Short overview

This Sunday, August 23, 2026 run inspected the arXiv HTML full text for all five ranked papers below. Four papers earn preserved notes. **Levy Attention** stays just below the preserve bar because the operator is genuinely elegant and the zero-extra-pass uncertainty story is real, but the current evidence is still anchored to irregular time-series forecasting rather than a broader set of agentic or world-model settings. **Most relevant today:** **Optimal Skill Selection for LLM Agents with Provable Bicriteria Guarantees**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Phantom Gains: Auditing Self-Improvement Against a Measured Null**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the severest paper in the batch because it audits self-improvement claims against a frozen control pushed through the identical pipeline, then shows how easily popular transition statistics hallucinate progress. The headline wound is concrete: a frozen Qwen3-8B run evaluated twice still "expands" **7/25 = 0.280** unreached AIME problems under the common thresholded statistic. Their replacement is much better behaved: a per-problem exact test against pooled baseline replicates under FDR control gives **zero detections on all 11 held-out baseline replicates**. Under the matched ladder, external distillation improves **8-11 of 22** rarely reached AIME problems while three self-training variants improve only **0-2**, and the asymmetry survives regression control at **p < 1e-8**.

### 2. **Optimal Skill Selection for LLM Agents with Provable Bicriteria Guarantees**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the most directly reusable agent paper in the batch because it correctly identifies skill selection as a budgeted set-level problem rather than independent semantic ranking. The contamination-controlled benchmark is pointed: tasks are unsolvable unless the injected skills expose the required private APIs and semantics. The concrete win is large: **BPS** reaches **0.73** measured task success against **0.20-0.52** for released routers, retrievers, and executor self-selection, while using **28% fewer tokens** than the strongest released router. The mechanism story is also sharp: one-capability skill sets get **0%** success, complementary skills hit **93%**, a redundant extra skill spends **225** more tokens for only **+1** point, and a semantically related but irrelevant skill drops success by **23** points.

### 3. **Harness Continual Learning: Continual Adaptation Beyond Model Parameters**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper earns its slot because it reframes continual learning around the mutable agent harness rather than around model weights alone. The useful move is explicit: define the evolving state as **Task Interface**, **Experience Memory**, **Capability Map**, and **Adaptive Router**, then gate every proposed update on current improvement, historical retention, and validity before committing it. The experimental signal is concrete enough to care about. Across textual reasoning, multimodal perception, and open-world interaction, the paper reports gains above **10%** over matched baselines in multiple settings. In the controlled textual-reasoning stream, relaxing the retention budget raises final average performance from **52.20%** to **64.70%** while adding only **0.07** average forgetting.

### 4. **Forgetting, plasticity, and co-observation: a third facet of continual learning**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest continual-learning paper in the set because it attacks a hidden assumption that many CL results quietly inherit: if you solved forgetting and plasticity, you would recover joint-training performance. The paper argues that you still miss the benefit of seeing related samples together. The useful diagnostic move is to use a full-retention ensemble plus linear probing to control for forgetting, then measure the residual gap to joint training as a co-observation effect. The replay result is the sharp part: around **15%** replay storage is enough to match the full-retention ensemble, while **30%** replay pushes past the ensemble and closes roughly half the gap to incremental joint training; distillation matches the ensemble but not the larger replay regime, which is exactly what you would expect if it preserves earlier features without recreating true co-observation.

### 5. **Levy Attention: Single-Pass Predictive Uncertainty for Continuous-Time Attention**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent runner-up.

Why it matters: This is the best narrow-method paper in the batch because it makes uncertainty emerge from the attention operator itself instead of paying for it with extra sampling or auxiliary heads. The mechanism is clean: model cross-attention as a stochastic integral against an inhomogeneous Poisson random measure, keep total compatibility mass as evidence, keep attended-value spread as disagreement, and emit a closed-form uncertainty estimate from the same deterministic pass. The evidence is respectable: the operator swap costs at most **5.6%** accuracy, the free disagreement signal beats **20-pass** MC dropout across matched five-seed suites, the calibrated zero-sample CRPS beats a **50-draw** sampler, and one pass ranks trust over **3,383** unseen patients in **1.4 seconds**. It stays just below preserved-note status because the current validation surface is still a specialized irregular-time-series world.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Optimal Skill Selection for LLM Agents with Provable Bicriteria Guarantees**.

It lands directly on a live cabbageland problem: a frozen executor with too many skills, too little context, and too many naive selectors that score skills one by one as if the selected set were not the real object. The paper's framing is the right corrective. What matters is capability coverage minus context damage under a hard budget, with redundancy and complementarity treated explicitly.

The rest of the batch rhymes with that taste. **Phantom Gains** says progress claims need their own measured null. **Harness Continual Learning** says the mutable harness itself is learned state and can forget. **Co-observation** says even perfect retention is not the same thing as seeing the right things together. **Levy Attention** says trust signals should fall out of structure, not post hoc sampling theater.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Phantom Gains** matters because it makes measured nulls a required control for transition-level self-improvement claims instead of an optional extra.

**Optimal Skill Selection** matters because it reframes skill routing as regularized submodular maximization under a hard token budget and backs that framing with both a theorem and a contamination-controlled benchmark.

**Harness Continual Learning** matters because it shifts continual learning from parameter updates to harness-state updates, then makes retention an explicit commitment gate instead of an afterthought.

**Co-observation** matters because it identifies a third source of continual-learning failure beyond forgetting and plasticity, then shows replay helps partly by restoring simultaneous observation rather than only by preserving memory.

**Levy Attention** matters because it derives uncertainty from the attention computation itself, preserving evidence and disagreement that softmax normally throws away.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 23 lesson is that you should optimize the real state transition, not the prettiest local proxy. A transition statistic without a measured null manufactures self-improvement. Skill routing without set-level budgeting wastes context and degrades execution. Harness adaptation without retention gates quietly breaks earlier behavior. Continual learning without co-observation never recovers the structure joint training got from seeing things together. Attention without surfaced evidence and disagreement cannot say how much a query should be trusted. Same rule all day: if the mechanism does not preserve or optimize the real object, the headline result is partly fake.
