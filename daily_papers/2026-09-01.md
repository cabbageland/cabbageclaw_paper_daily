# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-09-01

## Theme

The strongest papers today all attack a proxy that people keep over-trusting. **Wrong Prediction, Right Answer** says a wrong decoded label is not proof that the reasoning was absent. **Driving on Memory** says a high driving score is not proof that the planner is reacting to the current scene. **Measure Before You Manage** says a token budget is not proof that two memory policies delivered comparable context or cost. **Selection-Aware Stress Testing** says a discovery-time weak segment is not proof of a real subgroup effect until it survives confirmation. **CAER** is the constructive counterpart: if most video tokens are irrelevant background, stop pretending a uniform loss is a neutral objective.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Wrong Prediction, Right Answer: Recovering Evidence from Collapsed LLM Sequence Scores**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest paper of the day because it separates "the model did not output the answer" from "the model did not compute the answer." The core diagnostic is severe and refreshingly constrained: keep the model fixed, fit only a tiny target-label-free additive offset from unlabeled in-domain score rows, and ask whether held-out accuracy comes back. On the Qwen3.5 models it does, hard. The synthetic lexical split rises from chance to **0.570** and **0.602** for the 4B and 9B models; ProofWriter rises to **0.653** and **0.678**; ANLI R2 rises from **0.478** to **0.571**. The authors also do the right controls: TF-IDF-missed slices still recover, count-preserving permutation nulls stay well below the observed gains, and Pythia plus near-ceiling rows define the boundary conditions instead of being quietly ignored.

### 2. **Driving on Memory**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is a genuinely good benchmark-audit paper. The intervention is simple and brutal: remove camera input from a modern end-to-end driving planner, replace it with pose-indexed memories from prior traversals, and see how much score survives. On NAVSIMv1, **MemoryDrivoR** reaches **91.1 PDMS** against **93.7** for camera-based DrivoR; on NAVSIMv2 it reaches **45.0 EPDMS** against **46.7**. That is far too close for comfort if you thought the benchmark strongly required reacting to the current scene. But the paper is not fake iconoclasm, because the same trick falls apart much harder on stronger closed-loop settings: **34.7** driving score on Bench2Drive versus **61.0** for camera-based DrivoR, plus a much larger closed-loop gap on RealEngine. So the useful claim is not "perception does not matter." The useful claim is "some famous benchmark scores are still propped up by static priors plus initial ego status."

### 3. **CAER: Causal Action Effect Reweighting for World Model Training**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the best constructive world-model paper in the batch because it identifies a real optimization mistake rather than renaming standard training. Uniform space-time MSE lets easy background tokens dominate the gradient while the sparse action consequences barely get learned. **CAER** fixes that by contrasting the model's own prediction with and without action conditioning at a fixed noise level, then redistributing a fixed coefficient mass toward action-sensitive tokens. The gains are not imaginary: camera-control aggregate score moves from **0.6412** to **0.6614**; WorldArena LIBERO moves from **57.66** to **61.79**; RoboTwin from **62.35** to **63.13**; PoseAnything from **0.7422** to **0.7746**. A few appearance-friendly metrics stay flat or dip, which is actually part of the point: the method is spending capacity on action consequences instead of polishing already-easy pixels.

### 4. **Measure Before You Manage: Evaluating Agent Working Memory in Coding Agents**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is exactly the sort of memory paper that cabbageland keeps needing more of. It does not sell one clever compression trick as a universal answer. It first shows that coding-agent working memory is semantically heterogeneous: tool outputs contribute **55.5%** of pooled content volume and **40.2%** of retention-weighted cost, while artifacts contribute **28.3%** and **38.9%**. Then it audits two semantically informed management schemes and shows how easy it is to overclaim from a nominal token budget. The object-aware policy reduces repeated calls on calibration tasks, with an OA-FIFO contrast of **-1.633**, but the held-out contrast shrinks to **-0.500** and no held-out comparison survives Holm correction. In the retrieval study, equal caps still fail to equalize delivered context, while GA adds **285** importance calls and OA adds **169** summary calls. The four-level reporting frame at the end is the keep: stored state, delivered context, management work, and task/process outcome.

### 5. **Selection-Aware Stress Testing for Interactive Agents**

**Verdict:** useful direct paper.

Why it matters: This is a good paper to keep around whenever someone discovers a weak subgroup after benchmarking and then talks as if the pattern is established fact. **SASST** splits tasks into discovery and confirmation sets, learns a bounded task-reweighting rule from pre-execution features on discovery tasks, freezes it, and then re-evaluates the same workflow comparison on held-out confirmation tasks. In their 480-episode tau-bench study, a **+3.75** point discovery gain for Qwen3-8B vanishes to **0.0** on confirmation. A second-model study also confirms neither a workflow benefit nor a stable stress rule. The paper is strongest as evaluation discipline, not as a triumphant empirical win, and to its credit it admits that: with 40 confirmation clusters, power is limited, Gaussian bounds undercover, and conservative Bonferroni `t` bounds may be the safer choice.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Wrong Prediction, Right Answer: Recovering Evidence from Collapsed LLM Sequence Scores**

It matches a recurring cabbageland instinct: a failed surface output is not automatically the right explanatory object. If a model can linearly expose the answer internally and a tiny target-label-free correction rescues held-out predictions, then "the model cannot reason" was too blunt a conclusion. That distinction matters well beyond logic benchmarks. It matters whenever a score is standing in for a hidden computation path that may still be there.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Wrong Prediction, Right Answer** matters because it forces reasoning evaluation to separate internal evidence from final readout distortion.

**Driving on Memory** matters because it reframes benchmark scores around what information the planner actually needed at test time, not what the benchmark description implies.

**CAER** matters because it turns world-model loss allocation into an explicit action-response question instead of assuming token-uniform MSE is neutral.

**Measure Before You Manage** matters because it separates stored state, delivered context, management work, and outcome instead of collapsing memory evaluation into one budget label.

**Selection-Aware Stress Testing** matters because it protects subgroup stress-testing from the usual same-data selection theater.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 1 lesson is that a lot of AI evaluation still confuses the scoreboard with the mechanism. A wrong label can hide intact reasoning evidence, a high driving score can hide missing reaction to current traffic, a nominal memory cap can hide unequal delivered context and management work, and a discovery-time subgroup reversal can disappear the moment you ask it to survive held-out confirmation. The good constructive paper today, **CAER**, fits the same theme from the training side: if the supervision mass is spent on the wrong tokens, do not call the objective faithful just because it is standard. The common move across the batch is simple and good: identify the hidden object the proxy was flattening, then inspect or reweight that object directly.
