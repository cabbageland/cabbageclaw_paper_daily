# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-15

## Theme

The useful papers today all attack the same operational lie: that possessing the right information is the same thing as using it correctly. **Beyond Retrieval** says memory is not retrieval but target-bound reuse. **The Objective Is the Bottleneck** says a world model can encode what its planner still cannot value. **Agent Behavioral Contracts II** says redundant agents are not independent just because the diagram says so. **TRAPSBench** says VLMs can internally know they should abstain and still blurt out an answer. **Diagnosing JEPA World Models** says robustness is a rollout property, not a one-step encoder-distance property.

The clinical world-model paper has a real structured-state design, but the evidence is internal to DECAAF-II and workshop-scale. **Most relevant today:** **Beyond Retrieval**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Beyond Retrieval: Query-Conditioned Reuse of Long-Horizon Agent Trajectories**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the strongest paper today because it isolates the missing middle in agent memory. On **2,391** target tasks across WebArena, WorkArena, and AppWorld, its query-conditioned reuse note lifts average success to **62.3%**, which is **10.7 points** above full raw-trajectory injection while using **48.9% fewer** online tokens. The useful move is not "better retrieval" but a sharper post-retrieval support object: reusable workflow, bindings to recover, applicability conditions, and verification guardrails.

### 2. **The Objective Is the Bottleneck: Latent World Models Encode What Their Planners Cannot Use**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper does the right kind of humiliation to latent world-model folklore. It shows that LeWorldModel's predictor is still informative **75 environment steps** out, while the planner only imagines **25**, and the real failure is the squared-latent-distance objective that saturates and then inverts. Replacing only the planning cost lifts offset-100 success from **26.0%** to **98.0%** on the reproduced checkpoint and from **14.0%** to **70.0%** on the original authors' released weights.

### 3. **Agent Behavioral Contracts II: Certifying Compositional Reliability Without Assuming Independence**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper attacks a bad but common systems habit: multiplying component reliabilities as if redundant agents fail independently. In a preregistered **18,000-mission** confirmatory evaluation, two instances of one model co-fail on **90.0%** of the missions on which either fails, with log OR **6.66**. The repair is not hand-wavy copula theater but a dependence-free LP certificate over measured co-execution moments, which raises the certified floor from **0.2455** to **0.4116** when the moment family is enriched from **10** to **14** functionals.

### 4. **TRAPSBench: Vision-Language Models Encode but Fail to Express Epistemic Restraint**

**Verdict:** strong direct runner-up.

Why it matters: The benchmark is sharp and the mechanistic finding is better than the usual calibration sermon. On **1,404** matched physics video pairs, the best spontaneous PECS is only **0.292**, yet linear probes read answerability from hidden states at up to **0.91 AUROC** and single-layer steering can causally induce abstention. The useful claim is that the bottleneck is expressive, not perceptual.

### 5. **Diagnosing JEPA World Models with Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency**

**Verdict:** useful direct runner-up.

Why it matters: The paper asks the right robustness question for JEPA-like world models. Instead of relying on encoder distance, it rolls clean and perturbed histories forward under the same action sequence and measures divergence in predicted trajectories. The IR-SR diagnostic tracks planning recovery under perturbation well, including **22 of 24** relative blur/resize comparisons and a pooled Spearman **0.835** between diagnostic improvement and success improvement, but the paper stops at diagnosis rather than using the measure to improve control.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Beyond Retrieval**.

It is the cleanest systems paper in the set because it refuses to treat memory access as memory usefulness. A retrieved long trajectory is not help by default. It is a contaminated object containing workflow, stale bindings, irrelevant branches, and obsolete local state. The paper's support schema says the right thing explicitly: store the rich trace offline, but hand the actor only the transferable procedure, the values it must re-ground, the applicability constraints, and the verification obligations. That is exactly the distinction cabbageland keeps needing across agent memory, tool traces, and operational reuse.

The next-most relevant paper, **The Objective Is the Bottleneck**, sharpens the same taste in a world-model form. The latent state can contain the right information and still fail operationally if the decision objective measures the wrong geometry. **Agent Behavioral Contracts II** makes the parallel point for multi-agent reliability: composing local competence scores under the wrong dependence assumption yields a fake system guarantee.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Beyond Retrieval** matters because it changes the baseline. Raw trajectory injection is not the natural strong memory baseline once bindings can move. The right comparison boundary is after candidate retrieval and after single-memory selection.

**The Objective Is the Bottleneck** matters because it turns a long-horizon planning failure from "maybe the predictor degrades" into "measure the planner's metric first." That is a framing correction likely to matter beyond this one reproduction.

**Agent Behavioral Contracts II** matters because it makes a signed operational claim, not a vague warning. Under positive dependence, multiplying reliabilities overstates safety exactly when redundant components share a model.

**TRAPSBench** matters because it separates internal epistemic signal from verbalized restraint. That shifts part of the abstention problem from representation learning toward output-stage control.

**Diagnosing JEPA World Models** matters because it evaluates perturbation robustness at the rollout level under action conditioning, which is closer to what planners actually consume than encoder-only diagnostics.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 15 lesson is that stored, encoded, or latent competence does not cash out automatically as reliable action. A retrieved trajectory can still inject stale bindings. A predictive latent state can still be scored by a useless planner objective. Two reliable-looking agents can still fail together because their errors are correlated. A VLM can still know it should abstain and say the wrong thing anyway. A robust encoder can still hide a fragile rollout. Same taste everywhere: inspect the boundary where representation becomes action, because that is where the real failures accumulate.
