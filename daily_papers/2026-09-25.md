## Ranked papers

### 1. **Beyond Compression: Training Latent Representations for Stable Long-Horizon Rollout in Neural Surrogate Solvers**

**Verdict:** must read direct latent-dynamics paper.

Why it matters: This paper makes the right complaint about latent surrogate solvers: compression is not the task. A latent autoencoder trained only for reconstruction can look good at one-step metrics while being badly shaped for long autoregressive rollout. The paper tests training-level interventions rather than another architecture brand: Koopman-inspired autoencoder training, Hamming latent-noise injection, dynamics-side noise injection, and multi-step rollout fine-tuning. The core result is memorable because the metrics disagree: interventions that improve long-horizon stability often hurt reconstruction or one-step prediction. The cumulative recipe reduces long-rollout error by roughly 40%, uses about two orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs and half the GPU memory than full-resolution models in the reported comparison, and stays stable on high-cycle fatigue rollouts far beyond the observed training horizon.

### 2. **AD-WM: Action-Discriminative World Models for Counterfactual Model Predictive Control**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct world-model/control paper.

Why it matters: This is a robotics paper, but it clears the higher bar because the mechanism is not generic VLA branding. The paper isolates a specific mismatch: factual transition prediction does not guarantee that a planner can distinguish alternative candidate actions from the same state. AD-WM adds predictor-level action recovery through inverse dynamics and a normalized recovery objective motivated by conditional mutual information, then discards the auxiliary heads at test time. On OGBench-Cube, hard-start success rises from 3.7% for matched LeWM to 52.0%, and zero-shot Franka pick-and-place improves from 42.2% to 71.1%. The useful lesson is that a world model for control must preserve counterfactual action differences, not merely predict the next observed state.

### 3. **Not All Confusion Is Equal: A Source-Aware Uncertainty Diagnosis for Fine-Grained Aircraft Detection**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct uncertainty/diagnostics paper.

Why it matters: This is a good antidote to scalar uncertainty mush. The paper decomposes detector confusion into a 2x2 grid: aleatoric vs epistemic crossed with within-class vs between-class. That yields four different causes and four different remedies: affinity, heterogeneity, contested boundaries, and collapsed classes. The important detail is that the two epistemic sources are separated by construction: contested boundaries live in output-space mutual information, while collapsed classes live in a bias-parameter posterior and can be missed by disagreement. The intervention evidence is narrow but clean enough to keep: targeted ARJ21 oversampling changes the collapsed source specifically, while the A220-A350 boundary treatment supports the contested diagnosis.

### 4. **PEEL: Physics-Enabled Evidential Learning for Identifiable Uncertainty in CT Imaging**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct medical uncertainty paper.

Why it matters: PEEL is small and synthetic, but the mechanism is unusually crisp. Normal-inverse-gamma regression is not identifiable from the marginal Student-t likelihood; the likelihood leaves a one-dimensional fiber of equivalent NIG parameters. Instead of hiding that under a KL regularizer, the paper uses physical measurement to identify the missing coordinate. A CT reconstruction network first learns the identifiable Student-t coordinates from one noisy FBP image, then a frozen-feature aleatoric head learns a Monte Carlo teacher from repeated physical-noise realizations, after which beta and nu are recovered algebraically. On 30 held-out simulated objects across five photon levels, one-image aleatoric predictions reach pooled Spearman correlations of 0.832-0.951 against 400-repeat references, with 98.81-99.55% admissible pixels.

### 5. **Learning a Flow to Self-Supervised Representations**

**Verdict:** useful direct flow/representation paper.

Why it matters: This paper uses flow matching as a representation-shaping tool rather than a sample generator. Flow-Based Distribution Matching replaces adversarial encoder-critic distribution matching with spherical conditional velocity regression toward an ETF-inspired reference geometry. Two augmented views are assigned to the same target, reference-center capacity is limited, and an explicit alignment loss keeps the two views together. It is not a new ImageNet champion, but the mechanism is clean: explicit target geometry plus non-adversarial velocity learning. The reported speedup over critic-based DM is 1.48x-1.83x under matched training-cost comparisons, and the paper gives a conditional population bound connecting FBDM loss to nearest-centroid classification error.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Beyond Compression** matters because it reframes latent scientific surrogates around rollout-suitable representation geometry rather than compression alone.

**AD-WM** matters because it separates factual prediction accuracy from counterfactual action discrimination and evaluates the planner-facing elite set rather than only global prediction metrics.

**A2E2 source-aware uncertainty** matters because it turns confusion from a passive matrix into a diagnosis with different remedy classes and explicitly separates two forms of epistemic uncertainty.

**PEEL** matters because it treats evidential uncertainty identifiability as a missing-measurement problem instead of a regularization taste question.

**FBDM** matters because it turns flow matching into non-adversarial geometric representation alignment, with a reference geometry and a downstream-error story.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 25 pattern is task alignment inside the representation. A compressed latent has to be trained for long rollout, not only reconstruction. A world model has to preserve action differences that a planner will compare, not merely factual next-state accuracy. A confusion score has to name which source created the error before it can say what remedy makes sense. An evidential model has to identify the uncertainty parameters it claims to report. A self-supervised encoder can be shaped by explicit transport geometry instead of an adversarial critic. In each case, the representation is only as good as the downstream contract it was trained to satisfy.
