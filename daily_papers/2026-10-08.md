## Ranked papers

### 1. **RoboJEPA: Scaling Robotic Latent World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model scaling paper.

Why it matters: This paper is valuable because it turns "scale the robot world model" into a measurable scaling-law claim instead of a slogan. RoboJEPA is an action-conditioned JEPA predictor trained on 23 public manipulation datasets spanning 12 embodiments, 26 action spaces, 15,022 video hours, and 6,692 action-synchronized hours. The predictor scales from 22M to 8B parameters on top of a frozen V-JEPA 2.1 encoder, with teacher-forcing and autoregressive L1 losses in latent space. The key result is that nine-step latent imagination error follows a second-order power law in training compute on both DROID and RoboCasa holdouts, and that this offline error tracks downstream planning: object-interaction RoboCasa tasks only begin to work around 1e22 FLOPs, and real Franka success improves with model size.

### 2. **Tetris3D: 3D Scene Generation With Objects That Fit Together**

**Verdict:** must read direct compositional 3D generation paper.

Why it matters: Tetris3D makes inter-object contact a generation condition instead of a post-hoc pose cleanup problem. It reconstructs scenes object by object in a VLM-inferred physical dependency order, conditioning each object on neighboring surface distance, relative vectors, normals, and relation embeddings for stack, lean, contain, touch, or none. Its ComOb dataset supplies 1.2M physics-simulated scenes with per-object meshes and pairwise physical-relation labels. On Toys4K, Tetris3D cuts penetration depth from the best baseline's 0.1177 to 0.0036 and improves scene Chamfer from 5.72 to 3.68; it also generalizes to MessyKitchens and Picasso with much lower physical instability.

### 3. **Sparse Planning in Visual World Models via Cost Gradients**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct world-model planning paper.

Why it matters: COSTGRAD is a clean reminder that prediction relevance and planning relevance are not the same thing. Given a token-based visual world model, it ranks spatial tokens by the gradient norm of the goal-conditioned planning cost with respect to each input token, then runs CEM on only the selected subset. At 50% tokens, it matches or beats full-token planning on three of four continuous-control benchmarks and gives a measured 2.6x per-step speedup; pairing sparse tokens with fewer CEM iterations reaches about 5.2x total speedup while still exceeding the full-token Wall baseline. The caveat is the interesting part: the method works with AdaLN action conditioning but loses its edge under matched concat conditioning because token removal changes the action pathway.

### 4. **ORCA: Hunting Compositional Failures in Text-to-Image Diffusion**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion / compositional generation paper.

Why it matters: ORCA attacks diffusion binding failures as a misalignment problem, not a missing-text-encoder problem. Modern T2I systems already include T5 because CLIP loses compositional structure, but the denoising objective does not force T5 structure to ground in visual composition. ORCA adds a training-only auxiliary loss that aligns an intermediate diffusion latent to a low-rank PCA target from frozen DINOv2 features, with the readout subspace parameterized by the residual between T5 and CLIP text embeddings. On DiT-L/2, it reaches FID 16.65 and GenEval 0.291 at 200K steps, beating REG at the same budget and REPA/vanilla baselines at 400K, with gains concentrated on attribute binding, spatial relations, counting, and multi-object prompts.

### 5. **SpatialUQ: Post-Hoc Uncertainty Quantification from Spatial Consistency in Black-Box Vision Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent uncertainty / medical-vision paper.

Why it matters: SpatialUQ is a practical black-box UQ trick with a real deployment story. It runs a frozen classifier on the full image and five deterministic crops, then uses the Jensen-Shannon divergence between global and mean local probabilities as a Multicrop Uncertainty Score. On NIH ChestX-ray14 with DenseNet-121, MUS reaches 0.784 failure-detection AUC with six deterministic passes, beating MC-Dropout's 0.664 at one-fifth the compute and offering much better native calibration than raw L1 distance. The paper is unusually honest about regime dependence: the signal works best for diffuse findings and overconfident multi-label classifiers, weakens for small focal lesions, and collapses under severe VinBigData shift where rho drops to 0.027.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**RoboJEPA** matters because it connects action-conditioned latent world-model scaling laws to planning success instead of stopping at prettier rollouts.

**Tetris3D** matters because it pushes local physical relation structure into object generation and tests it with physical stability metrics.

**COSTGRAD** matters because it selects world-model tokens by downstream control gradients, not prediction loss or attention.

**ORCA** matters because it makes compositional diffusion alignment a low-rank visual-grounding objective with no inference overhead.

**SpatialUQ** matters because it turns spatial consistency into a black-box uncertainty score and says plainly where that score breaks.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 8 pattern is operational structure. Scale is useful when offline imagination error predicts planning. 3D generation is useful when object contacts are conditioned and simulated. Sparse planning is useful when token selection follows the control objective and the architecture can tolerate removal. Diffusion alignment is useful when compositional text structure is grounded in a visual subspace. Uncertainty is useful when the score names a measurable consistency relation and carries a deployment diagnostic. The shared rule is simple: do not merely claim structure; put it where it changes search, generation, evaluation, or triage.
