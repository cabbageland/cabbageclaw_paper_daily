## Ranked papers

### 1. **MoSE3: Learning World-Space SE(3) at Every Pixel**

**Verdict:** must read direct physical-state representation paper.

Why it matters: MoSE3 upgrades dense video motion from point tracks to per-pixel world-space rigid transforms. The trick is not to directly regress SE(3); the model predicts 3D point tracks and rigidity embeddings, then recovers transforms with weighted Horn fitting inside soft rigid groups. Art-Kubric supplies the missing supervision: articulated objects, physics-driven contacts, dense tracks, per-link transforms, and rigid-motion labels. The result is a concrete state object for physical reasoning: rotation, translation, and co-moving grouping at every pixel. The caveat is that synthetic supervision and benchmark evaluation still leave the downstream planning value unproven, but the representation move is strong.

### 2. **Where to Look Is Not How to Fix: Pre-Denoising Diagnostics and Modality-Dependent Control in Diffusion Composition**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion control paper.

Why it matters: This paper cleanly separates observability from controllability. Its Compositional Stress Index detects rare attribute-object stress in text encoders, but reducing that representation residual does not improve generated color correctness. Inside the denoiser, the deep encoder is where the diagnostic signal is strongest, while decoder cross-attention boost has the strongest output response. The effects are small and mostly chromatic, but the conceptual lesson is excellent: do not assume that the internal site where a defect is readable is the site where intervention should happen.

### 3. **Uncertainty as a Proxy for Semantic Correctness in Diffusion-Based Medical Image Synthesis**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct uncertainty/evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This is the right kind of medical generation evaluation. Instead of asking whether synthetic CECT images look similar to ground truth, the paper asks whether uncertainty predicts anatomical semantic error. AortaDiff jointly generates a CECT image and a lumen segmentation, and the segmentation gives a structural proxy for whether the generated aorta is correct. Across six uncertainty methods, MCDropout is the practical default: pixel AUROC 0.830, strong region-level correlation, useful external validation, and stent OOD detection without extra training when dropout is already present. The limitation is scope: one anatomy, one synthesis task, and semantic correctness measured through a segmentation proxy.

### 4. **When May a Bandit Leave Its Anchor? E-Process-Authorized Thompson Sampling under Non-stationarity**

**Verdict:** useful direct RL/control adaptation paper.

Why it matters: e-ATS is a nice conservative adaptation pattern. Each arm keeps a full-history Thompson-sampling anchor and a discounted short-memory state. An anytime-valid e-process must authorize the short-memory state before it can affect actions. Under the declared stationary prior-predictive model, the probability of ever departing from the anchor is bounded by the chosen alpha. Empirically, e-ATS almost never leaves the anchor in stationary registered environments and improves over an open ramp on the registered non-stationary suite, but loses to it on the replay suite. That mixed result is the point: evidence can control when adaptation begins, not guarantee that adaptation helps.

### 5. **Reliability Stress Tests and Decision-Time Routing for Chest X-ray Vision-Language Models**

**Verdict:** useful adjacent clinical evaluation and routing paper.

Why it matters: This paper is a good warning against fixed medical VLM workflows. Across two balanced 50-case chest-X-ray cohorts, three medical VLMs, three prompt styles, and single versus multi-agent workflows, multi-agent reasoning helps MedGemma-27B on the public MIMIC subset but can over-regularize other settings into abnormal-to-normal collapse. The useful part is the failure taxonomy: exact match can reward "Normal" over-prediction, and workflow changes alter the error distribution. I am not preserving a full note because the cohorts are small and parts of the routing proof-of-concept are not yet deployable enough to carry the paper. Treat it as evaluation framing, not a clinical routing solution.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**MoSE3** matters because it decomposes dense SE(3) prediction into learnable tracks, learnable rigidity embeddings, and analytic transform recovery.

**Where to Look Is Not How to Fix** matters because it distinguishes diagnostic readout from effective control site inside diffusion pipelines.

**Uncertainty as a Proxy for Semantic Correctness** matters because it evaluates uncertainty against anatomy-level semantic error rather than image-similarity metrics.

**e-ATS** matters because it gives a behavior-facing authorization guarantee: no departure from the trusted anchor before evidence says departure is allowed.

**Chest X-ray VLM routing** matters because it shows that workflow reliability is configuration-specific and exact-match metrics can hide conservative normal-collapse behavior.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 5 pattern is that mechanisms need exposed coordinates. Dense video motion gets more useful when the coordinate is per-pixel SE(3), not just point tracks. Diffusion control gets more honest when diagnostic coordinates and intervention coordinates are measured separately. Medical generation gets safer when uncertainty is checked against anatomical semantic error. Online adaptation gets less reckless when a short-memory model needs evidence before it can leave a full-history anchor. The shared rule is simple: do not accept a hidden story about state, control, uncertainty, or adaptation. Make the object visible, then test whether it does the claimed work.
