## Ranked papers

### 1. **Learn the Solid, Not the File: Canonical Inputs for Neural Networks on CAD Boundary Representations**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest state-carrier paper in the batch. The paper shows that common B-rep neural encoders are brittle to valid redescriptions of the same CAD solid: face splitting, rotations, NURBS re-expression, import/export changes, and human modeling variation can collapse performance even though the underlying object is unchanged. The proposed canonical region graph merges file-fragmented faces back into maximal connected surface regions, expresses features in a canonical frame derived from the solid, and feeds a graph transformer with region and boundary features. On MFInstSeg, MFCAD++, and CADSynth, the method stays near 0.99 macro mIoU under perturbations where AAGNet, UV-Net, and BRepNet often collapse; on 3,000 Fusion 360 retrieval queries it is the only method that remains broadly stable across perturbation types. The useful lesson is blunt: if the task is about the solid, the network should not read the accident of how the file was written.

### 2. **Thinking with Looped Flows**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: Looped Flows is a good recurrent-reasoning mechanism, not just another "more test-time compute" slogan. Standard looped models update a hidden state repeatedly, but training often cuts gradients through time, so early states are not forced to support later computation. This paper trains the recurrence through a sequence of local denoising objectives with decreasing noise and shared noise-target pairs, then performs inference by integrating a probability-flow velocity coupled to the recurrent state. On Sudoku-Extreme, Maze-Hard, ARC-AGI-1, and ARC-AGI-2, it improves over prior looped models, including 58.8% on ARC-AGI-1 and 12.2% on ARC-AGI-2. The best diagnostic is the failure analysis: it recovers 90.9% of TRM's Sudoku failures, including non-convergence and spurious-attractor cases. The caveat is that the benchmarks are compact reasoning tasks, not open-ended world modeling, but the mechanism is transferable.

### 3. **General Quantification of Covariate and Concept Shifts**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This ICML 2026 paper is useful because it makes distribution shift measurable instead of treating it as a vague postmortem label. It argues that existing concept-shift definitions break under source/target support mismatch, then defines a gamma*-concept shift using an entropic optimal-transport coupling over covariates. The resulting bound unifies covariate shift and concept shift across stochastic labeling, broad label spaces, and general Lipschitz losses; the paper also gives finite-sample estimators with concentration guarantees and wraps them in the DataShifts algorithm. Experiments on Novozymes enzyme stability, ColoredMNIST, PACS, and synthetic logistic tasks show the estimated bounds tracking or tightening target-error behavior. The caveat is that practical usefulness still depends on representations, Lipschitz constants, and estimator quality, but the framing is valuable: measure which part of shift is input movement and which part is conditional-law movement.

### 4. **LangStreet: Persistent Language Fields for Anchor-Decoded Street Gaussians**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent paper.

Why it matters: LangStreet solves a real representation mismatch in street-scale Gaussian scenes. Anchor-decoded reconstructions store persistent anchors and decoder slots, but render transient child Gaussians whose geometry and activity change by view; a child can route an observation, but it cannot own persistent semantics. LangStreet assigns semantic ownership to slots and anchors, uses renderer alpha-compositing responsibilities to accumulate additive language evidence, completes weak slots with anchor-aligned evidence, and stores slot detail as low-rank anchor-relative residuals. On KITTI-360, LangStreet base reaches 34.19 2D mIoU with a 2.72 GiB semantic payload, essentially matching max at 34.20 mIoU with 12.90 GiB; the same rate-distortion pattern holds on vKITTI2 and Waymo. The limitation is that all geometry and 2D language extraction stay frozen, so this is representation plumbing rather than a complete semantic mapping solution. Still, the ownership idea is exactly right.

### 5. **ActMap: Single-Pass Uncertainty Quantification from Generation-Time Activation Maps**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent paper.

Why it matters: ActMap is a practical single-generation uncertainty primitive. Instead of sampling multiple answers or probing one final hidden state, it records every layer and generated token during decoding, compresses the trajectory into a fixed 12 x 32 x 128 activation map of temporal statistics, and trains a small classifier to predict answer correctness. The artifact is only 96 KiB per generation and can be scored in under a millisecond. On TriviaQA, NQ-Open, GSM8K, and CNN/DailyMail across Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Mistral-7B, ActMap beats sampling, token-probability, attention, and embedding baselines on all twelve pairs and nearly matches a dense ACT-ViT tensor that is 67x larger. The important caveat is refreshingly severe: detectors do not transfer cleanly across tasks, generators, or model scales, so ActMap is strong for calibrated in-domain deployment and dangerous if treated as a universal truth probe.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Learn the Solid, Not the File: Canonical Inputs for Neural Networks on CAD Boundary Representations**

The transferable lesson is that equivalence classes matter. If many files denote the same object, the model should read a canonical object-level representation rather than memorize arbitrary file cuts. This is directly relevant to world models, generative CAD, 3D reconstruction, structured representations, and any system where the observation format is not the thing the model is supposed to reason about.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Learn the Solid** matters because it reframes B-rep learning around invariance to equivalent solid descriptions, not accuracy on one stored partition.

**Looped Flows** matters because it trains recurrent reasoning state through aligned local denoising objectives and gets inference-time scaling without full BPTT.

**General Quantification of Shifts** matters because it separates covariate movement from conditional-law movement with estimable optimal-transport-based quantities.

**LangStreet** matters because it distinguishes transient render-routing children from persistent semantic owners in anchor-decoded 3D scenes.

**ActMap** matters because it compresses a full generation trajectory into a small uncertainty artifact while admitting that transfer fails badly.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 13 lesson is that systems fail when they read the wrong carrier. CAD encoders read file partitions when they should read solids. Looped recurrent models lose future-useful state when training only rewards immediately useful updates. Distribution-shift analysis muddles covariates and concepts unless the coupling is explicit. Street-scale language fields attach semantics to transient children unless ownership is assigned to persistent slots and anchors. LLM uncertainty probes collapse a whole generation into one token unless the activation trajectory is kept. Different domains, same standard: preserve the variable that the claim actually depends on.
