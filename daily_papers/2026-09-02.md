# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-09-02

## Theme

The strongest papers today all refuse to let an opaque interface masquerade as the real object. **MemoryWalker** says agent training under context compression is not a sequence problem once the harness edits history; it is a conditioning-tree problem. **Cheap Verifiers, Large Blind Spots** says a cascade cannot truthfully grade itself through the same cheap verifier that filters the traffic. **Retrieved but not ranked** says embedding retrieval can look semantically competent while still ranking by lexical bait. **IntroConformal** says factuality control does not need an external verifier if the model already exposes useful internal stability signals. **RadMatch** says radiology evaluation becomes much more useful once the score decomposes into persisted findings and actionable errors.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **MemoryWalker: Stop Training Agents on Contexts They Never Saw**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest paper in the batch for cabbageland because it identifies a real training bug that agent-memory papers usually wave away. If the harness rewrites context during rollout, then replay-time training is no longer conditioning on one sequence. It is conditioning on a tree of pre- and post-eviction views. The paper then does the useful engineering work instead of stopping at diagnosis: **LogitTree** and the packed **4D mask** restore the exact conditioning invariant, while **SDCC** gives a one-backward-pass relaxation that still works for black-box harnesses. The evidence is concrete. On the low-eviction MemexRL anchor, LogitTree and 4D stay at the no-compression drift floor (**0.0133** and **0.0140** versus **0.0135** control). On the eviction-heavy AgentFold cell, 4D keeps drift around **0.022** while naive compressed replay blows up to **0.366**. On black-box harnesses, SDCC reaches **37.5** average EM on Claude Code and **36.9** on OpenCode, both above LogitTree in those cells while keeping low observed drift.

### 2. **Cheap Verifiers, Large Blind Spots: Measuring the Reliability Cost of Cost-Saving Cascades**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper isolates a nasty reliability failure that people will otherwise keep calling "cost optimization." The core object is the verifier blind spot: the fraction of student errors that the cheap verifier falsely accepts. That blind spot gets worse as the student improves. With a fixed `gpt-4o-mini` verifier on GSM8K, the blind-spot rate rises from **0.12** for a 0.5B student to **0.55** for a 14B student. Strong verifiers suppress the blind spot, but they also erase the savings: on hard MATH, `gpt-4.1` drives the blind spot down to about **0.05-0.09** while escalating **46%** of queries against a true error rate of **39%**. The ugliest result is the dashboard failure. In the corrective loop, verifier-estimated error sits near **3%** while true user-facing error ranges from **14%** to **32%**. That is not a small calibration miss. It is a system that cannot see its own degradation.

### 3. **Retrieved but not ranked: surface-form bias in structural retrieval, from mathematics to agent trajectories**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is one of the better retrieval-audit papers in a while because it refuses to use easy topical relevance as a fake semantic benchmark. The setup deliberately separates wording from structure in two unrelated domains: **500** competition-math queries against a **117,088** item corpus, and **118** ALFWorld-derived trajectory queries against **336** trajectories. In math, the failure is complete: strict Hit@1 at the hardest disguise tier is **0.0%** for both production embedders even though the gold item is almost always somewhere in the top 10. In **95.2% to 99.8%** of misses, the rank-1 item is simply more lexically similar than the gold. The trajectory side lands near or below chance once gold must differ in object and receptacle. The nicest extra result is the lexical-control sign flip: a lexical reranker hurts mathematics and helps trajectories, which makes it a cheap diagnostic of whether a benchmark's surface variation is adversarial or merely incidental.

### 4. **IntroConformal: Conformal Factuality Guarantees for Large Vision-Language Models via Introspective Signals**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This is a good uncertainty paper because it does not pretend that a single next-token confidence score is enough, and it does not default to an external verifier either. The method uses conformal risk control with two introspective scores drawn from the model itself: layer-wise semantic stability and a stronger verification probability score from a same-model binary factuality prompt. The results are respectable rather than magical, which is exactly why they matter. On MSCOCO with LLaVA-1.5, the stronger score cuts abstention from **57%** for CONFLVLM and **64%** for semantic stability to **25%**, while improving F1 from **0.504** to **0.581**. In the cross-method comparison, IntroConformal reaches **97.4%** claim-filtering efficiency and **91%** response accuracy, slightly above CONFLVLM's **95.3%** and **90%**, without external models or decoding-time perturbations.

### 5. **RadMatch: Auditable Radiology Report Evaluation via Finding-Level Matching**

**Verdict:** useful adjacent paper.

Why it matters: This is the strongest medical-side paper I saw today because it turns report evaluation from a mysterious scalar into a persisted clinical record. RadMatch decomposes candidate-vs-reference comparison into extracted findings, finding-level matching, and significance-aware scoring across seven attribute dimensions. The reported score is the **actionable-error count**, which is the right choice if the goal is to inspect failures rather than admire correlation. On ReXVal with Opus 4.8 it reaches `|tau_b| = 0.79`, matching inter-radiologist agreement, and on the harder RadEvalExpert it reaches `|tau_b| = 0.58`, more than doubling the best prior metric at **0.24**. The paper is honest about the tradeoffs too: the benchmark scope is still chest X-ray only, and the multi-call pipeline costs about **10.7 s** per pair serially or about **1.8 s** with eight workers.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **MemoryWalker: Stop Training Agents on Contexts They Never Saw**

It hits a recurring cabbageland pain point almost exactly. When a long-horizon agent compresses its own history, replay-time training can quietly become a lie about what the model actually knew at each step. MemoryWalker turns that vague discomfort into a precise conditioning invariant, then gives both exact and practical fixes.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**MemoryWalker** matters because it reframes memory-compressed agent training as a tree-consistency problem instead of a bookkeeping annoyance.

**Cheap Verifiers, Large Blind Spots** matters because it shows that cascade dashboards can improve while shipped error gets worse, so verifier-local metrics are not enough to certify reliability.

**Retrieved but not ranked** matters because it makes "semantic retrieval" answerable under structural disguise instead of letting lexical overlap do the work unpunished.

**IntroConformal** matters because it turns internal stability signals into finite-sample factuality control, rather than treating introspection as merely diagnostic.

**RadMatch** matters because it shifts report evaluation away from opaque correlation and toward actionable, auditable error records.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 2 lesson is that the hidden object is still where most of the real signal lives. If an agent harness rewrites history, the training object is a conditioning tree. If a cascade filters by a cheap verifier, the operational risk is the verifier's blind spot, not the dashboard score. If a retrieval benchmark never separates wording from structure, lexical bait will keep winning. If a factuality controller ignores the model's own internal consistency, it is probably throwing away useful supervision. And if a clinical evaluation metric collapses everything to one number, it forfeits the right to tell you what actually went wrong. The good papers today all make the same move: stop flattering the proxy, surface the hidden object, and make it inspectable.
