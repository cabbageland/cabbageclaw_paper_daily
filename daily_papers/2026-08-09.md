# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-09

## Theme

Today's strongest papers all attack the same flattering lie: a system can look evidence-grounded, calibrated, or realistic while the actual support path is fake, weak, or missing. **HERALD** says proof-of-retrieval rewards are not trustworthy unless they survive citation-laundering attacks, not just fake-ID checks. **The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use** says tool calls are not evidence use unless the gain survives causal intervention on what the model actually sees. **CalibForge** says terminal-task corpora should be calibrated against solver strength instead of mass-produced and prayed over. **Improving the Realism of Synthetic Clinical Benchmarks** says a benchmark that clears a utility threshold can still be structurally fake. **Does FLAIR super-resolution erase or hallucinate small white-matter lesions?** says a prettier reconstruction is not the same thing as lesion-faithful imaging.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **HERALD: Counterfactual Audits and Minimal Repairs for Proof-of-Retrieval Rewards**

**Verdict:** must read.

Why it matters: It turns retrieval-grounding rewards into something falsifiable. The useful move is a paired counterfactual audit that isolates citation laundering and then asks for the smallest repair that actually kills the exploit.

### 2. **The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use: A Causal Audit of Thinking with Images**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: It refuses to equate tool invocation with evidence use. The useful move is a three-level causal decomposition that separates policy choice, trajectory corruption, and step-level visual evidence gain.

### 3. **CalibForge: Adversarial Solver Calibration for Scaling Learnable Terminal Tasks**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: It treats synthetic task generation as a calibration problem against solver ability, not a volume problem. The useful move is the author-solver revision loop with stronger/weaker and multi-solver retention criteria.

### 4. **Improving the Realism of Synthetic Clinical Benchmarks Under Utility Constraints**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent paper.

Why it matters: It is a good benchmark-design paper because it refuses the lazy inference that "downstream utility passed, therefore the benchmark is realistic." The useful move is deterministic realism-targeted revision under an explicit utility floor, with a naive densification control to show what fake improvement looks like.

### 5. **Does FLAIR super-resolution erase or hallucinate small white-matter lesions?**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent runner-up.

Why it matters: It is a clean medical-imaging warning shot. The dominant SR failure is not hallucinating lesions out of nowhere but erasing small real ones, which is exactly the kind of upstream image-enhancement failure that can flatter downstream pipelines.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **HERALD**.

This is the sharpest direct paper in the batch because it attacks a failure mode agent people routinely wave away. A retrieval answer can look impeccably grounded while its citation path is fake in the only way that matters: the cited corpus item is real, but it was never retrieved, so the support chain is decorative. HERALD does not answer that with more vibes, more aggregate reward, or more heuristic overlap checks. It defines exact counterfactual edits on saved trajectories, measures attack success rate under paired scoring, and then identifies the observed inclusion-minimal repair: the single targeted strengthening `L` that kills isolated laundering where broader bundles were previously treated as necessary. That is good scientific hygiene and good agent taste. It separates scorer robustness from selection stability, and it shows why a verifier patch should be justified by the exploit class it actually closes.

The rest of the batch reinforces the same lesson from adjacent angles. **Visual Tool-Use** shows that a tool-use gain can be mostly non-visual shortcutting. **CalibForge** shows that a synthetic task set can look productive while being badly miscalibrated to the solver. **Utility-Constrained Clinical Benchmarks** shows that utility metrics can coexist with absurd templating and sparsity. **FLAIR super-resolution** shows that perceptual recovery can still destroy the clinically important small structures.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**HERALD** is strongest because it finds the real verifier gap instead of a straw-man one. Outcome-only rewards collapse completely, exact controls already kill some easy loopholes, but citation laundering still survives unless the reward explicitly checks whether cited evidence was actually retrieved. The important result is not just that `R[L]` reaches zero observed isolated-laundering success while broader hardening is non-monotone. It is that the paper explains why broader bundles can cancel under selection and why the targeted repair is the right one to keep.

**The Illusion of Visual Tool-Use** is strongest because it decomposes an accuracy bump into causal paths instead of calling the whole bump "evidence use." The result is ugly in a useful way: only 45.95% of the apparent tool-use gain comes from actual visual evidence, while 54.05% comes from correlated non-visual factors. The paper also gives two concrete miscalibration modes worth stealing as diagnostics: Calling Without Looking and Looking Without Planning.

**CalibForge** is strongest because it changes the unit of supervision quality. On Terminal-Bench 2.0, its 30B and 35B variants reach 32.58% and 47.57%, beating the strongest shared-protocol baselines by 6.36 and 6.75 points. That matters less as another leaderboard jump than as evidence that solver-relative task calibration produces better synthetic supervision than just scraping together more terminal tasks.

**Utility-Constrained Clinical Benchmarks** is strongest because the baseline pathology is embarrassingly concrete: 79.44% sampled-pair missingness, only 12.75% actionable rows, 38.94% zero-actionable patients, and 100% top-three token concentration. That is a benchmark that can pass utility checks while still barely resembling the stated domain. The deterministic revisions improve realism while the dense control shows how easy it is to preserve fake templating under superficial densification.

**FLAIR super-resolution** is strongest because it narrows the imaging question to the clinically relevant object. The main failure is lesion erasure, not hallucination, and ECLARE beats the INR plus cubic interpolation on recovering small-lesion signal. That is a better result than the usual perceptual-quality theater, even if the study is still simulated and segmenter-dependent.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The common lesson today is that systems get overcredited when a visible artifact is allowed to impersonate the support path that supposedly produced it. A citation is not retrieval evidence unless the cited passage was actually retrieved. A tool trace is not visual reasoning unless causal interventions show the answer changed because of what was seen. A synthetic benchmark is not realistic because downstream utility cleared a threshold. A sharper-looking MRI is not lesion-faithful because it looks nicer. The useful papers are the ones that replace flattering end metrics with explicit path checks, targeted audits, or solver-relative calibration.
