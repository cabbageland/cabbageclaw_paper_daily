# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-07-25

## Theme

Today's stronger papers all move the real claim to an inspectable interface. **OpenForgeRL** says agent training should happen in the deployed harness, not in a toy loop that only resembles deployment from far away. **Error Certificates for KV-Cache Eviction** says compression damage is unmonitorable unless the eviction design itself makes the missing mass statistically identifiable. **Sparse Concept Channels in Frozen 3D CT Vision Encoders** says clinically useful findings do not have to stay smeared across a giant latent soup; many live in a small, ablatable coordinate set. **Self-Supervised Learning of Structured Dynamics from Videos** says motion gets more usable once dominant change and residual change stop sharing a single token. **Do Pathology Vision-Language Models Truly See Pathology?** says a correct answer is not evidence of visual grounding if the benchmark lets text priors do the work.

The strongest papers today are not about bigger models looking impressive. They are about forcing a vague claim to expose its control surface. If you say you can train agents, train them in the actual harness. If you say cache compression is safe, make its damage identifiable online. If you say a medical encoder knows a finding, show which channels carry it and what breaks when you zero them. If you say a video representation understands motion, separate the dominant motion source from the leftovers. If you say a pathology VLM understands images, show that it actually needs the image and binds the queried concept to the right tissue. No robotics or VLA paper cleared today's top five.

**OpenForgeRL** is the most relevant paper today. The central lesson is simple and useful: if the harness is where agent behavior is actually made, then training outside the harness is training the wrong object.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **OpenForgeRL: Train Harness-native Agents in Any Environment**

**Verdict:** must read.

Why it matters: This is one of the clearest current attempts to close the train-deploy mismatch for agents. Instead of reimplementing a toy imitation of a real harness, it records real harness interactions through a lightweight proxy and couples them to standard RL infrastructure. The resulting models reach `31.7` `pass^3` and `55.9` `pass@3` on ClawEval, `33.7` on QwenClawBench, and strong GUI scores too, while also exposing a more interesting result: harness choice itself changes what is learnable.

### 2. **Error Certificates for KV-Cache Eviction via Randomized Design**

**Verdict:** must read.

Why it matters: The useful move here is not another eviction heuristic. It is the impossibility result plus the repair: deterministic top-`k` eviction cannot consistently know the error it caused, while a known randomized design can. The paper gets a per-step certificate with about `0.97` empirical coverage, strong certificate-error correlation, and a cleaner practical conclusion than the headline might suggest: randomization buys attribution, not generic failure prediction.

### 3. **Sparse Concept Channels in Frozen 3D CT Vision Encoders**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This is a rare medical-foundation-model paper that makes interpretability do real work instead of decorative post-hoc heatmaps. The claim is concrete: many radiological findings can be recovered from roughly `10` sparse channels, ablating those channels selectively collapses the target finding, and the same probe idea transfers across a second 3D abdominal VLM. The report-generation angle is also practical, beating CT-CHAT on clinical and NLG metrics at much lower latency.

### 4. **Self-Supervised Learning of Structured Dynamics from Videos**

**Verdict:** keep.

Why it matters: The paper's useful bet is that video dynamics should not be represented as one entangled transition blob. A recurrent model over frozen image features separates primary motion from residual motion and then probes better than naive global-feature baselines across the new ProbeMotion suite, even beating VGGT-style probes on `3/7` tasks despite much weaker supervision.

### 5. **Do Pathology Vision-Language Models Truly See Pathology?**

**Verdict:** useful direct evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This paper attacks a recurring multimodal lie: benchmark accuracy can go up while image dependence and grounding stay bad. Its best result is embarrassing in the right way: Gemini-3-Pro gets `53.5%` average accuracy across five pathology VQA benchmarks without visual input, and pathology-specific tuning can improve answer accuracy while reducing multimodal gain and attention IoU.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** OpenForgeRL.

The steal is not just "do RL on agents." It is to treat the harness as part of the model, because that is where tool use, context policy, branching, and state management actually live. The same control-surface lesson shows up in the other keepers too. **Error Certificates** says monitoring requires a design that preserves identifiability. **Sparse Concept Channels** says useful internal structure often lives in a small explicit subspace if you look correctly. **SDM** says motion becomes more usable when you split the dominant driver from the residue. **PathBind** says multimodal evaluation should test whether the evidence channel is causally necessary, not merely whether the answer string is right.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**OpenForgeRL** is strongest because it refuses the convenient fiction that agent training on simplified loops is "close enough." Caveat: the framework still inherits the quality limits of the chosen harnesses, synthesized tasks, and benchmark mix, and the behavioral analysis shows error recovery is still stubbornly weak.

**Error Certificates for KV-Cache Eviction** is strongest because it starts with a negative theorem rather than a new score. Caveat: the certificate is loose as an absolute bound, helps more with attribution than with generic failure prediction, and is most useful when cache damage is a real part of the error budget.

**Sparse Concept Channels** is strongest because it couples sparse probing to causal ablation and cross-backbone transfer instead of stopping at readout accuracy. Caveat: linear channel probes are not a full mechanistic story, and the report-generation win still depends on a fixed template verbalizer rather than richer open-ended generation.

**SDM** is strongest because it gives structure to temporal change without demanding a fully supervised 3D stack. Caveat: it still relies on weak synthetic supervision and probe-based evaluation, so this is more a representation paper than a full control-ready world-model result.

**PathBind** is strongest because it measures whether visual evidence is necessary and whether concept-to-region binding survives evaluation, not just whether a model says the right pathology word. Caveat: attention overlap is an imperfect proxy for grounding, and some of the strongest benchmark content depends on private atlas material.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The common lesson today is that systems look smarter when the decisive variable stays hidden. Agents look trainable when you ignore the harness. Cache compression looks safe when you cannot identify the damage channel. Medical encoders look mysterious when you never ask which coordinates carry a finding. Video dynamics look learned when camera motion and object motion stay entangled. Pathology VLMs look grounded when the benchmark never checks whether the image mattered. The better papers expose the hidden interface directly and then ask whether the claim still survives there.
