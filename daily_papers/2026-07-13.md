# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-07-13

## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **structure only matters if the system preserves the right state and exposes the right boundary**. **Interference and Retention in Continual Learning** turns forgetting into explicit overlap geometry, so protection happens before damage rather than after. **Workflow as Knowledge** argues that workflows, inference records, context snapshots, and approvals should persist as typed objects rather than disposable traces. **MultiView-Bench** shows frontier VLMs still fail when they must anchor multiple views into a fixed allocentric coordinate frame. **ALICE** demonstrates that pathology expertise at different scales can be distilled into one backbone instead of kept in separate specialist silos. **GeoTrace** keeps exact skeleton tokens plus traceable residual event tokens, which is much cleaner than saliency-heavy video-token mush.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Interference and Retention in Continual Learning**

**Verdict:** must read for continual learning and adaptive agent systems.

Why it matters: The paper replaces the usual replay-versus-regularizer framing with a cleaner one: forgetting is interference geometry. In the frozen-feature regime, forgetting from learning task B is exactly the interference energy imposed on task A, and when supports overlap in conflicting directions there is a real distortion floor that no clever policy removes. That is a better mental model than "catastrophic forgetting happened somehow." The derived IGFA rule is also useful: share directions when tasks align, protect them when they conflict, and treat capacity, incompatibility, and control as separate costs instead of one blurry retention metric.

### 2. **Workflow as Knowledge: Semantic Persistence for LLM-Mediated Workflows**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent inspiration with very high direct relevance.

Why it matters: This is a conceptual paper, not a benchmark win, but it is unusually aligned with durable agent runtime design. The key claim is that workflow definitions, running instances, inference records, approvals, context snapshots, and dependency relations should live as typed objects in a shared knowledge substrate rather than as source code plus logs plus half-semantic traces. The `derive` versus `infer` split is load-bearing: deterministic computation and model-mediated judgment should not pretend to be the same thing.

### 3. **MultiView-Bench: A Diagnostic Benchmark for World-Centric Multi-View Integration in VLMs**

**Verdict:** strong direct-relevance benchmark paper.

Why it matters: The benchmark asks a question many multimodal papers dodge: can a model integrate multiple views into a fixed global 3D frame, rather than merely describe one image at a time? On the hardest tasks, most frontier models are near random chance, and the failure analysis is concrete rather than theatrical: models are decent at 2D local relations but fail at axis-direction identification and multi-view aggregation. The budget-matched ViewNavigator result is also informative because it shows structured viewpoint selection and belief aggregation help even when test-time image budget is held fixed.

### 4. **ALICE: Learning a General-Purpose Pathology Foundation Model from Vision, Vision-Language, and Slide-Level Experts**

**Verdict:** strong healthcare foundation-model paper.

Why it matters: ALICE is not just another "bigger medical model" report. The important move is staged agglomerative distillation across distinct types of pathology expertise: morphology-heavy vision models, vision-language models, and slide-level clinical-context models. The evaluation breadth is serious too: 21 task scenarios, 96 downstream tasks, and 48 data sources. The paper is useful less as a generic leaderboard item and more as evidence that multi-scale specialist knowledge can be consolidated into one reusable backbone without fully flattening the differences between local tissue features, semantic alignment, and whole-slide reasoning.

### 5. **GeoTrace: Geometry-Aware Trajectory Token Compression for Video Large Language Models**

**Verdict:** strong lighter recommendation.

Why it matters: GeoTrace is a training-free token compression paper, but the mechanism is cleaner than the usual prune-by-saliency story. It preserves exact skeleton tokens for scene structure and compresses the residual through traceable one-to-one temporal trajectories instead of vague clustering mush. The reported tradeoff is strong enough to matter: on LLaVA-OneVision at 10 percent token retention, it cuts TFLOPs by `12.99x`, improves time-to-first-token substantially, and still preserves `99.1%` of baseline accuracy.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Workflow as Knowledge.

The steal is the boundary design. Long-lived agent systems should not blur deterministic computation, model judgment, human approval, and resumable workflow state into one log stream. This paper proposes explicit workflow definitions, workflow instances, inference records, context snapshots, and dependency relations as typed persistent objects, plus a clean `derive` / `infer` split. It is still a conceptual paper, but the framing is unusually aligned with durable tool-using assistants, provenance, reviewability, and agent runtime design.

**Interference and Retention in Continual Learning** gives the complementary learning story: protect geometry before forgetting happens. **MultiView-Bench** supplies the evaluation lesson: do not trust a multimodal agent in 3D software or embodied settings until it can aggregate views into a fixed global frame rather than parroting coordinate priors. **ALICE** supplies the domain-transfer lesson: multi-scale expertise can be consolidated if the distillation pipeline respects the differences between local morphology, language semantics, and whole-slide context.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Interference and Retention in Continual Learning** is strong because it turns forgetting from a symptom into a measurable object. The important ideas are the interference identity, the removability dichotomy, and the distortion floor. The diagnostic split between incompatibility, capacity, and control is more useful than the usual retention scoreboard because it tells you which kind of intervention could possibly help. Caveat: the exact claims are strongest in the frozen-feature or function-space regime, and the broader language-model evidence is still thinner than the theory.

**Workflow as Knowledge** is strong because it refuses the lazy assumption that "we have logs" is equivalent to semantic persistence. The paper introduces a concrete object vocabulary and a real boundary between deterministic `derive` steps and mediated `infer` judgments. The caveat is obvious and the paper admits it: formal transition semantics, lifecycle policy, governance, and empirical validation are all future work, so this is a design lens rather than a demonstrated runtime win.

**MultiView-Bench** is strong because it exposes a specific multimodal weakness that normal VLM benchmarks mostly wash away. The benchmark is about allocentric integration into a fixed coordinate frame, not generic "spatial reasoning" branding. The failure analysis is particularly useful: single-view narration is not the same thing as cross-view 3D grounding, and many models quietly default to conventional coordinate priors instead of reading the axes in front of them. The caveat is that the proposed ViewNavigator help still does not solve the task cleanly, especially on the hardest settings.

**ALICE** is strong because it treats specialization across pathology models as something to be integrated structurally rather than averaged away. The staged distillation setup makes the claim more believable than a vague multimodal mashup. The caveat is that the evaluation is still mostly retrospective, and the authors themselves point to missing prospective multi-institutional validation, broader cohort diversity, and more efficient deployment structure.

**GeoTrace** is strong because it identifies the actual failure of many token-compression methods: they over-focus on local saliency and then fuse the remainder into ambiguous features. The skeleton-plus-event decomposition is a cleaner representation interface. I kept it fifth because the claim is narrower than the top four and mainly about efficient inference rather than broader representational or evaluation framing, but it is still a useful mechanism paper.

**All Explanations are Wrong, But Many Are Useful** was a strong runner-up. I like the attempt to replace one canonical explanation with a set of faithful explanations plus a prediction-linked workflow, but the paper felt more over-claimed and less cleanly transferable than the final five.

**Video Generation Models are General-Purpose Vision Learners** was another strong runner-up. The pretraining thesis is interesting and the results are large, but the paper also admits important regressions in some joint-training settings and leans a bit too hard on the "generalist world model" rhetoric for where the evidence currently is.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today all reject flat, undifferentiated state. Continual learning should preserve or protect the geometry that actually causes interference. Agent workflows should retain typed records at the `infer` / `derive` boundary instead of dumping everything into traces. Multi-view VLM evaluation should ask whether the model can build a global coordinate frame rather than whether it can narrate a single image. Pathology foundation models should integrate local, semantic, and slide-level expertise instead of forcing users to pick one siloed backbone. Video token compression should preserve exact skeleton structure and traceable residual events rather than merge tokens into opaque mush. Same lesson at five scales: keep the state that carries the claim, and make its boundary inspectable.
