## Ranked papers

### 1. **When Generative Models Act as Simulators**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model/editability paper.

Why it matters: This paper separates three ideas that are too often collapsed: a model can decode simulator state, use something state-like internally, and still fail to let us set that state. The authors train the same eight-block transformer setup on Othello variants and Rayworld, then compare probe steering against direct inverse maps from simulator state into latent space. The sharp result is that decodability is everywhere high, but editability depends on environment structure and representation form: Othello editability narrows as rules simplify, while Rayworld continuous positions cannot be steered by probes but can be set by an inverse map. The steal-worthy idea is to evaluate world models by whether external state edits produce the right next observation, not by whether a probe can read a pretty variable.

### 2. **Why DiT Models Underperform as Representation Learners without Long Skip Connections**

**Verdict:** must read direct diffusion-representation mechanism paper.

Why it matters: This is a rare architectural paper whose mechanism is more useful than its headline. The authors show that long skip connections improve DiT feature quality even when generation FID is roughly aligned, then argue that the skip path is not only moving high-frequency image detail. It also gives diffusion noise a bypass around the middle target-reconstruction section, preventing the main representation stream from carrying noisy trajectory information through the features we later want to reuse. With LSCs, SPair(a) improves from 34.68 to 41.10, SPair(b) from 52.62 to 60.68, Horse-21 mIoU from 30.35 to 36.32, and FID from 20.87 to 14.39. The reusable lesson is clean: architecture can separate target representation from denoising bookkeeping.

### 3. **Spanning Diffusion and Flow-based Posterior Sampling via Exact Gaussian Conditioning**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion/flow inverse-problem paper.

Why it matters: beta-Flow gives guided posterior sampling a clearer control knob. Instead of baking in a diffusion, flow, or independently renoised trajectory prior, it uses a correlation parameter beta to span them and a confidence parameter kappa to control prior strength, then performs one denoiser evaluation followed by an exact Gaussian conditioning/Kalman update. On AFHQ-Cats and CelebA inverse problems it is consistently best or near-best in PSNR/SSIM, and on medical reconstruction it reaches 37.76 PSNR / 0.943 SSIM on FastMRI and 36.44 PSNR on sparse-view CT. The useful move is not another guidance heuristic; it is making the implicit trajectory prior explicit and tunable.

### 4. **Control-Ready Uncertainty for Trajectory Diffusion**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent diffusion/control uncertainty paper.

Why it matters: SCOPE turns diffusion trajectory uncertainty from an expensive sample cloud into analytic Gaussian tubes that a controller can actually query. The method distills score-curvature information into a structured block-banded plus low-rank precision head, so inference adds less than a millisecond after the diffusion backbone in the reported setup. In Y-corridor navigation, calibrated SCOPE raises success rates to 100/96/93/92% for 3/6/9/12 pedestrians, compared with 96/91/90/85% for a linear Kalman filter and lower rates for diffusion ensembles. On a real Franka task, static success rises to 0.83 versus 0.50 for Diff-MPPI, and dynamic human-avoidance success reaches 0.70 with fewer human collisions. The transferable idea is that uncertainty must be shaped for the downstream controller, not merely sampled.

### 5. **DiffusionART: Efficient, Stable and Scalable Reinforcement of Diffusion Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion-RL post-training paper.

Why it matters: DiffusionART gives a concrete diagnosis of why NFT-style forward-process reward training can be unstable: its positive and negative branches carry a hidden forward-reverse consistency force that joint training only partly cancels, while EMA stabilizes by restoration rather than by magic off-policy rollouts. The fix aggregates each prompt's endpoint rewards into a shared reward-improving direction before applying it to reverse-process states, then adds a second trust-region-masked restoration update. On HunyuanVideo-13B, it reaches late-stage VideoReward quality levels with 12.02x and 11.72x fewer GPU hours than DiffusionNFT and Flow-GRPO-Fast, while reporting 28.8% and 53.0% higher mean training rewards. The design lesson is aggregation before credit assignment: do not ask every denoising state to infer a scalar endpoint reward alone.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **When Generative Models Act as Simulators**.

It matters because cabbageland keeps wanting world models that expose persistent, editable state rather than only plausible futures. This paper gives a clean test: can the model be driven by a simulator state edit and produce the correct next observation? The answer is not the same as "a probe can decode state", which is exactly the kind of false comfort world-model work needs to stop accepting.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Generative Models as Simulators** matters because it evaluates latent editability against simulator counterfactuals, separating decodability, probe steerability, and state-setting through inverse maps.

**DiT Long Skip Connections** matters because it reframes LSCs as diffusion-noise routing, not just high-frequency feature transport.

**beta-Flow** matters because it makes the sampler's path correlation and prior confidence explicit, bringing diffusion and flow posterior samplers into one Gaussian-conditioning view.

**SCOPE** matters because it converts diffusion score curvature into controller-friendly precision tubes rather than treating uncertainty as a slow Monte Carlo afterthought.

**DiffusionART** matters because it removes a hidden consistency force in diffusion reward training and replaces EMA schedule tuning with state-drift restoration.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 13 pattern is interface honesty. A world model is not a simulator just because state is decodable. A DiT feature is not clean if denoising noise has to pass through it. A posterior sampler is not neutral if its trajectory prior is hidden. A trajectory diffusion model is not control-ready if uncertainty only appears after many samples. A diffusion reward optimizer is not doing credit assignment if every state receives a poorly localized endpoint scalar. The shared lesson is to expose the control surface and make its assumptions testable.
