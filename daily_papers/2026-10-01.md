# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-10-01

## Theme

Today is about making the generative interface carry structure. The strongest papers do not merely ask bigger models to smooth over mismatch. They put language and images into one continuous flow space, let a video world model keep both compact memory and explicit evidence, build physics constraints into a feasible manifold, use executable programs as material assets, and condition posterior sampling on the actual imaging operator.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **LOCI: Spatial Linear Memory for Streaming World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct video-world-model memory paper.

Why it matters: LOCI attacks the revisit problem cleanly: when a generated camera path returns to an old location, the model needs compact accumulated context and direct access to observation-level evidence. Full key-value history keeps detail but grows with time; pure recurrent state is bounded but can smear away the detail needed later. LOCI interleaves 15 recurrent-memory blocks with 15 historical-attention blocks in a Wan 5B video transformer. The recurrent path uses projective camera conditioning so memory reads and writes depend on viewpoint, then feeds its readout into the features that later query retained KV evidence. On MIND, zero-shot LOCI reaches 14.36 PSNR and 0.0455 MSE, beating several larger streaming world models under the authors' unified protocol. In the controlled same-backbone comparison, bounded-history LOCI improves PSNR by 0.89 dB over full softmax and is better in 44 of 50 segments. The caveat is that absolute fidelity is still low and real-world captures are not evaluated, but the mechanism is exactly the kind of memory interface cabbageland should track.

### 2. **Multimodal Flow: Unified Flow Modeling of Language and Vision in Embedding Spaces**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct generative-model paper.

Why it matters: Multimodal Flow replaces the usual split between discrete language prediction and continuous image generation with one continuous chunk-causal flow objective. Text blocks and images become ordered continuous hyperchunks: text preserves token order, images preserve spatial grid structure, and the same backbone predicts clean endpoints through flow matching. Joint attention handles cross-modal interaction, while modality-specific feed-forward networks keep representation statistics separate where sharing hurts. The result is not just conceptual symmetry: MF-1, a 1.6B model trained from scratch on 150B tokens, reports 0.821 GenEval, 83.44 DPG-Bench, and competitive VQA/POPE/MMBench performance. Matched comparisons beat fully discrete and hybrid baselines. The main caveat is that the paper stays in language-image tasks, with frozen codecs and no video or other structured modalities yet, but the hyperchunk interface is a serious candidate pattern.

### 3. **PMosFM: Preconditioned Manifold Matching for One-Step Physics-Constrained Generation**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct flow/physics paper.

Why it matters: PMosFM is a strong example of refusing to let physics be an afterthought in a sampler. Instead of training a generator and then projecting samples or optimizing residuals during sampling, it encodes constraints through a manifold decoder and learns transport in feasible intrinsic coordinates. Geometric preconditioning rescales coordinates using the decoder-induced metric; covariance preconditioning whitens interpolation states; finite-interval endpoint matching compares decoded physical endpoints. At inference it uses one learned transport evaluation followed by physical decoding. Across Darcy, Burgers, dynamic stall, Kolmogorov, and turbulence benchmarks, it keeps encoded residuals near numerical precision while reducing network evaluations from 20-200 in baselines to 1. The tradeoff is that the method depends on a usable feasibility parameterization and decoder, but that is also the point: when constraints are known, put them where they cannot be violated.

### 4. **BAM! Bayesian Anything Model: a foundation model for generative computational imaging**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct Bayesian/generative imaging paper.

Why it matters: BAM is the best non-robotics applied paper today. It upgrades the operator-conditioned RAM reconstruction backbone into a conditional flow map for posterior sampling, so the forward operator and noise level are supplied at inference time rather than baked into a model per instrument. The model is only 36M parameters, trained on large image corpora plus forward-operator libraries, and draws posterior samples in three steps without approximate likelihood guidance or manually tuned guidance weights. Across FFHQ, AFHQ, LSUN, DIV2K, and the Kohler camera-shake benchmark, the authors report that BAM variants beat specialized and leading zero-shot methods in LPIPS/sample quality at much lower cost, and the appendix extends to sparse-view CT and blind motion deblurring. The limitation is clear: additive Gaussian noise, mostly linear or mildly nonlinear known operators, non-blind inference, and no automatic model-mismatch detection. Still, it is a crisp interface lesson for physics-aware generative models.

### 5. **MatLoom: Layered Text-to-Material Generation in a Compact Program Space**

**Verdict:** highly relevant structured-generation paper.

Why it matters: MatLoom asks for a generated asset whose construction survives generation. It defines a compact layer-oriented material language where alpha-masked layers share spatial fields across coverage, color, roughness, height, and other PBR channels. A language model writes the source, parser feedback repairs it, preview critique revises it, and seed search explores stochastic variations while preserving non-seed source. The retained programs are short, with a median of 21 lines, and can be re-rendered, inspected, and edited. On a 141-prompt benchmark, the strongest configuration beats three diffusion text-to-material baselines across flat-layout prompt-alignment metrics; in a 30-person blind study over 20 prompts, MatLoom gets 59.2% of forced choices. The evaluation is not a proof of physical material accuracy or edit utility, and the seed-search budget is large, but the representation idea is useful: make the asset an executable, inspectable object rather than only a raster.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**LOCI** matters because it couples bounded recurrent scene memory with direct historical KV access instead of choosing one failure mode.

**Multimodal Flow** matters because it makes continuous flow matching a shared language-image generative objective while preserving modality-specific structure.

**PMosFM** matters because it moves physics constraints into feasible coordinates and one-step transport rather than sampling-time cleanup.

**BAM** matters because it treats the imaging forward operator as an inference-time condition for posterior sampling, not as a fixed training artifact.

**MatLoom** matters because it makes structured generation return a compact executable program, not just rendered pixels.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 1 pattern is interface accountability. A world model memory should retrieve old evidence by viewpoint, not hope recency is enough. A multimodal generator should define a shared unit of generation that does not quantize away images or split objectives by modality. A physics generator should represent feasible state directly. A computational-imaging model should condition on the actual measurement operator. A material generator should preserve the construction rules behind the surface. The shared lesson is simple and severe: the representation is not decorative; it is the contract with the next computation.
