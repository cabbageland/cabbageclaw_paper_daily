## Ranked papers

### 1. **What 30,000 Hours of Ego-centric Video Does Not Teach**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This is the best negative result in the carryover batch. The authors train Cosmos 3 world models on nested 300 to 30,000 hour ego-centric human-manipulation subsets and separate agent fidelity from object-interaction fidelity instead of hiding both inside a downstream score. Scaling improves both, but skeleton conditioning reaches the agent fidelity of the 30k-hour baseline with far less data, while object fidelity saturates much lower: the fitted skeleton-conditioned object ceiling is 0.565 SCS, with 93% of that already realized at 30k hours. Dynamic-region supervision raises object SCS from 0.527 to 0.546 at 30k, but the gap remains. The lesson is severe and useful: pixels and scale teach the agent's body before they teach contact, forces, and hidden object state.

### 2. **Slot3R: Set-Associative Spatial Memory for Streaming 3D Reconstruction**

**Verdict:** must read direct spatial-memory / 3D perception paper.

Why it matters: Slot3R fixes a clean memory bug in streaming 3D reconstruction. Point3R uses spatial proximity both to address memory and to decide whether evidence should merge; Slot3R separates those roles with K-way set-associative buckets, confidence-aware writes, and bounded sparse readout. Without retraining the Point3R backbone, it reduces Point3R point-cloud accuracy error by 57.1% to 63.1% on 7Scenes and 64.0% to 72.0% on NeuralRGBD at 300 to 500 frames, while increasing throughput by 5.6% to 14.3%. It also survives 600 to 1000 frame streams where Point3R and InfiniteVGGT run out of memory. This is a small architectural correction with large consequences.

### 3. **RiCo: Neural Simulation of Rigid-Body Interactions via Local Contact Reasoning**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct physical world-model paper.

Why it matters: RiCo makes contact local again. Instead of letting object-level attention summarize an entire collision, it builds sparse neighborhoods around nearby surface points, encodes signed contact geometry, relative motion, normals, and physical properties, reasons within each rigid body, and recovers a rigid transform from anchor displacements. On MOVi-A and MOVi-B, its 100-frame errors are 0.115 m / 11.40 deg and 0.111 m / 9.54 deg, clearly below RigidFormer and HOPNet. The important part is contact fidelity: on MOVi-B, ground-truth-relative penetration time is 11.0% with 2.22 mm mean depth, versus 44.8% and 267.70 mm for the RigidFormer reimplementation. The sim-to-real billiards result is only preliminary, but the mechanism is exactly pointed at the right failure mode.

### 4. **BudgetPix: Compute-Adaptive Tokenization for Pixel-Space Image Diffusion**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion / efficient generation paper.

Why it matters: BudgetPix turns token budget into an inference-time control knob for pixel-space diffusion. It uses an entropy-guided quadtree encoder, multi-scale patch embeddings, a scale-aware decoder, and training/inference schedules that let one checkpoint operate over variable token counts. The method is valuable because it does not merely prune tokens after the fact; it teaches the denoiser and decoder to work with non-uniform image layouts. On PixelDiT-T2I at 1024 squared, BudgetPix gets GenEval 0.725 at 25% budget versus the dense base model's 0.721 and the base model's own 0.323 at 25%. On MiniT2I-L at 512 squared, it stays near 0.874 GenEval even at 25%, while the base model collapses to 0.017. The caveat is that layout comes from intermediate predictions, so missed early details can remain missed.

### 5. **A Unified Bellman Operator for Safety-Critical Reinforcement Learning**

**Verdict:** useful direct RL/control paper.

Why it matters: This paper is theory-heavy, but the design move is good: put safety and task return into one Bellman operator instead of learning a reward policy and then stapling on a safety filter. The joint value truncates task accrual against a learned safety action-value, while a fast timescale tracks safety and a slow timescale tracks the joint value. The authors prove forward invariance and convergence to optimal safety-constrained task values under their assumptions, then scale the idea as JointSAC. Across 11 adversarial continuous-control environments, JointSAC reports near-zero evaluation costs, with maximum cost 0.03 plus/minus 0.08 on Hopper-v4 over 1,500 rollouts per environment setup. The caveat is large: the neural approximation no longer carries the strict proof, and the method assumes well-defined continuous safety margins under full observability.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **What 30,000 Hours of Ego-centric Video Does Not Teach**.

It matters because cabbageland keeps coming back to world models that can support planning, simulation, and counterfactual action evaluation. This paper says the uncomfortable part directly: scaling ego-centric video can make the agent visually plausible while leaving the agent's effects on the world much less faithful. For future work, that means object state, contacts, forces, occlusion, and supervision allocation cannot be hand-waved as things pixels will eventually teach.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**What 30,000 Hours Does Not Teach** matters because it separates agent fidelity from object-interaction fidelity and shows that conditioning can saturate the first while exposing a much lower ceiling for the second.

**Slot3R** matters because it separates spatial address from state identity in streaming 3D memory, avoiding premature fusion of nearby but distinct evidence.

**RiCo** matters because it models rigid-body interaction through sparse local contacts and signed geometry, then tests contact fidelity directly rather than only trajectory RMSE.

**BudgetPix** matters because it makes non-uniform tokenization native to pixel-space diffusion rather than a brittle post-hoc token merge.

**Unified Bellman Operator** matters because it puts safety constraints inside the value recursion and gives a concrete two-timescale learning story instead of relying on external action projection.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 10 pattern is that the carrier of state has to match the claim. Ego-centric video does not automatically expose hidden contact state just because there is more of it. Spatial memory should not average distinct local evidence merely because the pointers are nearby. A physical simulator should reason at contact surfaces, not only at whole-object summaries. A pixel generator should spend tokens where image structure demands them. A safe controller should make safety part of the recursive value target, not an afterthought. The shared rule is simple: more capacity helps, but only after the system is forced to carry the right object.
