## Ranked papers

### 1. **Frozen Flows Forget: Diagnosing and Restoring Lost Motion in a Latent-flow World Model**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model/evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This is a clean failure analysis of a latent world model that looks technically plausible while forgetting the thing manipulation needs most: motion. In the ODEWorld-style setup, a frozen DINOv2 reconstruction autoencoder keeps representation learning cheap and stable, while a trainable flow evolves latents toward a goal. The paper shows the pretrained flow can freeze the scene, and latent-only retraining can replace stillness with teleport-like object jumps because sparse latent anchors never say where motion belongs along the horizon. DART fixes part of this by keeping the encoder/decoder frozen and adding decoded-frame and region-level DINOv2 feature losses so the flow is graded in visible space. The best part is the metric warning: pixel L1 can reward static conditional means, so the "best" L1 model may be the one that erased motion.

### 2. **Training Object Permanence in World Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct video-world-model benchmark and training paper.

Why it matters: WROP is not another vibe benchmark for world models. It builds 150 Blender generators around object permanence and solidity, yielding a 1.5M-sample training corpus and a fixed 300-question exam. The evaluation is human pairwise judgment instead of a VLM judge, which matters because the tested capability is exactly the kind of core physical reasoning multimodal judges can miss. Fine-tuning Cosmos3-Nano into PWM-WROP makes it the top true-continuation model in their study and third overall, with 1679.5 Elo behind two reference-to-video systems that do not face exactly the same continuation constraint. The caveat is that benchmark-specific synthetic training can win without proving general physical intelligence, but the dataset/evaluation contract is unusually well-shaped.

### 3. **Accelerating Video Diffusion via Training-Free Trajectory Routing**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion systems paper.

Why it matters: TRACK is a practical deployment mechanism for video diffusion: run a large and small compatible denoiser offline on the same latents, measure step-level guided-prediction disagreement, and route the low-disagreement steps to the small model at inference. It keeps the scheduler, architecture, and weights unchanged and runs only one denoiser per online step. Across Wan 2.1, Cosmos 3, TurboDiffusion, and FastVideo, the paper reports 1.95x to 2.73x speedups while retaining comparable VBench-style quality and sample diversity. The transferable lesson is not just "use a smaller model sometimes"; it is that denoising timesteps have different substitutability, and that substitutability can be calibrated directly.

### 4. **Multimodal Thinking with Renderable Programs**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct multimodal reasoning paper.

Why it matters: SVGLM chooses the right intermediate medium for geometry-style visual reasoning: SVG is text-like enough for language models, visual enough to render back into the model's perception loop, and structured enough to inspect. The paper curates 8K SVG tool-call samples from MathCanvas-style geometry tasks using GPT-5 filtering, Gemini coordinate generation, and visual verification. Fine-tuning 7B/8B VLMs to generate SVG overlays improves MathCanvas-Bench weighted accuracy, with Qwen2.5-VL rising from 19.5 zero-shot and 21.8 direct SFT to 30.9 with SVGLM. The ceiling remains low, but the mechanism is real: explicit, deterministic visual workspaces beat raster image-editing hallucinations for auxiliary construction.

### 5. **Temporal Gradient Inversion for Private Trajectory Reconstruction in Embodied Reinforcement Learning**

**Verdict:** useful direct embodied-RL/privacy paper.

Why it matters: TRACE is a privacy paper with a useful systems lesson: temporally ordered gradients are not just a bag of per-frame leaks. In distributed embodied RL, per-step actor-critic gradients can expose both camera observations and actions, and adjacent gradient signatures carry physical continuity. TRACE uses a gradient encoder, causal transformer, and autoregressive decoder to reconstruct observation-action sequences, plus a policy-head argument for near-exact action recovery under ordinary entropy regularization. On held-out embodied scenes, it reports about 18.8 dB PSNR, 100% action recovery, and 3-4.5 ms per reconstructed frame, beating single-frame and optimization baselines. The result is less about one attack and more about treating ordered gradient streams as trajectory data that need sequence-aware defenses.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Frozen Flows Forget: Diagnosing and Restoring Lost Motion in a Latent-flow World Model**

It matters because cabbageland cares about world models that preserve the operational variables a downstream controller actually needs. DART is useful not because it is a giant new architecture, but because it catches a local pathology: a latent model can satisfy its training loss while losing motion timing, object continuity, and the scene-coupled dynamics that make the representation worth having.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Frozen Flows Forget** matters because it separates representation quality from training-signal placement and shows that pixel metrics can reward the wrong behavior.

**WROP** matters because it turns object permanence and solidity into a controlled train/eval contract for video world models, with human judgment rather than VLM-judge theater.

**TRACK** matters because it frames video-diffusion acceleration as step-level capacity allocation calibrated from cross-model disagreement.

**SVGLM** matters because it uses renderable vector programs as a checkable multimodal scratchpad rather than uninspectable latent image edits.

**TRACE** matters because it shows privacy leakage can be amplified by trajectory structure, not merely by individual gradient snapshots.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 27 pattern is explicit intermediate state. A latent flow needs supervision at the decoded motion level, not only in a convenient representation. A video world model needs object permanence tasks with known physical consequences, not generic perceptual similarity. A diffusion sampler can spend large-model capacity only where a small model demonstrably disagrees. A VLM can draw SVG programs and then inspect its own rendered work. A privacy defense has to protect ordered gradient streams, not just isolated updates. The shared lesson is harsh but useful: the state that matters must be externalized, measured, and stressed, or the model will optimize around it.
