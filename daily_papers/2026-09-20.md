## Ranked papers

### 1. **Conservation Buys Stability and Factoring Buys Counterfactuals in Physical World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model paper.

Why it matters: This paper is the cleanest mechanism paper in the batch because it refuses to treat "physics structure" as one blob. A symplectic learned-Hamiltonian update gives long-horizon stability, while an explicit linear factorization of the physical coupling gives changed-law counterfactual transfer. The double dissociation is the point: a symplectic but non-factored model remains stable yet fails the attractive-to-repulsive gravity flip, while a factored non-conserving graph model follows the flip but diverges; only the factored symplectic model does both. On the core three-body study it keeps drift bounded and reaches sign-flip nMSE around 0.03-0.18, while the non-factored symplectic twin stays near nMSE 2.7. This is exactly the kind of explicit-state lesson cabbageland needs.

### 2. **Generative Verification: Rethinking the Uncertainty Signal for Active Learning of Object Detection**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct uncertainty/evaluation paper.

Why it matters: The paper changes where an active-learning uncertainty signal comes from. Instead of asking a detector to judge its own outputs, it crops each predicted box and asks an independent conditional diffusion verifier to regenerate a label representation from the pixels. Disagreement covers localization failure, background false positives, and wrong-class detections in one scalar, and it exposes confident detector errors because the verifier never sees detector confidence. On MS-COCO, the method reaches 30.33 mAP50 at 6k labels and 31.53 at 7k, about one point above the strongest GMM baseline in both rounds. The caveat is scope: every experiment uses SSD/VGG16, but the mechanism is clean.

### 3. **BINDER: A Latent Variable Model for Probabilistic Medical Image Registration**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent medical uncertainty paper.

Why it matters: BINDER is useful because it takes an old registration workhorse, mutual information, and turns the hidden correspondence assumption into an explicit latent-variable model. That gives both a demons-like optimizer for multimodal nonlinear registration and a Gibbs/MCMC route to deformation uncertainty in high-dimensional 3D fields. The out-of-the-box robustness is the practical hook: in untuned settings, BINDER reaches abdomen MR-CT Dice 0.739 versus 0.336 for Elastix and 0.301 for ANTs, and lung CT-CT TRE 1.075 versus 1.821 for ANTs and 2.561 for NiftyReg. It is not a flashy foundation model, but it is a strong example of making uncertainty operational in a clinical image pipeline.

### 4. **EPIG-Tree: Compute-Optimal Branching for Gradient-Efficient Reinforcement Learning**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct RL paper.

Why it matters: EPIG-Tree is a good RL paper because it says entropy is only a proposal signal, not the thing branch budgets should optimize. Starting from a law-of-total-variance decomposition of local policy-gradient error, it separates decision uncertainty from continuation uncertainty and places branches where they should reduce gradient-estimator error per unit compute. In cloned-state dense continuous control, EPIG wins gradient-MSE in all nine dense environments of a thirteen-environment sweep. In online Wordle, it finishes at 0.850 win rate versus 0.805 for uniform turn trees and 0.790 for flat GRPO. The warning is important too: in single-turn math, tree-local credit helps, but token loss masks and advantage assignment dominate branch placement.

### 5. **Parallelism, Critical Windows, and Separations Among Diffusion Language Models**

**Verdict:** useful direct diffusion-theory paper.

Why it matters: This paper gives diffusion-language-model parallelism a sharper object than vibes about "committing tokens too early." It proves uniform and Gaussian diffusion can sample with query complexity scaling with dual total correlation, matching known masked-diffusion intrinsic-complexity scaling. Then it constructs random empirical measures where uniform and Gaussian diffusion need about O(sqrt(d)) score-oracle queries while masked diffusion needs about O(d). The claimed separation comes from critical-window width, not from token commitment: masked diffusion's informative noise range is asymptotically narrower. This is theory, not a recipe for training a better model tomorrow, but it clarifies what kind of bottleneck few-step dLLM sampling may actually face.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Conservation Buys Stability and Factoring Buys Counterfactuals in Physical World Models**

It matters because cabbageland keeps circling the same question: when does explicit structure actually do work? This paper answers in a way that is unusually useful. Conservation is not "physics" in general; it buys bounded long rollouts. Factorization is not "physics" in general; it buys counterfactual changed-law transfer. The paper's lesson is that a structural prior should name the failure it fixes, and should be tested by removing it while keeping the rest of the system matched.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Conservation / Factoring** matters because it separates stability from counterfactual transfer and shows each requires a different structural commitment.

**Generative Verification** matters because it moves active-learning uncertainty outside the detector being trained, making confident errors visible.

**BINDER** matters because it turns multimodal registration into a latent correspondence model that supports both optimization and posterior sampling.

**EPIG-Tree** matters because it treats rollout-tree construction as gradient-estimator design rather than entropy-based exploration theater.

**Parallelism / Critical Windows** matters because it identifies critical-window width as a source of diffusion-language-model parallelism separation.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 20 pattern is that useful structure is failure-specific. Energy conservation controls drift but does not know how a force law changes. A factored coupling flips a law but does not stop numerical drift by itself. A detector's own confidence cannot reliably reveal its confident errors. A registration metric cannot expose uncertainty until its hidden correspondences become variables. Entropy cannot decide a rollout-tree budget unless it affects the policy gradient. A diffusion corruption process cannot be judged only by whether it commits tokens; the informative noise window matters. The shared lesson is blunt: name the carrier, name the failure, then test the link.
