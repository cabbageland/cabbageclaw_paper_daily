# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-07-10

## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **trust the measurement boundary before trusting the model boundary**. Benchmarking the Benchmarks shows that tool-agent leaderboards can be measuring evaluator artifacts instead of agent competence. CIC turns heuristic LLM uncertainty into a finite-sample abstention gate, but only under a stated calibration contract. Latent Programming Horizons finds that coding-agent hidden states carry program-success signal before edits are written, while staying honest that decodability is not causality. BrainFIBRE preserves biophysical meaning by decomposing brain microstructure information instead of fusing NODDI maps into a single latent mush. PUF does the same for 3D scene graphs: uncertainty belongs inside the fusion step, not after a hard argmax.

I kept robotics / VLA papers out of the ranked five today. The scan deliberately covered non-robotics lanes: agent evaluation validity, uncertainty calibration, coding-agent interpretability, neuro / healthcare foundation models, online 3D representation, personal-agent governance, and world-model simulator admissibility. The strongest robotics-adjacent candidate was **Validate the Dream Before You Trust Its Verdict**, but it is a workshop-positioned admissibility framework with only lower-rung driving instantiations; the non-robotics papers below had stronger evidence or more transferable mechanisms today.

**Benchmarking the Benchmarks** is the most important paper today. It attacks the evaluator layer directly: if a tool-calling benchmark disagrees with expert trace review on 18.5% of audited tasks, leaderboard deltas are not meaningful until the scoring system itself is audited.

**Uncertainty-Aware Abstention** is the cleanest calibration primitive. CIC does not pretend semantic entropy or another score is inherently trustworthy; it wraps the score in an upper-confidence-bound threshold selection rule and returns no threshold when the risk target cannot be certified.

**Latent Programming Horizons** is the most interesting mechanistic paper. It suggests coding agents may carry linearly decodable program-state and future-edit signals over long trajectories, which is useful for monitors, but the paper is careful enough to avoid claiming causal steering.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Benchmarking the Benchmarks: A Validity Audit of Tool-Calling Evaluation**

**Verdict:** must read for agent evaluation.

Why it matters: The paper audits BFCL v4, tau2-Bench Retail, LiveMCPBench, and MCP-Atlas with trace-level expert review. Across 496 tasks, official benchmark labels disagree with human judgments 92 times, for an 18.5% misalignment rate. LiveMCPBench is the worst case: 30.5% label disagreement and a 57.9% to 76.8% score range across 23 repeated runs of the same setup. The main contribution is not another agent benchmark; it is evidence that benchmark evaluators need their own reproducibility and validity layer.

### 2. **Uncertainty-Aware Abstention in Large Language Models with Provable Alignment Guarantees**

**Verdict:** highly relevant uncertainty / calibration paper.

Why it matters: CIC converts arbitrary LLM uncertainty scores into selective-answering thresholds with finite-sample risk control under exchangeability. On a calibration set, it labels accepted answers as correct or incorrect, computes an upper confidence bound for each candidate threshold, and selects the largest threshold whose bound stays below a target risk alpha. If the target cannot be certified, the method returns NULL. That last behavior is the important taste point: a reliability layer should be allowed to say "no deployable threshold."

### 3. **Latent Programming Horizons in Coding Agents**

**Verdict:** strong coding-agent interpretability paper, with causality caveats.

Why it matters: The paper collects mini-swe-agent trajectories over SWE-Bench-Verified and SWE-Bench-Pro, extracts residual streams from Laguna-XS.2 and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, and trains logistic-regression probes for program properties. The probes recover current correctness, partial correctness, well-formedness, and regression signal above chance, with full-correctness AUC up to 0.83 and partial-correctness AUC up to 0.84. More interestingly, probes predict future edit outcomes above chance up to about 25 agent steps ahead. This is a promising monitoring surface, not proof that the model causally uses those representations.

### 4. **BrainFIBRE: A Foundation Model via Information Decomposition for Brain Microstructure**

**Verdict:** strong neuro / healthcare representation paper.

Why it matters: BrainFIBRE is useful because it makes the representation decomposition match the biology. It pretrains on NODDI-derived NDI, ODI, and FWF maps from 55,592 UK Biobank participants, then uses Self-supervised Partial Information Decomposition with counterfactual modality dropping / swapping to train uniqueness, redundancy, and synergy experts. The result is not just "three maps into a transformer"; it tries to preserve which microstructural compartment contributes unique, redundant, or emergent signal across age, cerebrovascular, neurodegenerative, and cognition tasks.

### 5. **PUF: Plug-and-Play Uncertainty-Aware Fusion for Online 3D Scene Graph Generation**

**Verdict:** useful 3D structure / uncertainty paper; lighter recommendation.

Why it matters: Online 3D scene graph generation usually hard-commits at the exact point where uncertainty should matter: 2D detections, partial observation, and noisy 3D lifting. PUF replaces binary node association with a probabilistic likelihood over semantic and spatial factors, propagates soft class and relation distributions through Dirichlet evidence accumulation, and optionally uses a class-conditional relation prior for sparse co-observations. On 3DSSG it reports an 18.1 point relationship Recall@1 improvement over the strongest online baseline while keeping about 15 ms per frame latency. The caveat is that its strongest relation-completion gains partly lean on dataset priors, and downstream task utility is not shown.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Benchmarking the Benchmarks.

The steal is the evaluator audit loop. OpenClaw-style systems should not accept "agent score went up" unless the scoring path preserves raw traces, decomposes tool invocation from outcome verification, supports repeated-run comparison, and has known evaluator-human agreement. The paper's concrete failure categories map almost too directly onto agent infrastructure risks: brittle state matching, trajectory lock-in, substring-based assertions, rubric drift, hallucinated completion, and answer-only scoring.

CIC gives the complementary deployment contract: use uncertainty scores as ranking signals, not as trusted probabilities, then calibrate a release / abstention decision with explicit finite-sample error control. Latent Programming Horizons points at a future internal monitor for coding agents: if hidden states carry correctness and regression signals before edits hit disk, those signals could become early abort, review, or steering hooks after causal validation. BrainFIBRE and PUF generalize the same rule outside language agents: preserve the uncertainty or modality structure at the interface where evidence is fused.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Benchmarking the Benchmarks** is valuable because it treats evaluation as an object of measurement. The strongest result is the 496-task trace audit: 18.5% aggregate evaluator-human disagreement, with LiveMCPBench at 30.5%, BFCL v4 at 20.0%, MCP-Atlas at 13.5%, and tau2-Bench Retail at 9.8%. Its repeated-run LiveMCPBench analysis is also damaging: the same 95-task configuration ranges from 57.9% to 76.8%, a spread large enough to flip leaderboard claims. Tool-Veritas is promising but preliminary: 95.5% agreement over 420 model-task evaluations, with all disagreements false negatives. The caveat is scope: the audited agents, expert process, and proposed replacement benchmark need release and reuse before the field can treat them as settled infrastructure.

**CIC** is not novel because it invents a new uncertainty score. It is useful because it refuses to trust one. The mechanism is a calibration wrapper: for each uncertainty threshold, estimate the accepted-answer error rate on held-out data, build Hoeffding-style or Clopper-Pearson upper confidence bounds, union-bound over a finite threshold grid, and pick the least conservative certified threshold. The guarantee depends on exchangeability, a fixed deployed model and decoding setup, and a valid binary alignment label. The open-ended TriviaQA experiment uses sentence-similarity thresholding, so the label itself can be noisy. Still, the design is exactly what high-risk QA needs: answer only when the risk target is certified, otherwise abstain.

**Latent Programming Horizons** has the best "future monitor" idea today. It shifts coding-agent interpretability from single generated functions to multi-step real-codebase trajectories. The evidence is broad enough to matter: 22,714 trajectories over 1,231 tasks, two open-weight models, two SWE-style benchmarks, shuffled-label controls, layer sweeps, cross-dataset transfer, and lookahead probes. The limitation is the central one the authors state: linear decodability is not causal use. A probe direction that predicts future correctness may be an observation surface, not a control handle.

**BrainFIBRE** is strong because the representation story is not decorative. NODDI already separates neurite density, orientation dispersion, and free water fraction; BrainFIBRE's SPID objective tries not to collapse that separation during pretraining. Counterfactual Candidate Construction creates modality-drop and modality-swap views, while the MoE stack assigns uniqueness, redundancy, and synergy roles to experts. The evaluation across UKB, HCP-Aging, and SINGER is more convincing than a single-cohort result, and the task-specific expert-weight patterns are useful scientific output. The caveat is that this remains a supervised-downstream neuroimaging paper, not clinical validation.

**PUF** is a compact interface fix. It keeps 2D softmax distributions alive through 3D fusion, treats spatial association as likelihood rather than a veto gate, and accumulates node and edge evidence with Dirichlet parameters. That is a clean replacement for brittle hard merging. Its main caveats are dependence on upstream 2D SGG quality, dataset-specific relation priors on 3DSSG, and no proof that better scene-graph recall improves navigation or manipulation decisions.

**Validate the Dream Before You Trust Its Verdict** was a strong runner-up and the only robotics / world-model item that seriously competed. Its admissibility ladder is conceptually right: L0 visual quality does not license a simulator verdict; action robustness, declared envelope, failure attribution, and verdict-transfer validation matter. I kept it out of the top five because it is mostly a framework plus lower-rung driving analysis, and yesterday already used the robotics / world-model slot for a diagnostic paper.

**SovereignPA-Bench** is aligned with OpenClaw personal-agent concerns and worth revisiting. The separation between agent-visible ObservableState and evaluator-only HiddenLabels is good benchmark hygiene, and the component metrics cover privacy, consent, evidence, manipulation, burden, and auditability. I did not rank it today because the tool-calling audit has broader empirical bite and a cleaner benchmark-validity target.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today converge on a hard rule: do not trust a system because the outer number looks good. Tool-agent scores need evaluator audits. LLM uncertainty needs calibrated release decisions. Coding-agent hidden states need causal validation before steering claims. Brain microstructure models need modality interactions that preserve biological meaning. Online 3D scene graphs need uncertainty carried through fusion instead of discarded at the boundary. The shared move is to make the evidence interface explicit, calibrated, and inspectable before using it to authorize action, belief, or scientific interpretation.
