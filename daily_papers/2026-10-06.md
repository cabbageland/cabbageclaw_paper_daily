## Ranked papers

### 1. **Frozen in a Frame: The Velocity Blind Spot in JEPA World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model representation paper.

Why it matters: this paper makes a clean, painful point: if the renderer is instantaneous and the JEPA target is a single-frame embedding, then velocity is not identifiable from that target for any encoder. The authors confirm the blind spot on official LeWM checkpoints, then introduce TI-JEPA, which separates pose and finite-difference motion codes. The useful part is not just the fix; it is the diagnostic discipline. RateIdent includes probe ladders, opposite-velocity kill experiments, and stop-at-goal planning tasks that force velocity to matter. The caveat is that downstream planning gains are still mostly controlled-environment results, with real PushT not yet positive, but the representational argument is strong.

### 2. **S2PD: Serial-to-Parallel Diffusion for Physically and Logically Consistent Video Generation**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct video diffusion paper.

Why it matters: S2PD gives a concrete answer to the failure mode where bidirectional video diffusion looks plausible but violates rules. It denoises autoregressively while noise is high, so interdependent state transitions get serial computation, then switches to full-video parallel refinement at low noise for detail and speed. Across games, physical simulators, Kubric, MPMWorlds, and real Rubik's Cube footage, serial methods beat matched bidirectional baselines by a wide margin. S2PD is not always uniquely better than other serial methods on symbolic tasks, but it is faster at matched block size and gives the right design lesson: commit structure serially, polish appearance in parallel.

### 3. **Learning to Read the Contextual Tokens in Diffusion Transformers**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct interpretability/control paper.

Why it matters: this paper treats the text-side contextual tokens inside MM-DiTs as readable internal state, not just conditioning residue. A Q-Former bottleneck maps frozen contextual tokens from multiple layers into a frozen LLM prefix, letting the LLM answer questions about the emerging image. The reader finds generation-specific semantics early in denoising, including details not specified in the prompt and even details produced under an empty prompt. The intervention is also useful: Contextual Alignment uses a SigLIP image embedding teacher to strengthen that semantic channel and improves FID / recall. The caveat is that the evaluation is still VLM-judge and human-centric image heavy, but the representation handle is real.

### 4. **H-JEPA: End-to-End Learning of Hierarchical World Models for Visual Planning**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct world-model planning paper.

Why it matters: H-JEPA trains a hierarchy of action-conditioned JEPAs, each with its own latent space and longer prediction scale. Planning runs top-down: upper levels optimize coarse progress toward the goal, lower levels track subgoals and emit primitive actions. The strongest result is not "hierarchy helps" in the abstract; it is the ablation showing that upper-level latent distances create better cost geometry on AntMaze, while temporal decomposition makes lower-level subgoal costs more monotonic than full-horizon costs. The limitation is honest: gains are environment dependent, Push-T degrades at deeper hierarchy because short episodes reduce usable training clips, and DROID is offline path-fidelity rather than closed-loop robot success.

### 5. **BrainTRACE: Tracing Longitudinal, Multimodal, and Volumetric Evidence in Brain MRI Clinical Reasoning**

**Verdict:** useful adjacent medical evaluation paper.

Why it matters: BrainTRACE is a good benchmark paper because it asks whether VLMs can trace a clinical evidence chain, not merely answer isolated medical-image questions. It builds 7,273 scored VQA instances from 1,778 longitudinal patients, 7,299 MRI studies, and roughly 29k co-registered 3D MRI sequence volumes. The benchmark tags longitudinal, multi-sequence, volumetric, and report-grounded evidence demands, then evaluates 20 VLM configurations. Current models can pick up isolated cues but collapse on open-ended and case-level synthesis; case reasoning fails by attrition across the six-step evidence chain. I am not preserving a full note today because the four mechanism papers above are more directly reusable, but this is worth remembering as evaluation framing.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Frozen in a Frame** matters because it turns "single frames do not show speed" into a formal target-identifiability critique of next-frame JEPA objectives.

**S2PD** matters because it separates serial structure formation from parallel detail refinement inside video diffusion sampling.

**Contextual Tokens** matters because it makes MM-DiT text-side contextual tokens readable and trainable as a semantic state channel.

**H-JEPA** matters because it separates the benefits of abstract cost spaces from the benefits of temporal subgoal decomposition.

**BrainTRACE** matters because it evaluates longitudinal clinical evidence chains rather than isolated image-answer accuracy.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 6 pattern is that temporal intelligence fails when the needed state is missing or trapped in the wrong interface. Video diffusion needs serial computation while structure is undecided, then parallel refinement after the skeleton exists. JEPA world models need velocity in the target state, not just in the predictor history. Diffusion transformers already carry semantic commitments inside contextual tokens, and those tokens can be read and strengthened. Hierarchical world models help when upper levels supply better costs and shorter subgoals, not because "hierarchy" is magic. The shared rule is blunt: if a model's behavior depends on time, make the temporal state explicit enough to inspect and plan with.
