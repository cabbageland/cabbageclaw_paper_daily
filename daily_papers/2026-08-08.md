# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-08

## Theme

Today's strongest papers all attack the same research sin: using flattering proxy scores where you really need traced evidence, calibrated targets, or physically grounded measurements. **TRAJDEBUG** refuses to call debugging "find the bad vibe in the trajectory" and instead traces explicit error triggers, lifecycle state, and terminal footprint. **GAUGE** refuses to treat visual plausibility as physics and measures simulators and video world models against real trajectories and calibrated parameters. **A Unified Risk View of Uncertainty** refuses to grade uncertainty with proxy tasks like OOD detection and instead builds oracle epistemic and aleatoric targets. **The Low Frequency Trap** refuses to trust final-answer accuracy for video reasoning and audits the reported event trace itself. **Big, Bright, or Invisible** refuses leaderboard mythology around medical encoders and shows a simpler physical bottleneck: small, low-contrast lesions stay hard no matter how fancy the frozen model sounds.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **TRAJDEBUG: Tracing Error Lifecycle to Identify Critical Failures in Long-Horizon Agent Trajectories**

**Verdict:** must read.

Why it matters: It turns agent debugging from a vague postmortem into a staged evidence problem: detect explicit wrong commitments, group them by violated reference, classify whether they were really repaired, and attribute failure only among terminally relevant candidates.

### 2. **GAUGE: A Measurement-Grounded Benchmark for Physical Fidelity in Simulation Engines and Video World Models**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: It is unusually disciplined about physical evaluation. The useful move is separating law-form agreement from actual parameter fidelity, using the same real-world measurement base for both classical simulators and generative world models.

### 3. **A Unified Risk View of Uncertainty: Posterior Risk for Disentanglement and Evaluation Beyond Proxies**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: It stops uncertainty work from hiding behind proxy tasks. The useful move is defining epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty as oracle-computable posterior-risk targets on semi-synthetic datasets with real covariates and known generative structure.

### 4. **The Low Frequency Trap: Video Language Models Fail at Simple Event Bookkeeping**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent paper.

Why it matters: It shows how badly final-answer accuracy can flatter a video model. The useful move is executable event traces and capability surfaces over count and frequency, which expose where event access, retention, and aggregation actually break.

### 5. **Big, Bright, or Invisible: A Frozen-Feature Benchmark of 3D CT Foundation Models**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent runner-up.

Why it matters: It replaces architecture worship with a simpler constraint: if the abnormality is small and low-contrast, frozen global embeddings mostly fail regardless of branding. That is a useful medical-imaging reality check.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **TRAJDEBUG**.

The mechanism is exactly the right kind of severity. It does not pretend "critical error" can be read off from whichever step looks dumbest in hindsight. It insists on a violated reference object, a citable wrong commitment, a state label saying whether the error was actually repaired, and a terminal-footprint test before final attribution. That is the right instinct for agents with long histories, tool calls, retries, and fake recoveries. The application result matters too: diagnoses converted into rerun guidance improved success by 10.80%, and compact failure memory transferred another 5.70% to held-out tasks. The paper is still LLM-judged rather than mechanically replayed, but the decomposition is strong enough to steal even if the exact benchmark disappears.

The rest of the batch reinforces the same taste from different angles. **GAUGE** says physical fidelity is not whatever looks plausible in a rollout video. **Posterior Risk** says uncertainty quality is not whatever correlates with an OOD proxy. **Low Frequency Trap** says a correct final count is not evidence that the model actually tracked events. **Big, Bright, or Invisible** says a foundation model win is often just a visibility win.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**TRAJDEBUG** is strongest because it decomposes debugging into auditable objects instead of one holistic prompt. The key move is not just candidate-error mining. It is lifecycle state: clean resolution, costly resolution, manifest active, or latent active, with only terminal-relevant instances surviving to attribution.

**GAUGE** is strongest because it grades simulators and video world models against the same real-world reference discipline. The numbers are sharp enough to matter: the best bouncing-ball simulator result is still 15.63 times the real baseline RMSE, and world models can fit the expected equation form while still getting acceleration, momentum transfer, or oscillation timing wrong.

**Posterior Risk** is strongest because it attacks the benchmark definition itself. On its held-out semi-synthetic suite, predictive accuracy is comparatively similar while uncertainty disentanglement is not: many methods stay well below near-oracle ranking quality, and the practical winners are more like deep ensembles, FSP-Laplace, and CatBoost-KGB than whatever uncertainty story a method markets.

**Low Frequency Trap** is strongest because it demonstrates a specific metric illusion. In the high-count, high-frequency region, Gemini 3.6 Flash gets only 0.2% final counts exactly right and recovers 18.1% of true events; denser sampling raises Bounce Ball final accuracy from 19.6% to 29.3% while trace agreement remains only 3.7%.

**Big, Bright, or Invisible** is strongest because it exposes a transferable bottleneck. Fine tokenization plus vision-language alignment helps, but the deeper constraint is physical salience: contrast and spatial extent dominate performance, while tiny low-contrast lesions collapse toward chance.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The lesson today is to stop rewarding summaries for impersonating the thing they summarize. A failed trajectory summary is not a failure cause. A plausible-looking rollout is not physics. An OOD proxy is not oracle uncertainty. A correct final answer is not faithful event tracking. A big encoder is not lesion sensitivity. The useful papers are the ones that replace a flattering aggregate score with an explicit diagnostic object you can inspect, falsify, or route on.
