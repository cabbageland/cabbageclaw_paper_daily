## Ranked papers

### 1. **Normalizing Flows are Capable World Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant world models, generative modeling, representation learning, and model-based RL. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.33872](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33872), Zijia Li, Yong-Hyun Park, Shusheng Yang, and Jiatao Gu; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33872).
- **Mechanism:** Jointly train an invertible observation encoder and an action/history-conditioned latent density by exact conditional observation likelihood: latent NLL minus the encoder log-Jacobian determinant. The main predictor emits a diagonal Gaussian; sampled latent futures can be rolled forward without image decoding. Reward/termination heads support planning and imagination RL. An auxiliary inverse-dynamics loss shapes coordinates specifically for Euclidean goal-distance planning.
- **Why it matters:** Information preservation, stochastic prediction, and decision geometry are different properties. Invertibility prevents a constant encoder, but does not make latent distance a good task score. The paper exposes that separation rather than treating successful reconstruction as sufficient evidence of a useful world state.
- **Evidence:** Likelihood-only world-model training plus subsequently fitted task heads reaches the 100-pipe cap in all ten held-out Flappy Bird games. Four deterministic control tasks average 84.5% success versus LeWM's 82.5% and SMWM's 74.8%. Stochastic Boxing imagination learning reaches 77.37 ± 9.28 return, versus 64.47 ± 6.32 for the deliberately three-frame-restricted DreamerV3 comparator. A one-run Push-T ablation falls from 86% to 34% without inverse dynamics. In the custom LIBERO action-ranking task, NFWM reaches 46% versus 23.3%/32.7%; using actual simulator endpoints raises its score only to 54%, exposing a goal-cost bottleneck beyond prediction error.
- **Catch:** Exact likelihood is for Gaussian-noise-smoothed observations, not exact physical dynamics or calibrated epistemic uncertainty. Full-dimensional patch states are expensive: Push-T planning takes 10.038 seconds per decision versus 0.286 for compact LeWM, though full-patch LeWM costs 10.478 seconds. Separate models are trained per setting; the robotic ranking result is one custom task, and the 40-task frozen-feature action experts are a different experiment. Final Mean scoring omits terminal variance. This is not a real-robot deployment or a general victory over unrestricted Dreamer.
- **Read scope:** Main construction/objectives, all main experiment and discussion sections, conclusion/limitations, and selected appendix data/splitting/CEM protocols. No full proof, full appendix, visual-gallery, or implementation audit. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/normalizing_flow_world_models_information_vs_geometry.md).

### 2. **Rank Before You Encode: Reading a Few Percent of a Whole-Slide Image**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical AI, efficient representation use, and evaluation design. **Verdict:** Highly relevant as a baseline/protocol contribution; preserve.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.34523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34523), Haoyu He and Mahdi S. Hosseini; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34523).
- **Mechanism:** Fit two small linear scorers to a frozen attention-MIL teacher's region-aggregated attention using KL loss. A DINOv2 thumbnail ranks regions before expensive encoding; encode only the top K at 5x, then inspect the best b regions at 20x. Feed those fine-tile features into an existing full-bag MIL model without retraining its weights. Compare both intelligent and random selection at matched encoder cost.
- **Why it matters:** An efficiency method must beat the existing model given less input, not only the existing model given every tile. Separating selection from aggregation reveals whether a new architecture adds anything beyond cheaply finding sufficient evidence.
- **Evidence:** Across the six reported non-mean-pooling aggregators and six design tasks, (K,b)=(1000,10) stays within 0.005 AUROC of each model's full bag in 33/36 pairs at about 6.6% encoding cost, with 10–14x measured speedup excluding tiling. The faster (500,10) point is within that margin in only 22/36 pairs. A frozen gated-ABMIL at that fast point exceeds nine efficient methods' ten settings in 55/60 paired comparisons, but its own full bag already wins 59/60. Seven aggregators in total, a second encoder, and held-out tasks/transfers broaden the investigation.
- **Catch:** Small lesions are the failure mode, not an incidental caveat: fast-budget CAMELYON17 AUROC losses reach 0.035, with specificity losses at fixed high sensitivity. A validation-driven performance-first budget can need most of the full-bag cost, and one cross-cohort budget transfer loses 0.017 AUROC. Teacher attention is a selection target, not lesion truth; selector fitting needs cached full training features. Baselines use published settings rather than a matched tuning search. Code/splits are promised upon acceptance, not established as released here; RCC staging is site-confounded.
- **Read scope:** Main selection algorithm and losses, cost/random-control definitions, task/split/statistical protocol, main budget/results/transfer/failure analysis, and conclusion. Selected supplement references identified but not exhaustively inspected; no slide-level or code audit. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/rank_before_encode_frozen_mil_budget_baselines.md).

### 3. **Understanding Mode Collapse in Diffusion Models under Classifier-Free Guidance**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow mechanisms and controllability. **Verdict:** Useful analytical companion; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.33719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33719), Daniel Pfrommer, Chenyang Yuan, Frank Permenter, and Ali Jadbabaie; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33719).
- **Mechanism:** Model the conditional distribution as a Gaussian mixture with means on an ellipsoid and the weaker unconditional distribution as one Gaussian. Derive a scaled-mixture transport plus a reweighting toward the trajectory originating at zero initial noise. In special one-dimensional/symmetric cases the density has an explicit closed form. The model predicts both outward component-specific feature magnification and inward skew, not merely universal variance shrinkage.
- **Why it matters:** A guidance scale can amplify features while distorting mode probabilities. Quality improvements and a larger spread along selected directions can coexist with biased semantic coverage; neither a single covariance statistic nor attractive samples establish diversity preservation.
- **Evidence:** Selected text-conditioned SD1.5/FLUX.2-klein-base-9B experiments and class-conditioned DiTs trained by the authors test directional magnification and standardized within-cluster skew, with 10,000 samples per condition/scale in the density experiments. A separate five-prompt FLUX mode experiment uses 512 shared initial noises per scale and a VLM mode scorer. Trends support the predicted directional effects; this is not a general benchmark win or a proposed sampler fix.
- **Catch:** Shared conditional/unconditional component covariance is explicitly unrealistic, mixture geometry is restricted, and exact learned flows are assumed. The general density formula still depends on solving a flow; fully explicit closed forms are special cases. Literal zero initialization produces artifacts, so empirical central-mode probes use low-frequency-zeroed noise. Cluster axes and VLM labels are measurement choices. Do not read this as a theorem that arbitrary guided models collapse to one image.
- **Read scope:** Model setup, main density/reweighting results and special-case statements, selected empirical protocol/results, appendix limitations and sampling details. Proofs and every plotted condition were not audited. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Two Timescales of Forgetting in Fine-tuning of Language Models: Fast Behavioral Suppression and Slow Representational Drift**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant continual learning and representation evaluation; adjacent to large-scale alignment. **Verdict:** Useful controlled mechanism study; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.33302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33302), Yilin Luo, Jin Hwa Lee, Andrew M. Saxe, and Antonio Sclocchi; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33302).
- **Mechanism:** Pretrain a transformer on a known hierarchical grammar, delete some production rules during SFT, and separately measure generating those rules versus interpreting them in context. Early output suppression is consistent with a fixed-feature correlation signal; slower parameter drift eventually degrades representations. Compare learning-rate scaling, weight decay, and a pull toward pretrained parameters.
- **Why it matters:** A behavior disappearing is not evidence that its supporting representation disappeared. Evaluate generation, contextual use, representation probes, and relearning separately before calling adaptation successful retention or successful erasure.
- **Evidence:** Controlled grammar experiments report behavioral timescales scaling roughly as inverse learning rate and representational forgetting as inverse learning-rate squared. English/French and English/Turkish studies recover the separation using context/coherence and relearning controls. Targeted formatting fine-tunes of Qwen2.5-Coder 0.5B/1.5B give selected larger-model examples, explicitly described as limited cases.
- **Catch:** The strongest account is for restricted-distribution SFT on controlled data, not arbitrary task shifts, RL post-training, or guaranteed knowledge removal. Similarity/probe results are operational evidence, not complete access to stored knowledge. The bilingual study uses a trained small transformer, not frontier-model clinical/safety behavior; the larger-model tests are narrow syntax changes.
- **Read scope:** Introduction/setup and probe descriptions, selected scaling/control claims, main natural-language results, discussion/limits. No full appendix derivation or large-model experiment audit. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Casper: A Projection-Based Neurosymbolic Layer for Scalable & Guaranteed Constraint Satisfaction**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant explicit structure and verification; adjacent inspiration for constrained decoders. **Verdict:** Useful with an important compilation boundary; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.33527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33527), Lohith Konathala, Luca Andolfi, and Eleonora Giunchiglia; [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33527).
- **Mechanism:** Offline signed-resolution compilation makes each categorical variable's feasible choices explicit given earlier assignments. At inference, project its predicted probability vector so its argmax lies in that feasible set, parallelizing independent variables. A closed-form surrogate preserves feasibility and the chosen admissible argmax, but is not the exact continuous Euclidean projection. The training analysis motivates a correction regularizer to restore attenuated gradient directions.
- **Why it matters:** Put checkable constraints at the output interface rather than hoping a soft penalty learns them. Separate the discrete guarantee from closeness to the original probabilities, correctness of the supplied constraints, and cost of building the interface.
- **Evidence:** MNIST arithmetic scales to 1,024-digit sums; tested 30x30 Warcraft outputs are all valid, while the strongest approximate comparator is below 7% validity. Sudoku intermediate solver accuracy is 66.0% for the neural-solver pipeline versus 25.2% for CTSketch; final pipeline accuracy is a separate 84.7% and includes a Prolog solver. These are distinct validity, intermediate accuracy, and pipeline metrics.
- **Catch:** Compilation is exponential in conflict width, explicitly in Theorem 5; fast inference is not polynomial-time general SAT. Guarantees concern satisfiable supplied categorical constraints and induced assignments, not semantic truth, global optimality, or well-calibrated probabilities. Variable order matters. A single forward pass can still contain sequential variable steps. No theorem-proof or implementation audit is claimed.
- **Read scope:** Problem specification, exact/surrogate projection distinction, gradient discussion, compilation complexity, and selected arithmetic/Sudoku/conclusion results. Warcraft result checked in main report, not the full protocol. No detailed note today.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Normalizing Flows are Capable World Models.** Steal the separation of concerns: an invertible state retains observations, a predictive density represents possible futures, and a task-aware readout or geometry decides which differences matter. The strongest actionable diagnostic is the simulator-endpoint control: even an accurate future can be ranked incorrectly by the goal metric. Rank Before You Encode is the complementary baseline lesson: test whether smarter decisions require a new model at all.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **World models / RL:** Compare likelihood-only and inverse-dynamics-shaped representations; distinguish reward-head planning from goal-distance planning. Match predictive-state size, context, rollout budget, and wall-clock cost. Include simulator-endpoint rankings to isolate the scorer's failure from the predictor's.
- **Medical / efficient modeling:** Add frozen existing-model plus cheap selection and random-subset baselines at equal total encoder cost, including thumbnail/coarse work. Report small-lesion sensitivity and transferred-budget failures, not just average AUROC and peak speedup.
- **Diffusion:** Test cluster-conditional magnification, skew, semantic mode mass, and independent quality. State which idealized distribution admits the theoretical result; do not turn a special-case explanation into a universal sampler law.
- **Continual learning:** Separate suppressed generation, contextual comprehension, representation accessibility, and relearning. Test new-distribution adaptation before extrapolating restricted-rule SFT dynamics.
- **Neurosymbolic systems:** Account for offline compilation, constraint width, variable ordering, and accuracy as well as inference feasibility. Exact validity does not imply exact probability projection or task optimality.

## One-paragraph takeaway

Preserving information is only one job. A flow can retain every pixel while its goal distance ranks failures first; a whole-slide model can keep its accuracy after most tiles are removed but miss the tiny lesion that matters; guidance can magnify features while reweighting modes; SFT can suppress outputs while leaving usable internal structure; and a logical layer can guarantee feasible assignments without guaranteeing good answers. The reusable experiment is to hold one layer fixed and audit the next interface: prediction versus scoring, selection versus aggregation, representation versus behavior, and feasibility versus correctness.
