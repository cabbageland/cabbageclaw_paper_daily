## Ranked papers

### 1. **World Models' Last Exam in Physics**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This paper does the thing video-world-model benchmarks keep avoiding: it measures physical relationships instead of trusting plausible pixels or a VLM's physics vibes. The benchmark has 40 tasks across mechanics, optics, fluids, phase changes, electromagnetism, granular media, surface tension, and viscous flow. Each generated video is first screened for temporal consistency and observability, then task-specific tools extract quantities like trajectories, periods, reflection angles, liquid levels, and event timing. The best of eight video models reaches only 57.76 out of 100 across 1,280 videos, and some tasks collapse completely. The important design lesson is the separation between "is there measurable evidence?" and "does the measured evidence satisfy the law?"

### 2. **Feature Information Dynamics in Diffusion**

**Verdict:** must read direct diffusion / representation paper.

Why it matters: This is a rare diffusion diagnostics paper that gives the folklore a ruler. Using I-MMSE, it defines feature information density as the denoising-loss gap between unconditional and feature-conditioned models at each SNR. The chained version avoids double-counting when features share information, such as class, mask, and Canny edges. The experiments show pixel diffusion behaves like spectral autoregression for frequency bands, but the semantic hierarchy depends heavily on representation: pixel space can put mask information before class identity, while RAE gives the cleanest class -> mask -> Canny order and the fastest matched-recipe convergence. The transferable point is that representation choice controls when information becomes available, not only what final FID says.

### 3. **Parallel Predictive World Models for Accurate and Efficient Long-Horizon Planning**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct world-model planning paper.

Why it matters: PPWM separates temporal causality from autoregressive decoded-state feedback. Instead of feeding each predicted latent into the next transition, it builds action-prefix queries for future horizons, lets them interact through causal transformer layers, and decodes the whole span in parallel. On OGBench-Cube, Push-T, Two-Room, and Reacher, PP-LeWM gets the lowest long-horizon latent error and the best CEM simulator success among the tested interfaces. It also cuts average CEM latency from 1235.2 ms for LeWM to 374.5 ms, a 3.30x speedup. The useful mechanism is not just multi-step prediction; it is making the predicted trajectory a joint planning object.

### 4. **DepthWorld: 3D World Model for Robot Manipulation**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct robotics/world-model paper.

Why it matters: This is the one robotics paper today that clears the higher bar because it makes geometry part of the world-model state. DROID-3D recalibrates more than 70,000 DROID episodes into dense metric depth and robot-grounded multi-view extrinsics through learned stereo depth and a joint factor graph over robot episodes. DepthWorld then trains a Stable Video Diffusion-based action-conditioned model to predict multi-view RGB and depth together by spatially tiling RGB and depth latents, preserving pretrained video priors. Joint depth prediction improves RGB itself by +1.48 dB PSNR on external views and +1.02 dB on wrist views, while producing metric depth for downstream geometric reasoning.

### 5. **Sensor Geometry as a Flow-Matching Prior for Multi-Channel Brain Signals**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent generative-modeling / neuro-signal paper.

Why it matters: This paper makes a simple prior do real work. Flow matching usually starts from isotropic Gaussian noise, but EEG sensors have known spatial layout and nearby electrodes share structure through volume conduction. The method builds a sparse k-nearest-neighbor graph over sensor coordinates, uses the graph Laplacian eigenvectors as spatial modes, and samples source noise from a graph-Matern covariance. Across eight EEG datasets and four flow-matching formulations, the graph prior lowers PSD-KL by 12% to 17% in geometric mean, with large reductions on dense montages such as PhysioNet-MI. The best ablation is the warning: empirical covariance priors perform worse than isotropic noise, while the sparse physical graph eigenbasis carries the gain.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**World Models' Last Exam** matters because it turns physical evaluation into measured relationships with explicit failure evidence.

**Feature Information Dynamics** matters because it localizes when feature information appears in diffusion and shows representation choice changes the generation order.

**PPWM** matters because it removes decoded-state feedback while preserving causal future-state interaction for planning.

**DepthWorld** matters because it makes metric geometry a native rollout output for robot world models and shows depth supervision can improve RGB prediction.

**Sensor Geometry Prior** matters because it puts known sensor geometry into the flow source distribution instead of making the drift network rediscover it.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 7 pattern is explicit structure at the right interface. A video world model should be scored by measured physical variables, not visual plausibility. A diffusion representation should be judged by when its features enter the trajectory, not only final FID. A planner should see a joint future trajectory, not a brittle decoded autoregressive chain. A robot world model should predict metric depth, not hallucinate geometry behind RGB loss. A sensor generator should start from the geometry everyone already knows. The shared rule is blunt: if the structure matters downstream, put it somewhere the system can use and the evaluator can check.
