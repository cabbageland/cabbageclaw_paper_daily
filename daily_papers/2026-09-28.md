## Ranked papers

### 1. **OneWorld: Learning Consistent Physics Across Actions in World Models**

**Verdict:** must read direct world-model evaluation and training paper.

Why it matters: OneWorld names a failure that single-rollout video metrics largely miss: several futures from the same initial scene can look plausible while implying different friction, mass, gravity, stiffness, or fluidity. The method trains a physical mechanism interpreter over action-outcome branches, computes a prior-corrected shared-world evidence score, and uses that score both as a shared-world loss and as sampling-time flow guidance. The useful part is the evaluation contract: compare the best branch-wise physical fit with a joint simulator fit that forces all branches to share one physical configuration. In Push Cube, Push Rope, and Pour Water settings, OneWorld cuts the mean shared-world gap by 84.3% versus ACWM-DiT while slightly improving masked MSE. This is exactly the sort of test a planning world model needs: not just "does each imagined future look fine," but "can these futures coexist in one world?"

### 2. **FuseReg: Regularizing Layer Fusion Mitigates the Reconstruction-Generation Gap in Representation Autoencoders**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct generative-representation paper.

Why it matters: FuseReg is a clean diagnosis of an interface problem in representation autoencoders. Frozen visual encoders expose many layers; shallow layers help pixel reconstruction, deeper layers can be easier for the generator, and a fixed fusion ties the decoder and DiT to one compromise. FuseReg samples normalized random subsets of encoder layers during training, so the deployment latent stays mean-preserving while downstream models are penalized for sensitivity to cross-layer disagreement. On ImageNet-256 with DINOv3-L, one FuseReg decoder reconstructs from full, sparse, and single-layer fusions, and simply swapping the decoder into a fixed RAEv2 DiT-XL reduces unguided gFID from 3.01 to 2.21. Joint decoder/generator regularization cuts DiT-Base unguided gFID from 13.96 to 9.93. The transferable lesson is sharp: the representation is not just what the encoder emits; it is also how robustly downstream consumers read across its hierarchy.

### 3. **NEMSim: Learning Control-Conditioned Multi-Event Physical Dynamics via Executable Event-Mechanism Priors**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct scientific world-model/control paper.

Why it matters: NEMSim is a strong example of turning domain rules into executable neural structure instead of sprinkling "physics-informed" language over a black box. It targets control-conditioned multi-event physical systems where macroscopic state evolves through many local discrete events, using a 3D KMC plasma-etching benchmark with explicit event rules. The model compiles rule attributes into event intensities, event-to-mechanism attribution, and state-dependent responses, then trains only on one-step state-transition loss. Across 100-step autoregressive rollouts, it reduces Avg. RMSE by 63.3% under control interpolation, 58.9% under joint control extrapolation, and 81.3% under temporal extrapolation versus the strongest standard baselines. Prior-feature baselines still do not close the gap, which supports the main claim: executable organization matters more than merely handing the network rule-derived features.

### 4. **DyMD: Preserving Interaction Dynamics through Distribution Matching Distillation in Few-Step Video World Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct embodied-video distillation paper.

Why it matters: DyMD catches a specific distillation pathology: a four-step video student can stay visually sharp while suppressing the robot-object motion that made the teacher useful. The paper diagnoses both sides of DMD. Weak re-noising leaves teacher guidance stuck near motion-deficient rollouts, while stronger-motion samples are harder for the fake-score critic to fit. DyMD fixes this with temporal affinity-conditioned re-noise sampling and dynamics-guided fake-score tracking, both training-only. Distilling a 14B PF-Wan teacher into a four-step 1.3B student raises R-Bench task adherence from 34.6 to 44.2, PAI-Bench-G Domain from 74.4 to 79.5, and downstream WorldArena planning success from 16% to 34%. The caveat is that the turning profile is teacher/schedule specific, but the failure mode is real and widely relevant: distillation can preserve image quality while amputating dynamics.

### 5. **GraphWrit3R: End-to-End 3D Scene Graph Writing**

**Verdict:** useful direct 3D structure paper.

Why it matters: GraphWrit3R is less fundamental than the top four, but it is a useful structure paper because it makes the output explicit: a JSON scene graph with objects, attributes, and relationships. Instead of a multi-stage pipeline that assumes ground-truth boxes or calls proprietary models, it encodes point clouds with Sonata, Gaussian splats with Chorus, aligns co-located voxel features with an asymmetric per-voxel contrastive loss, and decodes the graph with a small local LLM. On 3DSSG and ScanNet-style evaluation it reports state-of-the-art recall while jointly detecting objects and relations without ground-truth nodes at inference. The limitations are real: indoor reconstructed scenes, benchmark subgraphs that are smaller than full scenes, and truncation around very dense JSON outputs. Still, it is a good candidate pattern for turning 3D perception into an inspectable relational state.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**OneWorld** matters because it separates single-rollout physical plausibility from cross-intervention physical consistency.

**FuseReg** matters because it reframes layer fusion in RAEs as a robustness problem for downstream consumers, not a fixed encoder-design choice.

**NEMSim** matters because it compiles event-rule priors into executable transition structure and shows that rule access alone is not enough.

**DyMD** matters because it diagnoses motion loss in DMD-style few-step video distillation and allocates teacher/critic effort based on interaction fidelity.

**GraphWrit3R** matters because it generates an explicit 3D relational state directly, locally, and without assuming ground-truth object annotations at inference.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 28 pattern is compatibility pressure. A world model should make several interventions compatible with the same latent physics. A representation autoencoder should let its decoder and generator survive different layer compositions instead of pretending one fusion is globally optimal. A physical surrogate should execute event-mechanism rules, not just ingest rule features. A distilled video model should preserve interaction dynamics, not only visual quality. A 3D perception model should output structured relational state that can be inspected and queried. The shared lesson is simple and harsh: plausible local output is cheap; preserving the hidden structure that makes outputs jointly usable is the actual work.
