# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-27

## Theme

The worthwhile papers today all refuse to let one surface-level success metric hide the thing that actually matters. **Tunable Tool-Call Rates in LLM Agents via Representation Steering** separates whether to call a tool from which tool to call. **Learning What to Share and What to Personalize** separates universal memory rules from persona-specific ones. **Where vs What** separates structural placement from value recall. **ICON Decomposition** separates genuine concept use from correlated concept theater. **Auditable CT Phenotyping** separates phenotype accuracy from clinically valid evidence.

## Short overview

This Thursday, August 27, 2026 run inspected full arXiv HTML text for all five ranked papers below. The batch is stronger on explicit control and audit than on raw capability chasing. Three papers are direct cabbageland hits: tool-use control, personalized memory management, and structured-output failure diagnosis. Two are adjacent but preserve-worthy because they show how to audit representation claims instead of trusting headline performance. **Most relevant today:** **Tunable Tool-Call Rates in LLM Agents via Representation Steering**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Tunable Tool-Call Rates in LLM Agents via Representation Steering**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest paper in the batch because it finds a real control knob rather than another prompt superstition. A single residual-stream direction, extracted with no training from the log-probability of the shared `<tool_call>` opener token, can move tool-call rates from near zero to almost always while preserving valid tool syntax over a useful operating range. The result is not just interpretability garnish. On PopQA, the main model goes from **0.29** accuracy without search to **0.56** at about **1.1** searches per question, and the same direction transfers across dense, MoE, and multimodal models. Better still, the held-out tool test is severe: a direction learned from search, calculator, and Python still suppresses six unseen tools and beats tool-specific directions on **5 of 6**.

### 2. **Learning What to Share and What to Personalize: Hierarchical Strategy Co-Evolution for Agent Memory**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper earns a kept note because it attacks a real memory-policy question that most memory agents flatten away. Some memory rules should be shared across users, some should stay persona-specific, and the boundary should move as on-policy evidence arrives. HiPS makes that boundary explicit with a universal strategy, a persona delta, a divergence gate, and cross-level rule flow that can promote a local rule upward or specialize a broken universal rule downward. On the main benchmark table it is not a tiny win: PersonaMem **128K** rises to **62.01** versus **55.37** for MemSkill and **20.74** for raw long context, while the out-of-domain PERMA settings show why the flow mechanism matters at all.

### 3. **Where vs What: Decomposing Structural and Content Failures in LLM-Generated Structured Outputs**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest structured-output diagnosis paper in the batch because it identifies a concrete failure mode that exact-match and schema-validity benchmarks collapse into mush. Models often know the right values but bind them to the wrong addresses. The paper names that gap with Value Placement Accuracy and Displacement Rate, then shows it cleanly across both nested JSON and tables. At high JSON complexity, even strong frontier models still misplace **24-35%** of recalled values, and Qwen2.5-7B misplaces **74%**. The training follow-through is also useful: SA-RLVR pushes JSON VPA from **0.264** to **0.629**, while a matched SFT baseline only reaches **0.281**.

### 4. **ICON Decomposition: Multivariate Concept-Level Explanations of Deep Representations for Model Auditing**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent paper.

Why it matters: This paper is valuable because it attacks a familiar interpretability failure with more than a complaint. Standard concept probes and CAV-style analyses score concepts one at a time, so they confuse correlation with actual model reliance. ICON reframes the problem as multivariate variance decomposition over layer activations, concepts, and the outcome, and then reports both concept importance and unexplained variance. In the controlled skin-lesion shortcut test, ICON keeps false-positive artifact importance below **0.01** where baselines drift to **0.05-0.12**, and as shortcut corruption increases it tracks the microscope artifact from about **0.00** to **0.44** instead of barely moving.

### 5. **Auditable CT Phenotyping Through Report-derived Radiological Observations**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent paper.

Why it matters: This is the most useful medical-imaging paper in the batch because it does not stop at "better AUROC." ACT builds a report-derived observation bank that can be used for annotation, phenotype prediction, audit, and restriction inside one shared representation. The audit is the important part. Across **221** phenotypes, only **97** observations ever occupy the top rank, and a single calcification phrase ranks first for **20** clinically unrelated phenotypes. Restricting the bank to clinician-specified evidence redirects those probes and even improves the mean held-out AUROC across the **86** eligible phenotypes from **0.741** to **0.751**.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Tunable Tool-Call Rates in LLM Agents via Representation Steering**.

It is the strongest direct hit because it turns a vague agent behavior into an explicit, measurable, steerable object. Cabbageland keeps caring about tool-use policy, cost-awareness, and getting control without retraining a whole model. This paper gives a concrete pattern: read the model's own tool-call opener token, isolate a whether-to-call direction from mixed-tool activations, and use that direction as an inference-time dial.

The rest of the batch rhymes with the same standard. HiPS says memory policy should expose the boundary between global and local rules. Where vs What says structured-generation metrics should separate placement from recall. ICON says concept audits should separate genuine use from collinear clutter. ACT says clinical image models should expose named evidence rather than hiding behind phenotype AUROC.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Tunable Tool-Call Rates in LLM Agents via Representation Steering** matters because it separates tool-use propensity from tool choice and shows that the first one is a transferable control variable.

**Learning What to Share and What to Personalize** matters because it treats personalized memory policy as a dynamic partition problem rather than a static one-size-fits-all strategy.

**Where vs What** matters because it upgrades structured-output evaluation from binary pass/fail theater into a placement-aware decomposition that can also drive RL.

**ICON Decomposition** matters because it replaces one-concept-at-a-time probing with multivariate concept attribution plus an unexplained-variance term.

**Auditable CT Phenotyping** matters because it shows how a concept bank can be used not just to explain a model after the fact, but to redirect the evidence it is allowed to use.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 27 lesson is that many systems look better than they are because the benchmark asks the wrong collapsed question. Tool-use success hides whether the model knew when to call. Memory quality hides whether shared and user-specific rules were separated correctly. Structured-output accuracy hides whether the values landed in the right places. Concept audits hide whether a concept is genuinely used or merely correlated. Clinical phenotype AUROC hides whether the evidence is medically valid. The good papers today do not merely complain about that collapse. They name the missing variable and make it inspectable.
