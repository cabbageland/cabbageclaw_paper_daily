**Scale Buys Interpolation, Structure Buys a Horizon** is the strongest direct hit. The paper argues that an equivariant latent world model can certify how far a rollout remains trustworthy: exact equivariance makes rollout error constant over symmetry orbits, while approximate equivariance yields a finite per-channel horizon controlled by the latent predictor's Lyapunov spectrum. The best idea is the distinction between a universal audit and an a-priori guarantee. Any smooth latent loop can be spectrally audited, but exact structure is what makes the number trustworthy without a held-out divergence check. I did not independently verify the proofs, so the note preserves the framing and caveats rather than treating the theorem stack as settled law.

**Action-Effect Memory Pretraining for Robot Manipulation** is the best robot-memory note. AEM interleaves visual and action tokens, masks aligned action-effect timesteps, and trains a Mamba encoder to compress history into the encoded final vision token. That one vector is then concatenated with current perception inside Diffusion Policy or ManiFlow. The results are useful because the paper compares against no-history policies, direct DINOv2 feature stacking, randomized scenes, and memory-dependent tasks. The caveat is that the real-world result prose has inconsistent averages, so I trust the direction more than every exact margin.

**Learning What to Remember** is adjacent inspiration for long-running agents. Its useful point is methodological: if a retention policy scores memory relevance against the held-out future question, it is measuring retrieval with an oracle, not forgetting. In the blind consolidation regime, a learned multi-factor value over reliability, emotion, self/user relevance, and goal/session relevance beats recency and single-factor policies on LongMemEval gold-evidence retention. I am not preserving a separate note today because the evaluation is still retention-only, three of the seven factors are inert in the API-free setup, and final QA/task return is left for follow-up. Still, the oracle-vs-blind distinction is worth remembering.

**MoVerse** is the strongest 3D adjacent paper. It turns a single narrow-FOV image into a gravity-aligned panorama, lifts it into a persistent panoramic 3D Gaussian scaffold, and uses a distilled causal video renderer for interactive roaming. The representation lesson is excellent: keep long-range spatial memory in explicit geometry and let the video model repair local rendering quality. I am not preserving it today only because the paper is mostly a large staged system with qualitative-heavy evidence, while today's two kept papers are sharper for trust and robot-memory interfaces.

**TetherCache** is useful citation material for long-form video/world-model memory. Its sink/memory/recent cache split, attention-diversity recall, and trusted statistic alignment are sensible for drift-resistant autoregressive video. It is less central to cabbageland today because the mechanism is cache management for generation rather than embodied state or planning.

**ProPlay** is useful agent-memory/planning material. It builds a procedure graph with reliability records, preplays likely procedural trajectories, and updates the graph after environment feedback. The idea is aligned with procedural world models for LLM agents, but the abstraction remains natural-language procedural guidance over public text/game benchmarks, not a stronger direct hit than today's preserved papers.

**Bounding Boxes as Goals** is mostly citation material. It compiles language into bounding-box goal states and uses GroundingDINO plus closed-loop proportional control. The explicit goal-state interface is nice, but the evaluated robot task is still mostly grasp-and-lift; full end-to-end sorting and goal-similarity verification are left to future work.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Scale Buys Interpolation, Structure Buys a Horizon.

The steal-worthy idea is to make trust an object in the model interface. A world model used for planning should expose when it is calibrated, when it is optimistic, when it should abstain, and how quickly its rollout horizon decays under the demanded tolerance. If that number is backed by exact structure, it may be usable a priori. If it comes from a generic dense latent loop, it needs cross-validation.

AEM adds the practical robot-memory companion. The action history should not be a pile of frames for the policy to sift through. If the robot needs temporal state, pretrain a compact action-effect representation and feed it to the policy as a deliberate interface.

Learning What to Remember adds the agent-memory companion. Forgetting policies should be evaluated under the information available at consolidation time. Anything that scores against a future query is measuring retrieval, not memory survival.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Scale Buys Interpolation** raises the bar for world-model trust claims. Average error, model size, and plausible rollouts are not enough; a serious planner-facing model should report a horizon, an abstention region, or at least a cross-validated latent audit.

**AEM** raises the baseline for manipulation-memory papers. Direct history stacking and single-frame visual pretraining are weak baselines when the claimed problem is partial observability or task phase. A better baseline should compress action-conditioned history under a comparable latency budget.

**Learning What to Remember** raises a useful evaluation warning for agent memory. Query-defined gold evidence can create an oracle retrieval ceiling that hides whether the memory system made a good forgetting decision earlier.

**MoVerse** is useful for 3D world-model framing: explicit geometry should carry persistent spatial memory, while generative video handles perceptual repair. That is a cleaner decomposition than asking a video model to remember the whole world in hidden state.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best paper today is **Scale Buys Interpolation, Structure Buys a Horizon** because it gives world-model trust a shape: a per-situation horizon that structure can certify and generic latents must cross-check. **Action-Effect Memory Pretraining** is the robot-memory note to keep because it compresses vision-action history into a small pretrained state that downstream policies can actually use. **Learning What to Remember** is the agent-memory warning label: do not evaluate forgetting with future-query information. The common lesson is that long-horizon systems need explicit budget interfaces, whether the budget is rollout trust, history length, or memory survival.
