## Ranked papers

### 1. **Reinforcing Few-Step Diffusion Models via Shadow Posterior Matching**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow post-training and reward credit. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.25998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25998), Jingqiao Xiu and Yicong Li; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25998).
- **Mechanism:** Renoise deployed final samples into inputs for each of the four generator calls. A separately trained conditional fake-score model estimates the velocities of final samples and per-call outputs. Regress generator velocity toward a stop-gradient target combining reward-correlated residuals, a marginal-distribution teacher anchor, and endpoint matching that aligns shadow-input and deployed-input distributions. The reward supplies scalar values, not gradients or a fitted differentiable reward surrogate.
- **Why it matters:** A distilled generator's velocity is not automatically the forward velocity of its generated distribution. Treating it as one introduces a posterior-mean pull unrelated to reward. This paper separates reward direction, teacher anchoring, and input-distribution consistency rather than hiding all three inside one reweighted denoising loss.
- **Evidence:** One DMD2-distilled SD3.5-Medium backbone, four calls, three separately optimized tasks. Test-prompt GenEval reaches 0.94 versus TDM-R1's 0.86 and RTDMD's 0.77; OCR reaches 0.96 versus 0.95 and 0.92; PickScore reaches 23.84 versus 23.18 and 23.36. On OCR, independent DrawBench ImageReward is 1.14, versus 1.10 and 0.74. Removing endpoint matching lowers OCR to 0.95 and ImageReward to 1.06.
- **Catch:** The posterior reward-gradient identity is exact only under stated affine-reward or Gaussian-posterior conditions. Scalar precision normalization has further symmetry assumptions; learned scores, clipping, and approximate endpoint matching add approximation. This is not an unbiased general black-box gradient theorem. At teacher-anchor weight 0.01, OCR rises to 0.98 while ImageReward collapses to -1.50: reward hacking is not solved. One backbone, one step count, no reported multi-seed uncertainty in the headline table, and proxy quality metrics limit generality. Four inference calls do not imply cheap training.
- **Read scope:** Full main paper, including setup, equations, algorithm, Table 2, and ablations; reference list not independently audited. PDF-based note, no code execution or result reproduction. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/shadow_posterior_matching_few_step_rl.md).

### 2. **VoxForge: A Unified Foundation Model for Dense 3D Features Across MRI, CT and PET**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical/neuro representation learning and foundation-model evaluation. **Verdict:** Highly relevant follow-up; lighter recommendation today.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.25309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25309), Rushil Anirudh and Jayaraman J. Thiagarajan; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25309).
- **Mechanism:** A roughly 388M-parameter Primus-derived 3D ViT uses 8-by-8-by-8 voxel tokens, DINO/iBOT self-distillation, KoLeo spreading, and Gram anchoring. Train MRI first, increase crop resolution, then mix MRI/CT before CT-only continuation. Intensity augmentation encourages anatomy rather than raw contrast. Freeze the encoder for all downstream comparisons, training task heads or using head-free retrieval/anomaly readouts.
- **Why it matters:** Reusability should survive freezing. The stronger experiment is whether one feature space supports local anatomy, patient-level labels, and a modality absent from pretraining, not whether separate fine-tuned copies eventually win. Exposure audits, head-free probes, and random-weight floors are useful baseline design.
- **Evidence:** About 235K MRI/CT volumes, about 71K within-collection subjects, no PET pretraining; nine MRI and seven CT segmentation benchmarks plus classification, correspondence, and registration. On five-fold autoPET evaluation, lesion-positive Dice is 69.7 versus nnF-CNN's 66.6 and SPECTRE's 66.0; lesion F1 is 59.1 versus 39.6 and 53.3. A random-weight VoxForge encoder with a trained head still gets 55.2 Dice. The training schedule uses 216 hours on 64 H200 GPUs.
- **Catch:** Unseen PET means unseen in encoder pretraining, not label-free PET adaptation: PET-specific supervised heads and input mappings are fitted using training studies. The PET protocol removes 22 lesions wholly inside blanked end slices from the reference for every encoder. Stage comparisons confound data, duration, and resolution; ingredient-isolating ablations remain future work. Public adult data and frozen-head results do not establish clinical deployment or end-to-end superiority. No downloadable code/weights release was verified in this run.
- **Read scope:** Main recipe, frozen/exposure-aware protocol, selected classification/segmentation findings, analysis and limitations; supplementary PET protocol and Tables S17-S18. Not every appendix result or exposure record audited. No detailed note today.

### 3. **φTD: Distributional Reinforcement Learning using Characteristic Functions**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant non-agent RL, uncertainty representation, and operator-aligned learning. **Verdict:** Useful transferable formulation; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.25208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25208), Thomas Mousseau, Tyler Kastner, Davide Baldelli, and Amir-massoud Farahmand; submitted November 18; NeurIPS 2026 according to the manuscript; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25208).
- **Mechanism:** Represent a return law with a family having a differentiable closed-form characteristic function. For a sampled transition, compare the current function at frequency omega with exp(i omega reward) times the stop-gradient next-state function at gamma omega. Optimize squared complex residuals over sampled frequencies. Reward translation and discount scaling become multiplication and frequency rescaling; Gaussian mixtures and jointly learned atom locations/weights can use the same loss interface.
- **Why it matters:** Change coordinates so the Bellman operation becomes simple, then make distribution-family choice explicit. This removes the need to derive a bespoke spatial projection for every family; it does not remove approximation or estimation error.
- **Evidence:** Controlled four-action contextual bandits, four MinAtar games, and Craftax, with five seeds in the principal experiments. For Gaussian-mixture bandit targets, reported Cramer squared distance is 0.0086 for φTD-quantile versus QTD's 0.0388. A matched-backbone DQN comparison uses 10M steps per game: Gaussian-mixture φTD gets 104.59 on Breakout versus QR-DQN's 108.38, with overlapping intervals. The deep-RL case is competitive flexibility, not universal return superiority.
- **Catch:** Population Cramer-distance equivalence depends on frequency weighting; practical Pareto sampling truncates low frequencies, while weighted exponential sampling changes the effective objective. The control runs use the latter. Wider/nonconvex families can trade lower approximation error for higher estimation error, and general convergence remains open. Modeling return distributions does not by itself validate tail calibration or safety.
- **Read scope:** Main motivation and parametric-family setup, empirical loss/frequency choices, controlled experiment, deep-RL discussion and limitations; supplementary matched-backbone DQN protocol/Table 13. Full proof appendix and all sensitivity/risk experiments not audited. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Sequential Anytime Valid Tests for Efficient Inference-Time Alignment**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant uncertainty, adaptive computation, and verification; adjacent inspiration beyond language models. **Verdict:** Useful guarantee with an important target boundary; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.26413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26413), Vishesh Kumar Singh and Sayak Ray Chowdhury; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26413).
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a separate betting e-process for each observed final-answer cluster. Assign first-appearance rank j weight 1/[j(j+1)], and stop when a weighted process crosses 1/alpha. Reward-weighted self-consistency determines predictable bets, not the event being certified. A new answer's first occurrence initializes its process; betting starts on subsequent samples. Budget exhaustion returns an uncertified aggregation fallback.
- **Why it matters:** Separate an untrusted efficiency signal from the statistical claim. Reward-model errors can make bets inefficient without invalidating the majority-dominance test under its sampling assumptions. Optional stopping and changing answer leaders must be handled explicitly, not patched with ordinary Wald thresholds.
- **Evidence:** Math, knowledge, and code-reasoning tasks. On MATH500 with Qwen2.5-3B, ArmoRM, and a 24-candidate cap, e-RWSC reports 74.4% accuracy at 15.59 average candidates versus vanilla Best-of-N's 71.0% at 24. ConSol reports 75.4% at 8.02 in the same table, so the method is not the empirical accuracy/cost winner in every setting. Its defensible differentiator is the anytime-valid dominance certificate.
- **Catch:** The certified event is that an answer has more than half the fixed generator's probability mass, not that it is correct. A confidently wrong generator can pass. Validity assumes independent fixed-policy samples, stable answer extraction, predictable bounded bets, and valid error-budget allocation. Fallback answers receive no certificate; candidate reductions are not automatically token or wall-clock reductions. The empirical error audit uses a finite 512-sample estimate of dominance, not ground-truth probabilities.
- **Read scope:** Main setup, per-answer test and stopping equations, guarantee/assumptions discussion, selected cross-model/cross-reward tables; algorithm/fallback and empirical audit descriptions. Full proof and all latency tables not exhaustively audited. No detailed note today.

### 5. **World-δ: Continually Integrating Observations for Incremental 3D Scene Generation**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant explicit scene state and controllable 3D generation; adjacent inspiration for real-to-sim reconstruction. **Verdict:** Useful design, provisional empirical confidence; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.25802](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25802), Haonan Wang, Hanyu Zhou, Xiaobin Hu, and Luxin Yan; submitted November 19; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25802).
- **Mechanism:** Store scene latents, topology/evidence, provenance, observational confidence, and generative uncertainty separately. Assign KEEP/FUSE/REVISE/EXTEND edits when RGB evidence arrives. Masked flow matching can write only selected latents; unaffected history is hard-frozen. Decode affected Gaussian chunks with a read-only two-cell halo matching the decoder's convolutional dependency radius. Generation explicitly adds no observational support.
- **Why it matters:** Generated completion is a hypothesis, not a new measurement. Provenance must survive generation, and update ownership must be distinguished from read context. The decoder-halo argument is more concrete than simply calling the scene representation a world model.
- **Evidence:** Continual updates on ScanNet-v2/Replica, plus single-image generation tests. In the reported ScanNet-v2 component ablation, selective decoding takes 5.2 seconds per update versus 16.7 for full decoding, with identical reported LPIPS 0.192, correction gain 0.081, and retention drift 0.008. Global diffusion gets slightly better LPIPS 0.189 but drift 0.042. These are paper-reported ablations, not reproduced timings.
- **Catch:** Static-scene assumptions make motion look like a bad prior completion. Confidence/uncertainty are learned estimates, not demonstrated statistical calibration guarantees. The inspected main text does not make all state-action supervision construction, sample counts, or run variance sufficiently transparent to treat the results as settled. Fifty Euler steps, frozen evidence backbones, and multistage training also complicate efficiency attribution. Scene completion is not action-conditioned dynamics or validated real2sim2real policy transfer.
- **Read scope:** Main state/update equations, objectives, implementation, selected correction/retention and decoding ablations, and limitations. All dataset construction details and baseline implementations not audited; no code reproduction or detailed note today.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Few-step post-training:** Compare final-call credit, teacher anchor, and shadow/deployed distribution alignment separately. Match initialization, inference calls, rollout budget, fake-score capacity, and training cost. Test precision approximations and weak-anchor collapse; do not market a conditional identity as an exact universal gradient.
- **Medical foundation models:** Freeze first, audit pretraining exposure, then distinguish trained-head, head-free, and genuinely unseen-modality evaluation. Include random-weight encoders, PET preprocessing/label changes, and matched compute before crediting an MRI-first curriculum.
- **Distributional RL:** Match parameter budget as well as atom count. Separate distribution-fit error, mean return, tail calibration, and optimization stability. State which sampled-frequency objective was actually used.
- **Inference-time verification:** Compare certified and uncertified outputs separately. Keep a strong efficient heuristic such as ConSol in the comparison; a statistical guarantee does not automatically imply the best accuracy/cost frontier. Report wrong-majority and no-majority cases.
- **Scene memory:** Test provenance, reliability estimates, mask selection, hard freezing, and decoder context independently. A small writable region must not imply a small readable region, and generated content must never promote itself into evidence.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The reusable idea is to define the object before optimizing it. A four-step sampler is not its distribution's score; a frozen representation is not a collection of fine-tuned models; a return law is not just its mean; a dominant answer is not a verified truth; a generated room is not an observation. Better coordinates and interfaces help, but the boundaries carry the scientific content. Keep the approximations, preprocessing, fallback behavior, and counterexamples visible rather than letting a good headline erase them.
