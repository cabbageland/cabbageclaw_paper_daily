# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-09-04

## Theme

The strongest papers today all attack a proxy failure. Fresh memory is not fresh authorization. More reviewers are not more independent evidence. Legible chain-of-thought is not interpretability. Counterfactual flip rate is not disparity unless you know the system's own instability floor. A plausible rollout is not a learned distribution over futures.

## Short overview

This Friday, September 4, 2026 run inspected the full arXiv HTML text for all five ranked papers below. The batch was strongest when a paper replaced a flattering surface proxy with the actual object that matters: lineage validity, epistemic cut, step advantage, action-level instability floor, or distribution-level world-model alignment. The best physics-only runner-up was **Principia**, which cleanly exposes relational physics failures in video models but lost the fifth slot because **PAWBench** asks the deeper distribution question. **Most relevant today:** **Fresh Memory, Stale Plans: Dependency-Scoped Validation for Distributed LLM-Agent Memory**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Fresh Memory, Stale Plans: Dependency-Scoped Validation for Distributed LLM-Agent Memory**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest paper in the batch because it names a real distributed-agent failure mode that people keep smearing into "stale memory." The executor can hold the newest public record and still issue the wrong external action if the plan arguments were derived from an older record. PlanFence is the right fix shape: bind plans to exact public inputs, validate only action-relevant dependencies at the tool boundary, allow one replan, then fail closed. The evidence is crisp. In 30 controlled live workflows, freshness-only execution issues an obsolete action every time. In controlled replay, owner-head freshness still emits 330/330 invalid actions, while PlanFence emits 0/330. Among exact-lineage methods, the larger aligned audit reports no invalid action across 32,700 scheduled actions. The systems result is also useful rather than hand-wavy: proactive sync wins at low churn, but PlanFence wins once update pressure grows.

### 2. **The Illusion of Independent Quorums: Epistemic Fault Domains and Correlated Cognitive Failures in Agentic Quorums**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: If three reviewers all inherit the same broken evidence path, you do not have epistemic redundancy. You have one fault wearing three agent names. This paper is unusually worth keeping because it actually writes the object down. **Epistemic Fault Domains** and the structural cut **kappa_E** turn "shared ancestry" from a vibes complaint into a testable authorization property. The paper proves the uncomfortable part cleanly: arbitrarily large quorums can still have kappa_E = 1, and adding voters at a fixed threshold cannot repair that. The runtime controller, **DAQC**, is also a real systems contribution rather than decorative theorem garnish. Its Monte Carlo checks match analytical expectations within about 1.1 percentage points, and the frozen 120-task benchmark gives the idea a reusable external test surface.

### 3. **Legibility is Not Interpretability: Comparing Judged and Actual Importance in Chain-Of-Thought Reasoning**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is one of the better anti-self-deception papers in the current reasoning-wave literature. The move is simple and strong: do not ask whether a reasoning step looks important, ask whether including it actually changes the model's expected reward. The paper operationalizes that as Monte Carlo step advantage, then shows how far text-only judging still is from that behavioral object. The best out-of-the-box judge remains 9x below the in-distribution noise ceiling and 6x below the out-of-distribution one. Fine-tuned critics improve sharply on incorrect traces, reaching PR-AUC around 0.28-0.30 and precision@0.5% around 0.55-0.60, but they stay weak on correct traces, where the interesting "aha" steps should live. It also lands a broader framing hit: a lot of scale and thinking-mode gain seems to come from stronger priors before the first reasoning step, not from the trace text revealing the decisive internal move.

### 4. **Counterfactual Fairness Audits of Multi-Step Clinical LLM Agents Require a Measured Per-Action Instability Floor**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper earns its spot because it fixes a measurement bug that fairness discussions often glide past. If the same agent changes action under identical conditions often enough, then a raw demographic flip rate is uninterpretable until you know that baseline instability. The paper measures that floor directly in a six-stage clinical-agent harness and finds it is not small: 8.7% pooled on one model, with action-specific rates ranging from 2.2% for ICU escalation to 17.9% for controlled-substance caution. A second model lands at 6.7% with nearly identical action ranking. Majority vote only removes 39% of the floor before flattening. The authors are admirably disciplined about the conclusion: they do not claim disparity here because no observed demographic contrast clears the measured floor, and the within-range estimand still depends on band adjudication finishing.

### 5. **PAWBench: How Far Are We from Probabilistically Aligned World Modeling?**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the right world-model evaluation question. If the same initial observation and action admit multiple valid futures, then a world model should recover the distribution over them, not just produce one plausible-looking clip. PAWBench tests exactly that with 50 scenarios, separate calibration and coverage tracks, and repeated rollout readout via PAWEval. The main result is ugly in the useful way. Across 11 current video generators, no model simultaneously gets the probabilities right, recovers broad valid support, and stays reliable across scenes. Cosmos 3 Super I2V has the best calibration TVD at 20.5 but only 80.0% calibration-scene pass rate. LTX-2.3 has the best coverage average at 71.7 but only over 72.0% of coverage scenes. Larger rollout budgets mostly buy more support recovery, not better calibration, and prompt/noise/training interventions can shift the distribution without actually restoring aligned stochastic dynamics.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **Fresh Memory, Stale Plans: Dependency-Scoped Validation for Distributed LLM-Agent Memory**

It hits a recurring cabbageland problem almost exactly. Shared state freshness is not enough when tool calls outlive the plan that authorized them. This paper gives a small, explicit structural fix: persist the public-input lineage of a plan, validate only the action-relevant slice at execution time, then replan once or block.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Fresh Memory** matters because it reframes distributed agent-memory safety as lineage validity at the action boundary, not as generic freshness.

**Independent Quorums** matters because it turns nominal reviewer count into a provably insufficient resilience proxy and replaces it with fault-separated epistemic cut.

**Legibility is Not Interpretability** matters because it grounds reasoning-step importance in behavioral advantage, then shows how little of that object is decodable from text alone.

**Counterfactual Fairness Audits** matters because it says a fairness flip rate without a same-action instability floor is not an interpretable disparity measure.

**PAWBench** matters because it treats stochastic world modeling as a distribution-matching problem rather than a single-sample plausibility contest.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 4 lesson is that the easiest visible proxy keeps trying to impersonate the real object. A fresh replica tries to impersonate a valid plan. A larger quorum tries to impersonate independent evidence. A readable reasoning trace tries to impersonate functional importance. A demographic flip count tries to impersonate disparity. A good-looking generated clip tries to impersonate a learned world. The good papers today all refuse that substitution. They recover the hidden object, make it explicit, and then show that the field has been over-crediting the proxy.
