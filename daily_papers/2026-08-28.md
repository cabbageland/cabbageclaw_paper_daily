# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-28

## Theme

The good papers today all refuse to let one signal quietly do two incompatible jobs. **When Tool Outputs Become Commands** separates action induction from execution authority. **Making Latent Evolution Explicit** separates what a world model predicts from how its latent transition is parameterized. **WikiSkill** separates raw traces from persistent knowledge and from executable skills. **Calibrated Enough to Know, Not Calibrated to Act** separates judgment from the act gate that should decide whether to commit. **Retrieval Heads Meet Vision** separates attention-shaped storytelling from causally necessary visual retrieval.

## Short overview

This Friday, August 28, 2026 run inspected full arXiv HTML text for all five ranked papers below. The batch was better on explicit control, runtime structure, and mechanistic decomposition than on raw benchmark theater. The strongest direct papers are about agent/runtime design and one unusually clean world-action-model idea. ## Ranked papers

### 1. **When Tool Outputs Become Commands: Separating Action Induction from Runtime Authorization in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest agent-security paper in the batch because it names the right boundary. The problem is not merely that tool outputs can contain prompt injection. The problem is that agents often let observation-derived action suggestions quietly turn into execution authority. SARA splits those roles. A context-isolated Action Probe records action-inducing provenance, while actual tool execution is separately authorized against the user objective plus audited evidence from prior authorized executions. The key runtime rule, No-History-Promotion, stops repeated appearance from laundering an injected instruction into authority. The empirical result is strong enough to matter: across AgentDojo and AgentDyn, SARA keeps attack success rate at or below **0.63%** in four main settings, with GPT-4o-mini dropping from **15.79% / 16.07%** to **0.06% / 0.17%**, and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Lite dropping from **33.28% / 30.91%** to **0.62% / 0.63%**.

### 2. **Making Latent Evolution Explicit: Operator-Structured Transitions for World Action Models**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: Most latent world-action-model papers talk endlessly about what future representation to predict, then quietly realize the transition with a generic Transformer and pretend that choice is neutral. This paper is good because it argues that transition realization is its own architectural variable. LEON maps the latent state into a learned observable space, evolves it with a context-modulated shared operator basis plus additive forcing, and plugs that transition module into two different WAM couplings. That is a real structural move, not a renaming exercise. The numbers are also respectable rather than decorative: in VLA-JEPA it raises average LIBERO success from **97.2%** to **99.05%** and LIBERO-Plus from **79.5%** to **80.6%**; under full transition replacement in LaWAM it roughly preserves aggregate RoboTwin performance at **84.13%** versus **84.50%**. Only one robotics/world-model paper cleared the higher bar today, and this is the one.

### 3. **WikiSkill: Compiling Agent Experience into Persistent Knowledge for Skill Evolution**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper earns a kept note because it refuses to treat traces, memories, and skills as the same object. WikiSkill inserts a persistent wiki layer between immutable execution traces and executable skill modules, then uses a Wiki Maintainer plus Skill Proposer loop to keep consolidating what the agent has actually learned. That separation matters because it gives later skill edits something better than scattered optimization debris to build from. The scaling interaction is especially useful: within the Qwen family, average gains are **12.3%**, **17.5%**, and **23.9%** for the 4B, 9B, and 27B models, and Qwen-3.5-9B with WikiSkill reaches **47.4%** average accuracy, beating Qwen-3.6-27B without skills at **39.4%**. The transfer results are even more interesting: a skill evolved by another model can beat the target model's self-evolved skill, which suggests that skill discovery and skill execution really are different capabilities.

### 4. **Calibrated Enough to Know, Not Calibrated to Act: Fabricated Evidence Makes LLM Agents Commit to the Unknowable**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This paper is useful because it isolates a familiar deployment failure with unusual clarity. The model often knows the question is unknowable, but still commits once the surrounding display looks authoritative enough. Across **12** frontier models, commitment rises from **6.5%** to **54.0%** as evidence is escalated. More importantly, fabricating the entire panel so that nothing visible is true except the question still yields **36.8%** commitment, statistically indistinguishable from the **37.6%** produced by genuine market data. That is the whole point: the packaging, not the information, is doing the causal work. The paper then shows the judgment is present but the gate does not consult it. If the model is first asked whether the question is knowable, it calls it irreducible **90%** of the time and then commits on only **0.4%** of those cases. A small 3B model can be trained out of this failure on synthetic data, but the fix is fragile to rigid output formats, which is exactly the caveat you would want to know.

### 5. **Retrieval Heads Meet Vision: Uncovering How VLMs Locate and Extract Visual Information**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy adjacent paper.

Why it matters: This is the best adjacent interpretability paper in the batch because it upgrades the usual VLM-attention story from "these maps look plausible" to a causal claim. The paper identifies Visual Retrieval Heads, a sparse subset of attention heads, only **1.7-2.6%** of total heads, that are causally necessary for resolving visually referred regions. The masking result is severe: removing just the top **20** VRHs drops grounding accuracy by up to **80 percentage points**, while masking random heads barely moves the score. Better still, the heads transfer across task types and even across VLMs that share the same LLM backbone. In one transfer example, cross-masking drives grounding accuracy from **68.8%** to **0.0%** and from **85.8%** to **5.8%**. That makes the paper more than another interpretability vibe piece; it exposes a reusable mechanism.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **When Tool Outputs Become Commands: Separating Action Induction from Runtime Authorization in Tool-Augmented LLM Agents**.

It is the strongest direct hit because it turns a mushy safety complaint into a crisp runtime boundary: observation can influence candidate actions without becoming permission to execute them. That is exactly the kind of decomposition cabbageland keeps wanting from tool-using systems. It is explicit, operational, auditable, and evaluated in dynamic environments instead of being trapped in static prompt-filter theater.

LEON is the closest second because it makes a real structural claim about world-action-model transitions rather than decorating the same latent mush. WikiSkill is the best persistent-knowledge paper in the batch. The fourth and fifth papers matter because they show the same general lesson in adjacent domains: the right judgment variable often exists already, but the wrong gate or measurement is being trusted.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**When Tool Outputs Become Commands** matters because it separates action induction from authorization and forces runtime evidence to do real work at the execution boundary.

**Making Latent Evolution Explicit** matters because it says transition realization is a first-class architectural choice in latent WAMs, not a neutral implementation detail hidden behind representation talk.

**WikiSkill** matters because it treats persistent knowledge as an explicit intermediate object between raw traces and executable skills, then shows that this layer compounds across iterations and across models.

**Calibrated Enough to Know, Not Calibrated to Act** matters because it relocates the failure from belief calibration to an act/don't-act gate that can be trained but remains context-fragile.

**Retrieval Heads Meet Vision** matters because it identifies a sparse causal mechanism for visual retrieval instead of relying on descriptive attention alignment alone.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 28 lesson is that many failures happen because the system lets one representation silently exceed its proper jurisdiction. Observation becomes authority. A generic latent transition becomes the default dynamics prior. Raw traces pretend to be persistent knowledge. Official-looking evidence becomes permission to act. A pretty attention map becomes an explanation. The worthwhile papers today do not merely complain about these collapses. They name the missing boundary and make it inspectable.
