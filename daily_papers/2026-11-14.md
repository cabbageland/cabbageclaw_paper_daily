## Theme

Specify what the constraint actually certifies. A feasible sample need not follow the learned physics; a connected assembly need not be collision-free; a passed test need not establish correctness. The strongest mechanisms separate those jobs instead of hiding them inside a bigger model.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Manifold Attracting Flow Matching: Physics-Constrained Sampling via Continuous Geometric Dynamics**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant flow matching, scientific generation, explicit state, and constrained sampling. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16677), November 12, 2026; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16677).
- **Mechanism:** Maintain a persistent candidate endpoint, not just a transient noisy state. Track frozen-model endpoint estimates, optionally backpropagate an observation mismatch through the frozen network, then solve a constraint-Jacobian multiplier system. This retains the candidate velocity's tangent component while replacing its normal component with prescribed residual contraction. Rebuild the next model query by interpolating the original noise and updated anchor.
- **Why it matters:** Persistent endpoint state separates useful generative history from hard feasibility control. The sampler can change an unobserved field through model sensitivities while independently keeping measured coordinates correct. This is a concrete alternative to repeatedly replacing the endpoint with a newly projected prediction; its claim is feasible, physically consistent generation, not automatically correct conditional sampling.
- **Evidence:** With the same frozen models and paired sources, the 64-sensor Poisson test gives 1.70% PDE-consistency error versus the best feasible baseline's 5.55%; terminal projection gives 22.7%. Feasible own-initial-condition rollout errors are 0.965% for Burgers and 2.74% for reaction-diffusion, versus next-best feasible 1.03% and 2.96%. Removing Poisson's adjoint raises PDE error to 38.0% without changing observation feasibility. A Burgers intervention that replaces only tangent history raises rollout error from 0.965% to 7.68% at comparable mass residuals.
- **Catch:** Exact exponential residual contraction needs full-row-rank Jacobians, exact unregularized solves, and continuous dynamics; finite steps add curvature error. Relaxation's stability result is local and frozen-time, not universal. One training seed, synthetic systems, simple sensor/mass/energy constraints, and task-specific guidance limit generality. Shifted energy targets need terminal projection to meet tolerance. Distributional fidelity is not uniformly best: reaction-diffusion q95 error is slightly worse than ECI despite better mean error. No guarantee of sampling the desired probability law on the feasible intersection. Input VJPs and dense constraint solves can be expensive.
- **Read scope:** Full-text main formulation, algorithm, propositions, main comparisons, ablations, and conclusion; selected appendices on endpoint regression, target-law ambiguity, local stability, model/data definitions, projection audits, tails, and timing. Not an exhaustive proof or artifact audit. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/mafm_persistent_anchor_constrained_flow_matching.md).

### 2. **Physics-based simulation unlocks scaling laws for generalizable MRI reconstruction**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant medical inverse problems, representation learning, and transferable simulation design. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; next preservation candidate, lighter recommendation today.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.15758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15758), November 11, 2026; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15758).
- **Mechanism:** Turn natural-image channels into complex-valued objects; randomize phase, magnitude, coil profiles, noise, and sampling masks through the multi-coil Fourier forward model. A shared multi-channel ViT predicts residual corrections to each aliased coil image independently, then combines them with root-sum-of-squares. Train with image MAE. Distinguish simulation-only SIM-zero from SIM, which mixes simulated and real fastMRI data.
- **Why it matters:** The simulation need not reproduce anatomy if it teaches the right inverse operator over sufficiently diverse signals and acquisition conditions. Phase, magnitude/SNR, coil, and mask coverage are actionable design choices. This is more useful than the blanket claim that synthetic data or a generic transformer eliminates the need for physical priors: the forward operator and output combination still encode physics explicitly.
- **Evidence:** Evaluation spans 14 datasets, seven centers, five vendors, and 0.3-7 T. At effective 3.2x acceleration, the paper reports OOD gains over the second-best baseline of 1.06-3.95 dB PSNR and 0.008-0.045 SSIM. A blinded two-radiologist study covers 50 subjects with three contrasts, hence 150 scans, not 150 independent patients. SIM reaches 100% diagnostic-usability ratings at 3.2x in this cohort. Benchmark and clinical models use about 510M and 1.01B parameters respectively; the latter mixes about 1.01M simulated samples with 70,748 real samples.
- **Catch:** Retrospective undersampling, a motion-screened single-center reader cohort, and reference-assisted image-quality ratings are not prospective diagnostic accuracy. The present model targets magnitude-only, slice-wise reconstruction, not unrestricted 3D/complex imaging. Training must cover the evaluated mask geometries; omitted masks degrade badly. Attention-map alignment is suggestive, not causal proof of a learned physical algorithm. Baselines differ in target/mask protocols and some untrained methods use 500-slice subsets; do not read the headline as a fully matched compute/data comparison. Code and weights were not independently verified in this run.
- **Read scope:** Full-text simulation, architecture, MAE objective, scaling setup, zero-shot comparisons, clinical study, attention analysis, and discussion; selected baseline/protocol passages. Not every supplementary result, clinical image, or released artifact. No detailed note today.

### 3. **BrickFlow: Connectivity-Guided Brick Reconstruction**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant compositional 3D generation and analytic guidance; adjacent to physical assembly, not a demonstrated robot controller. **Verdict:** Useful mechanism reference; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17321](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17321), November 12, 2026; NeurIPS 2026; [project](https://kulits.github.io/BrickFlow/).
- **Mechanism:** Pretrain a set-conditioned SE(3) flow model on 100M procedural 100-part assemblies, then finetune on 1M rendered structures with frozen DINOv3 image conditioning. Convert candidate connector seats into electrical conductances and penalize excessive effective resistance between parts. Apply late-step gradients through predicted clean poses to encourage global connectivity without prespecifying the connection graph.
- **Why it matters:** Effective resistance makes global connectedness into a differentiable signal over typed local interfaces. Pretraining learns a structural grammar; test-time analytic guidance repairs alignment. This separates pose resemblance from assembly validity and gives a reusable design pattern for continuous generation over discrete compatibility rules.
- **Evidence:** On 512 human-designed BrickNet structures, the large image-conditioned model improves validity from 30.5% to 35.1% with guidance; mass-movement error barely changes, 31.0 to 30.9 LDraw units. On procedural structures, validity rises from 13.4% to 15.8%. For the small model, training from scratch without the pretrained hand-off gives only 2.3% BrickNet validity versus 19.4% with pretraining. Evaluation draws 100 samples per structure and reports structure-bootstrap intervals; single-sample and best-of-many curves are distinct.
- **Catch:** The energy does not explicitly penalize collisions or guarantee validity. Single-sample validity falls below 1% beyond roughly 50 parts; even best-of-100 falls below 1% beyond roughly 85. The exact part inventory is supplied, with no missing/extra pieces. The large structural pretraining budget and altered rotational sampler matter; the pretraining ablation also removes the late hand-off. Real-image results are qualitative, and final connected geometry does not establish an executable assembly order or stable contact dynamics.
- **Read scope:** Full-text representation, procedural data, flow objective, architecture, resistance energy, sampling, evaluation, and limitations; not all implementation/supplementary details. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Consistent Video-to-Video Translation via Explicit Correspondences**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant video generation, explicit memory, and controllability; not generic agent memory. **Verdict:** Useful; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17298), November 12, 2026; ECCV 2026; [vid2vid-long project](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~vid2vid-long/).
- **Mechanism:** Store input-latent patches as keys and previously generated output patches as values. Retrieve three sufficiently similar neighbors per current patch and inject them through per-token correspondence cross-attention. Write only novel inputs to memory. Train the memory-augmented VACE student on its own generated history with the existing distillation objective, rather than adding a new consistency loss.
- **Why it matters:** The observed input stream supplies an address space for reusing generated appearance. Memory selection follows repeated content rather than temporal proximity, and retrieval bypasses full-history attention. The reusable lesson is to index by a stable observable and retain the generated consequence, not simply enlarge a KV cache.
- **Evidence:** With Rolling Forcing on VACE-Bench, adding correspondence memory lowers revisit LPIPS from 0.427 to 0.240 and DreamSim from 0.172 to 0.066; on DL3DV, LPIPS falls from 0.445 to 0.305. Gains also occur with Self-Forcing and LongLive, although quality does not improve in every configuration. On one H100, the two-step variant runs at 13.88 fps with 1.09-second first-frame latency; five-step generation gives 8.10 fps and 3.72 seconds.
- **Catch:** Evaluation deliberately creates revisits: ABAB concatenations of 100 clip pairs and 180 looping 3DGS-rendered sequences. This supports reuse, not arbitrary changing-world memory. Similar inputs can have different desired outputs under state, lighting, or prompt changes. Fast motion and conflicting text/depth fail. Novelty-gated storage is not a hard memory bound; continual novelty can keep growing it. Reported fps is below the datasets' native 16 fps, so “real-time” needs qualification. No unconditional text-to-video or physically validated world-model result follows.
- **Read scope:** Full-text memory read/write, conditioning, training/inference, main comparisons and limitations; selected dataset-construction and memory-growth appendices. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Negative-Only Policy Optimization for One-Sided Verifiable Rewards**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant RL post-training, partial verification, and optimization under asymmetric feedback. **Verdict:** Useful; lighter recommendation with substantial evaluation caveats.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17287](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17287), November 12, 2026; NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17287).
- **Mechanism:** Suppress verifier-rejected responses using reference-relative Negative Preference Optimization. Multiply each group's negative loss by its fraction of non-falsified responses, raised to an exponent. With the default exponent one, all-failed groups contribute nothing; accepted responses supply a support count but receive no direct positive loss. Reference-relative attenuation limits repeated suppression of the same negative.
- **Why it matters:** “Not falsified” is an unlabeled outcome, not a positive training target. Group support and sample-level self-attenuation address different failure modes of negative-only updates. The design is relevant when partial executable checks identify errors much more reliably than they certify success, but the required asymmetry must be measured rather than asserted for LLM judges.
- **Evidence:** On Qwen3-4B, average pass@16 across five code/logic/math settings is 52.86 versus GRPO's 48.93; on 8B it is 52.13 versus 48.27. Training on CodeContests yields unseen-target average pass@16 of 29.49 versus base 23.66 and GRPO 23.30. On 4B CodeContests, pass@512 reaches 66.95 versus base 51.05, but pass@1 is 26.87 versus GRPO 37.23. The 8B OlympiadBench result is below GRPO.
- **Catch:** Main-table training and evaluation use the same benchmark test prompts; hidden oracle answers are reserved for evaluation, but this is still transductive optimization, not ordinary held-out generalization. Cross-benchmark transfer is the cleaner evidence. Negative-dominated methods select checkpoints before observed entropy divergence, so the support gate does not eliminate instability. All-failed groups get no learning signal. Finite-budget newly solved problems do not prove the base model had zero probability of solving them. Judge false rejections, weak negative recall, and unknown failure modes remain important.
- **Read scope:** Full-text one-sidedness measurements, NPO/support-weight equations, principal results, transfer/scaling and ablation sections; selected appendix passages on shared test prompts, checkpoint selection, verifier protocols, and post-training noise. No detailed note today.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**MAFM.** Preserve the persistent anchor plus tangent/normal split, and preserve the boundary of the claim just as carefully. A tiny declared residual can coexist with poor dynamics or wrong conditional weights. Its most useful experiment controls feasibility while changing only tangent history: that is substantially sharper than comparing two differently trained “physics-aware” systems.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Constrained generation:** Separate numerical feasibility, learned physical support, distributional weights, and computational cost. Include terminal projection, PCFM/ECI, adjoint-on/off, tangent-history-only interventions, constraint-tail statistics, and matched wall-clock budgets. The ingredients are classical; the contribution is how the endpoint state and correction dynamics are coupled.
- **Medical simulation:** Compare SIM-zero and mixed-data SIM explicitly. Match scanner masks, noise, coil count, targets, parameter count, and compute. Keep reconstruction quality distinct from lesion preservation and diagnostic decisions. Attention pictures do not establish causal interpretability.
- **Compositional assembly:** Score whole-assembly connectivity and collision validity separately from pose error. Include inventory uncertainty, unseen connectors, larger assemblies, sampling cost, and an executable construction sequence before claiming embodied competence.
- **Generative memory:** Test naturally occurring revisits and changing-state counterexamples, not just repeated clips. Measure erroneous retrieval persistence, growth under continual novelty, fps, and first-frame latency separately.
- **RL with partial checks:** Report verifier false acceptance, false rejection, and negative recall before and after optimization. Use disjoint train/evaluation prompts, fixed checkpoint-selection rules, pass@1 through large k, and genuinely unseen tasks.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The keep is not “add constraints.” It is to give each requirement its own state, update, and evaluation: MAFM separates tangent history from normal feasibility; SIM separates object diversity from a known measurement operator; BrickFlow separates a learned assembly prior from connector guidance; vid2vid-long separates observable addresses from generated appearances; NOPO separates failure evidence from unlabeled success. Every separation leaves something uncertified. Keep that missing guarantee visible rather than laundering it into the method's name.
