## Ranked papers

### 1. **eDMD: Endpoint Distribution Matching for Few-Step Diffusion Distillation**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion/flow distillation and objective design. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17194](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17194), November 12, 2026. The paper links [eDMD code](https://github.com/Westlake-AGI-Lab/eDMD); no implementation was executed here.
- **Mechanism:** Fit the auxiliary fake denoiser only to the four-step student's final outputs, then use teacher-minus-fake corrections at every selected student step. Intermediate predictions still receive local gradients; the rollout is detached. Add short teacher integration only for the final step's low-noise queries and precompute an intermediate query distribution from teacher cross-level consistency.
- **Why it matters:** A critic can be well fit to the wrong reference. Mixing unfinished and final predictions introduces a reference residual: it can keep moving a correct final distribution or hide final errors behind compensating intermediate errors. The useful principle is to define the auxiliary model's target by the delivered artifact, without forcing internal states to look finished.
- **Evidence:** Four-step image students cover SD3-Medium, FLUX.2-klein-4B/9B, and Qwen-Image-2.1. On FLUX-4B, peak HPSv3++ is 12.699 versus DMD2's 11.337; removing the endpoint component lowers it to 11.095. Adding the endpoint fake to an otherwise retained TDM implementation improves peak scores by 0.61, 1.45, and 0.53 on SD3/FLUX-4B/FLUX-9B. On FLUX-4B, switching a trained student to a mixture fake loses 1.71 points within 100 updates; pre-fitting that fake does not rescue it.
- **Catch:** The zero-correction theorem assumes exact denoisers and, for teacher matching, an actual target-distribution denoiser. Guided and integrated teacher predictions do not automatically satisfy that condition. Local detached updates are not a proof of optimizing the full rollout's endpoint KL. Preference scores are learned proxies, not a new human trial; checkpoints are selected on HPSv3++, albeit with held-out prompt-selection checks. Some baselines are adapted/reimplemented, and DMD2 omits its GAN loss. Training is costlier per update: 17.6 versus 11.8 seconds for DMD2 on FLUX-4B, plus calibration. Applying the precise teacher everywhere collapses FLUX-4B. No video, action, or physical-world validation.
- **Read scope:** Full-text main equations, algorithm, tables, and ablations; selected appendix passages on theorem scope, TDM weighting, teacher integration, compute, query calibration, baseline protocols, seed variation, held-out selection, and reference switching. No exhaustive proof audit or reproduction. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/edmd_endpoint_distribution_matching_diffusion_distillation.md).

### 2. **Unbundling Multimodal Vision Encoding**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant representation learning, modular capacity, and multimodal architecture. **Verdict:** Highly relevant experimental-design reference; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17334](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17334), November 12, 2026; [project/blog](https://unbundling.github.io).
- **Mechanism:** Separate three usually bundled choices: visual pretraining, vision-only parameter capacity, and placing that capacity before multimodal fusion. Train controlled dense decoder models on common data and matched lifetime FLOPs. Move the encoder's parameter budget into vision-only additive FFN and attention-projection branches in the earliest shared backbone layers, keeping joint attention and shared weights.
- **Why it matters:** Vision-only additive branches inside early shared layers retain dedicated capacity without a separate encoder. Removing an encoder changes more than the existence of a pretrained visual prior. Modality-specific capacity and where it operates are independently testable architectural decisions. This is a better experiment template than comparing differently pretrained public checkpoints and attributing the gap to one ingredient.
- **Evidence:** With a 1.7B backbone and 2.6e21 lifetime FLOPs, a 0.4B scratch encoder reaches 44.4 average VQA versus encoder-free 39.0; adding the same parameters to the shared backbone gives 38.6–38.8. Vision-only FFNs in layers 1–8 reach 45.0, versus 35.1 when placed in layers 21–28. In-fusion tracks the encoder across a 0.6B–6.4B scale ladder; the encoder-free scaling fit needs roughly three times the compute. Midtraining and a one-H200 image-request latency study support the same direction.
- **Catch:** These are common-data experiments, not a refutation of released encoders trained on extra data. Pretraining is at 224-pixel resolution, with 448-pixel instruction tuning for evaluation; the data mixture was selected for the scratch-encoder recipe. Dense models stop at 6.4B; sparse MoE, interleaved sequences, video, and higher-resolution pretraining remain open. A hierarchical encoder-free embedding can match the encoder when supplied 640-pixel images compressed into the same token count, so the information/compute regime matters. Lifetime accounting excludes instruction tuning, and latency depends on batching/deployment.
- **Read scope:** Main protocol, objective, data/compute accounting, architecture controls, principal tables/scaling figures, midtraining, serving setup, and limitations; not the entire 47-page-plus benchmark/appendix audit. No detailed note today.

### 3. **Back to Pixels: Lifting Latent Video Diffusion into Pixel Space**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant video generation, controllability, and interactive-model interfaces. **Verdict:** Highly relevant engineering follow-up; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17336), November 12, 2026; [PixVid project](https://chenshuo20.github.io/PixVid/).
- **Mechanism:** Reuse a pretrained latent DiT, replace the VAE interface with frame-wise pixel patch embeddings and a causal convolutional detailer, initialize the input through fitted pixel-to-latent alignment, and calibrate signal-to-noise levels. Predict clean pixels while applying the flow loss in velocity space; add temporal/perceptual losses and use a larger noise shift. After Self Forcing distillation, the model can generate one frame per action rather than await a temporally compressed block.
- **Why it matters:** Frame-wise pixel tokens plus a causal detailer make each action condition its own output frame. Compression defines an intervention granularity, not just a storage ratio. A temporally merged latent can impose control latency that more throughput cannot remove. Conversely, “pixel space” need not mean abandoning a pretrained semantic backbone or making every pixel a transformer token.
- **Evidence:** The Wan2.1-14B lift uses about 1.3M training clips from 64K synthetic videos and reaches VBench 84.78 versus 84.05 for the latent baseline. Fast-motion depth editing retains 98% of source motion versus a matched finetuned latent model's 83%. On one B200, the camera-controlled variant cuts median control-to-screen latency from 495 to 34 ms, while throughput falls from 72.0 to 29.4 fps; both exceed the native 16 fps. Doubling spatial patch size permits 960x1664 generation at the same token count per frame as 480x832.
- **Catch:** At low resolution, removing temporal compression increases tokens per video-second about 3.9-fold. The interactive variant uses about three times the DiT FLOPs per frame and has worse average camera rotation/translation errors despite better abrupt-turn responsiveness. Editing FVD worsens from 91 to 118. The primary text-to-video clips are only 21 frames/1.3 seconds, and VBench downscales before scoring, so it does not establish high-resolution detail gains. Constant tokens per frame is not constant total runtime or decoder cost. A visual camera model is not verified physical dynamics or a planning-capable world model.
- **Read scope:** Main architecture/training, text-to-video, high-resolution, matched editing, streaming results, ablations, and limitations; selected setup/compute passages, not a complete supplementary or implementation audit. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Capacity-Constrained Delayed Feedback for Online Conformal Risk Control**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant uncertainty, verification, and deployment-aware feedback allocation. **Verdict:** Useful; lighter recommendation with theorem assumptions intact.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.17059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17059), November 12, 2026, Bowen Wang and Osvaldo Simeone.
- **Mechanism:** Jointly schedule pending-label tracking and update prediction-set thresholds. Sample an independent proxy deadline for each round, admit it only if capacity permits, evict at deadline, and inverse-weight received losses for proxy survival and the chosen delay horizon. Optimize the proxy distribution to balance receiving labels against saturating the pending-feedback buffer; saturation remains an explicit bound term.
- **Why it matters:** Randomized proxy deadlines and inverse-weighted threshold updates connect feedback scheduling to calibration. Keeping only the newest pending predictions can silently exclude almost all useful feedback when labels arrive slowly. A resource limit changes the statistical observation process. Buffer policy and uncertainty calibration must be designed together, rather than treating missing labels as an incidental systems detail.
- **Evidence:** Theory addresses bounded arbitrary delays and i.i.d. stochastic delays; optimized risk-gap rates depend on capacity K through exponent K/(K+1). Experiments span polyp/tumor segmentation, Natural Questions, hierarchical ImageNet, and wireless beam selection. In segmentation with mean delay 32 rounds, maximum 150, and 2,151 predictions, FIFO needs K at least 8 to approach target FNR 0.1; the proposed schemes use scarce capacity more effectively. Distribution-aware masks approach unlimited-tracking sizes at K=32. Correlated-delay experiments explicitly revert to the arbitrary-delay design.
- **Catch:** The guarantee concerns long-run risk in expectation over the policy's randomness and applicable delay model, not patient-specific coverage or every realized prefix. The improved stochastic design uses knowledge of the delay law; its theorem does not cover correlated delays. Known delay bounds, bounded scores, special loss endpoint behavior, learning-rate choices, and simulated label delays are substantive assumptions. Large inverse weights and conservative larger sets remain costs; adaptive unknown-delay policies are future work. This is a calibration wrapper, not evidence that its frozen base models improved.
- **Read scope:** Main feedback model/assumptions, scheduling algorithm, weighted update, stated rate results, experiment protocols and reported trends, and future work; no appendix proof verification. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Automated PD-L1 Tumor Proportion Scoring with Uncertainty-Guided Triage in Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer**

- **Classification:** Adjacent medical-AI inspiration for structured prediction and selective review. **Verdict:** Useful, not clinical-readiness evidence; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.16907](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16907), Kesi Xu and colleagues; full-text PDF inspected.
- **Mechanism:** Separate tumor-region selection, positive/negative tumor-cell counting, and review selection. Fuse detection and density-map counts using cellularity-dependent weights, aggregate counts into a slide score, and rank disagreement across 15 trained models. TPS-Guard adds cutoff-specific reason flags near clinically meaningful 1% and 50% thresholds; those flags explain/advise review but do not themselves force referral.
- **Why it matters:** Count-based scores, ensemble disagreement, and cutoff-specific reason flags expose different failure modes. Global predictive uncertainty and decision-boundary risk are different interfaces. The transferable idea is to retain count-level evidence and named review reasons, then evaluate which errors survive selective referral instead of advertising lower average error alone.
- **Evidence:** On 114 whole slides unused for cell training/validation, leave-one-reader-out model MAE is 6.96 percentage points versus individual-reader MAEs 4.24–8.29. Against the three-reader mean, referring 17/114 slides lowers MAE from 6.70 to 4.96 on the remainder. Model uncertainty correlates with absolute score error and reader disagreement. External evaluation uses 98 ROIs from 21 test patients, including a stain unseen in training; zero-shot fused MAE is 21.12 points, and referral ranks higher-error cases.
- **Catch:** The reduction in average error does not establish uniformly better decisions: internal 50%-cutoff sensitivity on auto-reported cases falls from 10/16 to 2/4 after referral, partly because true positives are referred too. At 1%, model category disagreement remains worse than a single reader. External stain shift is severe: MAE 11.06 on SP263 versus 29.66 on unseen E1L3N. The 154-slide full cohort includes training-exposed uncertainty estimates, so use the clean 114-slide analysis. Review flags were developed on the same development cohort; the external study is ROI-level, not a second whole-slide deployment. No prospective reader-benefit result or calibrated guarantee follows from ensemble SD.
- **Read scope:** Main pipeline, cohort/split accounting, counting and triage mechanisms, reader comparisons, cutoff outcomes, external/stain-shift results, and selected supplementary-rule references; not a complete supplementary audit. No detailed note today.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**eDMD.** Preserve the distinction between the final distribution we care about and the intermediate objects used to produce it. Its endpoint-only fake is a small intervention with a clear algebraic diagnosis, transfer into another distillation implementation, and a destructive reference-switch control. The next experiment is not “use endpoints everywhere”: separate fake-reference choice, teacher integration, guidance, query sampling, and total training cost, and keep the exact-denoiser theorem separate from practical local updates.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Diffusion distillation:** Specify exactly which student distribution the auxiliary model learns. Compare endpoint, mixture, and stage references while holding rollouts and guidance fixed; include matched wall-clock costs, fixed-budget checkpoints, held-out selection, diversity, and independent human judgments. Endpoint fitting has precedents; eDMD's contribution is the analysis and every-step local supervision, not inventing final-output training.
- **Multimodal modularity:** Match modality-specific capacity, placement, data, and lifetime compute separately. A failed encoder-free ablation does not establish that external visual pretraining was the missing ingredient.
- **Interactive generation:** Report action arrival-to-display latency separately from fps. Match temporal block size, input information, teacher, hardware, and streaming-quality controls. Do not call next-frame responsiveness improved physical reasoning.
- **Uncertainty:** Treat label acquisition/retention as a modeled sampling process. Report risk together with prediction-set size, pending-feedback load, inverse-weight behavior, and delay-law misspecification.
- **Medical review:** Report clean held-out cohorts, stain/site shift, cutoff-specific sensitivity with its changing denominators, and actual human-plus-model workflow outcomes. An average-error reduction after removing difficult cases is not a clinical utility demonstration.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The useful structure today sits at a boundary: final versus intermediate samples, vision-only versus shared parameters, individual frames versus compressed time blocks, observed versus discarded feedback, and numerical uncertainty versus decision-specific review. eDMD is the keep because it turns one such boundary into a falsifiable objective correction. The other four are strong experiment-design references, with real trade-offs: more distillation compute, restricted architectural regimes, lower video throughput, delay assumptions, and clinical domain shift. Preserve those conditions alongside the mechanism.
