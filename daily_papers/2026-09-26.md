## Ranked papers

### 1. **TrackEverything: Long Horizon Dense Tracking via De-Duplicating 3D Scene Representations**

**Verdict:** must read direct 3D/video representation paper.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest state-representation paper in the remaining batch. TrackEverything treats long video not as an ever-growing stack of frames but as repeated observations of a persistent 3D world. Its core trick is practical and transferable: voxelized cross-window de-duplication merges co-located surface tracks, so memory scales with unique geometry rather than frame count. The method also splits endpoint prediction from dense trajectory decoding, sending only dynamic points through the expensive trajectory refiner, and replaces 4D correlation volumes with 3D WAFT feature sampling. The result is the first dense 3D tracker in the paper's comparison that can track all visible points across 1000+ frame videos within 40 GB, while beating open-source all-frame dense baselines by more than 20% APD on short TAPVid-3D clips.

### 2. **FB-GDM: Fully-Bayesian Guided Diffusion Models for High-Dimensional Linear Inverse Problems via Unsupervised Variational Inference**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct diffusion/inference paper.

Why it matters: FB-GDM attacks a real deployment problem in diffusion inverse solvers: the guidance balance between prior and measurement is usually a scalar knob tuned with ground truth. The paper reinterprets PiGDM's guidance variances as latent precision variables and infers them by fast variational inference at every reverse step. That gives a conditional score using only the observation and forward operator, not the true noise level or a validation oracle. On CelebA-HQ inverse problems, it matches a ground-truth-tuned PiGDM oracle within about 0.1 dB in the Gaussian-blur setting, stays close under operator and noise shifts, and avoids the face-prior hallucinations that DPS can create on ImageNet images. The useful lesson is Bayesian calibration inside the sampler, not another diffusion prior.

### 3. **PoEM: Predicting RL Outcomes from Existing Policies**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct RL/post-training geometry paper.

Why it matters: PoEM is interesting because it moves the question from parameter arithmetic to policy-space geometry. If a new reward is a linear combination of old rewards, the KL-regularized optimal policy has a log-mixture form over the old policies. The more surprising empirical claim is that even unrelated single-reward adapters often occupy a low-rank space in log-policy ratios, despite nearly orthogonal parameter updates. PoEM estimates mixture weights from a small calibration set scored by the target reward, then composes existing policies at inference time. It recovers much of direct RL's reward gain on combined rewards, uses a coverage score to warn when held-out rewards are unreachable, and beats the top expert on most RM-Div held-out rewards. The caveat is important: uncovered reward directions still fail, and the evidence is on relatively small language models and Stable Diffusion v1.4 adapters.

### 4. **RD-JEPA: Predictive latent pretraining for few-trajectory transfer across reaction-diffusion equations**

**Verdict:** useful direct representation/world-model paper.

Why it matters: RD-JEPA is not a giant foundation-model report; it is a controlled test of whether predictive latent pretraining can transfer across related PDE systems. A context encoder observes four fields, an EMA target encoder represents future fields, and a lead-time-conditioned predictor learns future-state representations without reconstructing the field during pretraining. The predictor has diffusion-inspired neighborhood and reaction-inspired pointwise latent pathways. After pretraining on five reaction-diffusion systems, the same checkpoint adapts to Lambda-Omega, Barkley, and Oregonator with one, five, or ten trajectories, beating FNO, LNO, ReViT, RieszNO, CNextU-Net, a no-predictive-latent control, and an architecture-matched scratch control in the reported mean errors. The scope is narrow, but the design cleanly separates predictive representation reuse from architecture alone.

### 5. **ExplorationBench: Measuring AI Systems' Exploration in Verifiable Alien Worlds**

**Verdict:** useful direct exploration/evaluation paper.

Why it matters: This benchmark is agent-adjacent, but it earns its slot because the evaluation target is specific: evidence acquisition under unfamiliar executable rules. AlienCode and AlienLogic give systems flawed manuals, four rounds of tool-mediated exploration, and then closed-book held-out tasks graded by an interpreter or proof checker. This separates recall from exploration more cleanly than ordinary static benchmarks. The strongest AlienCode trajectory reaches 87.6% after exploration while the same number of turns without environment feedback stays at or below 11.0%. The paper also shows the ugly part: ranks barely transfer across the two sandboxes, rule reports do not guarantee rule use, and trajectories from the same system can end far apart. It is synthetic, but its diagnostics are sharper than another generic agent benchmark leaderboard.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**TrackEverything** matters because it frames long video tracking as persistent 3D scene memory with de-duplication, not as a larger frame-token sequence.

**FB-GDM** matters because it turns diffusion guidance calibration into latent precision inference rather than task-specific knob tuning.

**PoEM** matters because it suggests previous RL policies can span reusable log-policy directions, and it gives a coverage test for when composition is likely to fail.

**RD-JEPA** matters because it tests predictive latent pretraining as equation-level transfer, with controls for architecture and no-predictive-latent alternatives.

**ExplorationBench** matters because it evaluates exploration as choosing evidence and applying discovered rules, with executable grading rather than model-judge vibes.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The September 26 pattern is reusable computation. TrackEverything reuses physical scene state instead of re-encoding video redundancy. FB-GDM reuses a diffusion prior while inferring how much to trust it from the current observation. PoEM reuses old RL directions when a new reward lives inside their policy-space span. RD-JEPA reuses future-state predictive structure across related PDEs. ExplorationBench checks whether a system can reuse evidence it gathered itself rather than merely recall familiar rules. The common lesson is that long-horizon competence often depends less on adding another loop and more on keeping the right state, calibrated to the job it will actually serve.
