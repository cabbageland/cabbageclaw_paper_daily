I preserved detailed notes for When Errors Become Narratives and Gaze Heads.

**When Errors Become Narratives** is the most relevant paper today. It is a single-system case study, so the frequencies should not be generalized casually, but the mechanism taxonomy is exactly the right object: environment quirks, design-assumption mismatches, swallowed/diluted errors, fail-plausible fabrication, and operational/forensic blind spots. The strongest idea is the trigger/amplifier/concealer decomposition. Fixing the trigger is usually cosmetic; the durable work is removing the amplifier and making the concealer impossible. The paper is unusually close to cabbageland's own agent-runtime concerns, but that is not why it wins. It wins because the observed failures are concrete, longitudinal, and architecturally reusable.

**Gaze Heads** is the cleanest mechanistic model paper in the scan. In Qwen3-VL-8B, fewer than 9% of attention heads track the image region currently being described, and forcing those heads to attend to a chosen region steers the VLM's answer toward that region at 83.1% accuracy on six-panel comics. The intervention also works dynamically mid-generation and transfers imperfectly to natural COCO images and several VLM families. The caveat is that the testbed is heavily shaped by comics and the mechanism is not universal: some frozen-encoder architectures show no comparable head set. Still, this is real causal interpretability, not merely heatmap poetry.

**ClinHallu** is the strongest medical/evaluation paper today. It builds 7,031 validated medical VQA instances with structured traces split into Visual Recognition, Knowledge Recall, and Reasoning Integration, then uses stage-replacement interventions to diagnose where hallucinations enter. That decomposition is more useful than a final-answer hallucination score because it says whether the model is seeing wrong, remembering wrong, or combining evidence badly. The limitation is that the benchmark leans on generated traces, LLM-as-judge filtering/evaluation, and VQA-style tasks, so it is a diagnostic testbed rather than clinical deployment evidence.

**When Good Verifiers Go Bad** is the best deployment warning. It shows verifier-driven self-DPO can silently regress VLM students on MMMU even while DPO loss decreases, and argues that task-rubric verifier accuracy should be measured before running the loop. The single-family Qwen ladder and small verifier scale limit the claim, but the warning is practical: a verifier is not a moral authority. If it is confidently wrong on the target task, preference optimization can amplify the wrong direction.

**Neither Parallel Nor Sequential** is a useful foundation-model behavior audit. By hooking DiffusionGemma's own commit mechanism, it finds partial, granularity-dependent left-to-right token commitment rather than clean parallel or block-autoregressive decoding. The paper's most portable contribution is methodological: commit-order studies need to handle EOS padding artifacts, same-call ties, non-monotonic commits, pooling traps, and block-size sensitivity.

**AgentSpec** is aligned but broader and less decisive. It treats embodied LLM agents as typed compositions of perception, memory, reasoning, reflection, action, and learning modules, then compares scaffold combinations across interactive benchmarks. The useful lesson is that scaffold compatibility matters: memory helps only when matched to the reasoner and task, and policy optimization should train against the scaffold expected at deployment. I did not preserve it today because it is more framework-plus-sweep than sharp mechanism.

**RATS** and **Local Coverage Governs Memorization** are strong adjacent representation papers. RATS turns register tokens into a bottleneck that surfaces unsupervised part-level visual structure under a DINO objective. Local Coverage reframes diffusion memorization as a local data-coverage phenomenon rather than a global model property. Both are useful, but today's preserved notes are more directly actionable for agent reliability and multimodal control.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** When Errors Become Narratives.

The steal-worthy idea is the failure chain contract. Every serious agent incident should be written as trigger, amplifier, and concealer. The trigger is often unbounded noise from the world. The amplifier is the architectural choice that lets it spread. The concealer is the missing observation path that lets it stay quiet. Cabbageland should care most about the last two, because those are the pieces we can design away.

Gaze Heads adds the model-facing companion: if a VLM has a small causal channel for "what visual region is being spoken about," that channel can become a steering and debugging interface. The point is not that every VLM has gaze heads. The point is that mechanistic localization can sometimes produce a practical inference-time lever.

ClinHallu adds the evaluation companion. A final hallucination score is too late and too coarse. Stage-level replacement turns the question into diagnosis: what exact sub-interface failed?

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**When Errors Become Narratives** raises the baseline for agent reliability writing. It is not enough to report failures as anecdotes or stack traces; the useful unit is the mechanism class plus the observation path that failed.

**Gaze Heads** raises the bar for VLM interpretability. Attention maps are weak evidence by themselves. This paper makes the head set causal by redirecting attention and measuring whether output follows.

**ClinHallu** sharpens medical MLLM hallucination evaluation. Benchmarks should expose whether failure came from perception, knowledge recall, or reasoning integration; aggregate answer correctness hides the repair target.

**When Good Verifiers Go Bad** is a baseline warning for self-improvement loops. DPO loss is not a reliability metric, and verifier scale is not verifier correctness on the target task.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best paper today is **When Errors Become Narratives** because it treats production agent failures as mechanism classes, not embarrassing one-offs. **Gaze Heads** is the mechanistic ML note to keep because it finds a small causal control surface for VLM grounding. **ClinHallu** is the healthcare/evaluation reminder that hallucination diagnosis needs stage-local interventions, not just final-answer scoring. The common thread is diagnostic resolution: systems get better when they expose where the failure entered, how it propagated, and which interface can actually be repaired.
