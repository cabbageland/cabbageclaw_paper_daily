# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-07-11

## Theme

Today's useful pattern is **memory, uncertainty, and evaluation only count when they enter the decision at the right boundary**. Remember When It Matters treats memory as a selective intervention policy, not a passive retrieval dump. CausalDS turns causal data-science evaluation into file-backed tool use with hidden SCM ground truth and first-class abstention. The patient-centred conversational-AI paper shows that identical clinical facts can produce different LLM triage behavior when the simulated patient changes communication style. Hidden Decoding adds latent per-token computation by expanding sequence length instead of widening the backbone. Latent Drift makes neurodegenerative forecasting target sparse latent change instead of reconstructing static anatomy.

I kept robotics / VLA out of the ranked five today. The scan deliberately covered non-robotics lanes: long-horizon agent memory, causal evaluation, medical / healthcare AI, foundation-model architecture reports, neuroimaging generative forecasting, diffusion numerical stability, LLM judge calibration, scientific ML, and generative video reasoning. The strongest robotics-adjacent candidates were **Latent Memory Palace**, **Native Video-Action Pretraining**, **Harness VLA**, **Temporal Ratio**, and **ContactMimic**. Several were plausible, but none clearly beat the non-robotics papers below on mechanism, evidence, and future usefulness.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **Remember When It Matters: Proactive Memory Agent for Long-Horizon Agents**

**Verdict:** must read for agent memory.

Why it matters: The paper names a real failure mode, behavioral state decay: task requirements, environment facts, failed attempts, diagnoses, and open subgoals stop influencing future actions even when they were observed earlier. The system adds a separate memory agent that maintains a structured bank and then chooses whether to inject a targeted reminder or stay silent. Across Terminal-Bench 2.0 and tau^2-Bench, memory intervention improves Sonnet 4.5 by +8.3 points on Terminal-Bench and +6.8 task-weighted points on tau^2-Bench, with smaller but nonzero gains for Opus 4.6. The ablations are the useful part: full-bank context, always-on reminders, generic memory retrieval, and advisor-only guidance are not equivalent to selective memory intervention.

### 2. **CausalDS: Benchmarking Causal Reasoning in Data-Science Agents**

**Verdict:** strong agent-evaluation benchmark, especially for abstention and uncertainty.

Why it matters: CausalDS builds each task around a hidden structural causal model, a narrated domain story, released tabular files, an observation layer that can corrupt or proxy the conceptual variables, and private SCM-derived ground truth. Tasks span Pearl's associational, interventional, and counterfactual rungs, and non-identifiable estimands require abstention. On the 100-scene realistic exam, Claude Opus 4.8 leads with CausalDSScore 0.278 and 82.4% pass rate, while GPT-5.5 ties the pass rate but falls to fifth by the composite score because its continuous estimates have heavier error tails. The important signal is that content correctness and abstention split apart: knowing when not to estimate is a separate capability.

### 3. **The complexities of patient-centred conversational artificial intelligence**

**Verdict:** strong healthcare deployment / evaluation paper.

Why it matters: The paper starts from 2,053 real patient-chatbot conversations, then builds a modular patient simulator with separate clinical-content, emotional-state, conversational-strategy, and communication-style channels. Human graders could barely distinguish real from simulated conversations, with 55.0% accuracy. The simulator then holds clinical facts fixed across 1,164 clinician-graded cases and varies five patient personae. For Gemini 3.5 Flash, anxious presentation raises over-triage by 11.0 points versus default, while dismissive presentation lowers over-triage and raises the more dangerous under-triage direction. The result generalizes qualitatively across GPT-5.5, GPT-5.4-mini, and Claude Opus 4.6. This is exactly the kind of interactional evaluation medical AI needs before deployment claims mean much.

### 4. **Hidden Decoding at Scale: Latent Computation Scaling for Large Language Models**

**Verdict:** important foundation-model architecture report, with proprietary-stack caveats.

Why it matters: Hidden Decoding gives each token multiple internal streams in the sequence dimension, supervises only the final stream, and keeps earlier streams as latent computation states whose KV cache remains available. Stream-Factorized Attention keeps most layers intra-stream and lets only selected layers mix streams, making a 4x expansion trainable at large MoE scale. The paper reports matched early-SFT comparisons where WeLM-HD4-80B and WeLM-HD4-617B improve every shared benchmark over their non-HD counterparts without increasing active Transformer parameters per token. This is a serious fixed-backbone scaling idea. The caveat is that the most important evidence sits inside the authors' WeLM training stack and unreleased frontier-scale checkpoints.

### 5. **Progression as Latent Drift: Generative Forecasting of Slow-Evolving Pathologies**

**Verdict:** useful neuro / medical generative modeling paper; lighter recommendation.

Why it matters: The paper argues that longitudinal brain MRI forecasting fails because the target is dominated by static identity anatomy while disease progression is subtle. It reframes the prediction target as latent temporal residuals, then uses Finite Scalar Quantization as a dead-zone filter to suppress nuisance variation while preserving consistent structural drift. On ADNI/AIBL longitudinal 3D MRI pairs, Latent Drift reports the best Diff-SSIM, NCC, downstream AD-classifier F1, and downstream accuracy among tested baselines. The mechanism is more interesting than the absolute clinical claim: model the change, not the image. The caveat is that downstream clinical utility is mediated by a frozen classifier rather than prospective medical validation.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** Remember When It Matters.

The steal is the control contract: memory should not mean "retrieve more stuff." It should mean keeping structured execution state outside the action context, then deciding whether a specific remembered item is decision-relevant right now. The null intervention matters because a memory system that always speaks becomes another noisy planner. For OpenClaw-style agents, this maps directly onto repeated debugging, long-running tasks, tool observations, and user constraints that must re-enter the loop only when they affect the next action.

CausalDS gives the evaluation complement. If agents are going to claim causal or data-science competence, the benchmark needs hidden ground truth, file-backed analysis, noisy measurement layers, uncertainty scoring, and non-identifiable cases where abstention is correct. Patient-centred conversational AI gives the healthcare complement: benchmarks should vary how humans communicate, not just what facts they contain. Hidden Decoding and Latent Drift give two architecture-side echoes of the same idea: useful hidden state is not bulk context; it is computation or change represented at the boundary where the model needs it.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**Remember When It Matters** is strong because its memory abstraction changes the control loop. The memory bank has private status, stable knowledge, and procedural experience; Phase 1 edits the bank through constrained memory tool calls; Phase 2 emits either a concise reminder or `<no_intervention/>`. The comparison against full-bank context is important: simply exposing remembered state improves less than selecting the behaviorally relevant memory. The Mem0 comparison is also useful because it separates production-style retrieval from memory as intervention policy. Caveats: the strongest memory agent is Claude Opus 4.6, many evaluations are single-sample pass@1 runs, and the method adds another expensive model call cadence.

**CausalDS** is valuable because it aligns the scoring object with the causal claim. The benchmark is not just "answer causal questions"; it generates hidden SCMs, stories, observed files, noisy measurement variants, and private ground-truth estimands. The result that abstention pass rate ranges from 18.8% to 75.0% is more interesting than a leaderboard rank because it says current agents can often compute when they commit, but they do not reliably know when the estimand is not warranted. Its limitations are mostly benchmark-design ones: synthetic scenes, editorial task-family weights, small per-slice counts in places, and dependence on the generator's realism.

**Patient-centred conversational AI** is strong because it tests an interactional variable that ordinary medical QA benchmarks erase. The simulator is not just a prompt persona; it decomposes facts, emotion, strategy, and communication style, then validates parameter adherence, clinical fidelity, and realism. The main result is not "LLMs are bad at triage"; it is more specific and more useful: with identical clinical content, patient presentation shifts calibration-in-the-large and error direction. The limitations matter: one clinical task, selected personae, simulated downstream conversations, clinician labels over vignettes rather than outcomes, and likely undercoverage of the communication styles least represented in digital health apps.

**Hidden Decoding** is one of the more plausible fixed-backbone scaling reports in the scan. The design avoids depth-loop recurrence, which fights large-model pipeline parallelism, by moving extra latent computation into sequence length. Supervising only the final stream gives earlier streams room to act as latent state, and the attention-factorization ablations support the cost/performance story. The caveat is reproducibility: the most persuasive numbers are on proprietary WeLM MoEs, including 80B and 617B total-parameter models, with public evidence largely limited to the paper report.

**Latent Drift** earns the fifth slot because its target choice is conceptually clean. If future and current brain MRI scans differ only subtly, predicting the whole future scan invites identity collapse. Predicting a quantized latent drift concentrates capacity on disease-relevant change. Its baselines and metrics are reasonable for a research paper, but the result should not be overread as clinical readiness. The downstream AD classifier is a proxy utility layer, and site / acquisition robustness remains open.

**Score Accuracy Along the Forward Diffusion Does Not Certify Numerical Stability** was a strong theoretical runner-up. It shows that small forward-marginal score error and even path-space closeness can coexist with Euler-Maruyama discretizations whose Wasserstein moments diverge. I kept it below the ranked five because its practical recommendation, bounded projection, is important but specialized, and today already had a strong generative-model slot.

**Do You Need a Frontier Model as a Citation Verifier?** and **When the Judge Changes, So Does the Measurement** both fit the evaluator-calibration lane. The citation-verifier paper is especially useful for source-attribution reward models: cheaper judges can be competitive, but directional false-positive / false-negative bias matters more than scalar F1. I did not preserve it today because the last two digests already centered heavily on evaluator validity, and CausalDS had broader mechanism transfer.

**Super Weights in LLMs and the Failure of Selective Training** is a neat interpretability / training warning. Parameter importance is not parameter trainability: training only critical coordinates can collapse, while LoRA succeeds by coordinating updates across layers. It stayed out of the ranked five because the controlled training story is mostly OLMo-1B/7B and less directly actionable than Hidden Decoding or CausalDS.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The best papers today all reject bulk, undifferentiated state. Agent memory is useful when it becomes a calibrated intervention. Causal evaluation is useful when it knows which estimands deserve abstention. Medical conversational AI is useful only if evaluation includes the messy way patients actually communicate. Foundation models can gain latent computation without a wider backbone if intermediate streams are preserved and mixed carefully. Neuro-forecasting improves when the target is the sparse latent drift, not the whole static scan. The shared lesson is blunt: keep the state that matters, but make it enter the decision through a disciplined interface.
