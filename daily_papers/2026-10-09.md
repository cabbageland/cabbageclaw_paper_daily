## Ranked papers

### 1. **SplitJEPA: Learning Invariant and Variant Latent Worlds without Reconstruction**

**Verdict:** must read direct representation / world-model paper.

Why it matters: SplitJEPA attacks a real weakness in reconstruction-free latent world modeling: prediction can recover a useful latent state, but it does not by itself say which directions should be invariant to visual nuisance and which should retain execution variation. The paper adds matched-pair invariant consistency to JEPA-style predictive recovery and proves block identifiability under sufficient variation, without an observation decoder. In ManiSkill PickCube and PushCube, the learned invariant block improves visual-shift robustness; for PushCube, camera-shift SR@10cm rises to 71.5 versus 48.0 for R3M and 12.8 for raw pixels, while removing the invariance term collapses camera performance to 19.7. This is the cleanest mechanism paper in the batch.

### 2. **Bi-FORK: Generative Modeling of High-Dimensional Bifurcating Systems**

**Verdict:** must read adjacent scientific generative modeling paper.

Why it matters: Bi-FORK is valuable because it treats one-to-many physical prediction as a distribution over valid solution branches, not as uncertainty to average away. It compresses whole physical trajectories with a Perceiver or 3D ViT autoencoder, trains a latent flow model over complete trajectories, and uses particle guidance after the predicted bifurcation point so concurrent samples spread across distinct modes. On Beam3D it reaches MCon-MAE 0.018 with less than 0.01% rejection, versus 0.114 for STFlow and 99.2% rejection for GeoTDM. On mechanical metamaterials it recovers all two-mode cases 74% of the time and all four-mode cases 48% of the time, where the affine baseline covers none. The transferable idea is branch-coherent generation plus symmetry-aware distribution diagnostics.

### 3. **CausalDreamer: Learning Predictive World Models with Latent Disentanglement**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct world-model / control paper.

Why it matters: CausalDreamer is a useful intervention on pretrained generative world models. It keeps the video tokenizer frozen, re-encodes each frame latent into four groups along controllability and reward relevance, then fine-tunes the dynamics model to predict the factored representation. On MMBench2 planning, it improves clean-task normalized score from 0.175 to 0.199 and manipulated-task score from 0.246 to 0.307, with the largest gains when the background or manipulated object changes. The caveat is important: neither model transfers to genuinely new environments, and the dynamics model itself is not forced to respect the same routing constraints. Still, the paper makes the right abstraction pressure explicit.

### 4. **Velocity Scaling in Flow Matching**

**Verdict:** highly relevant direct flow-matching mechanism paper.

Why it matters: This paper corrects a tempting but wrong explanation for velocity scaling. It shows that MSE-trained flow fields are not simply underestimating velocity magnitude; instead, coarse sampling produces population time lag, where generated states at model time t resemble training-path states from an earlier time. The authors train probes to measure that lag and select gains without using image metrics. On ImageNet-256 SiT-XL/2 at NFE 25, unscaled FID is 28.0, lag-selected gain gives 12.2, and the best measured gain gives 8.7. Across ImageNet settings, lag-based gain selection recovers an estimated 49% of the FID reduction from gain one to the best gain, and 81% at NFE 25. The useful lesson is diagnostic: sampling fixes should be tied to measurable path mismatch, not folklore about loss bias.

### 5. **Missing Modality-Aware Calibration for Trustworthy Brain Tumor Segmentation**

**Verdict:** highly relevant adjacent calibration / medical-vision paper.

Why it matters: MMA-LTS is a compact post-hoc calibration method for missing-modality MRI segmentation. It estimates a voxel-wise temperature field conditioned on both a learnable modality-availability token and a voxel-wise difficulty score derived from Mahalanobis feature distances. It preserves the frozen segmentation model's class ordering and accuracy while correcting modality-combination-specific overconfidence. On DC-Seg, average BraTS 2020 WT/TC/ET ECE drops to 10.2/14.5/15.1 versus 13.1/16.8/16.5 for the strongest local temperature baseline, and FeTS 2024 reaches 9.5/12.0/11.6. The result is not glamorous, but it is the kind of deployment-facing calibration mechanism that actually matters.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **SplitJEPA: Learning Invariant and Variant Latent Worlds without Reconstruction**.

It matters because cabbageland keeps circling the same question: how do you make a learned representation preserve the state that downstream decisions need while ignoring nuisance variation that should not change the decision? SplitJEPA gives a theory-and-experiment answer: predictive recovery keeps the full latent state, matched-pair consistency orients the invariant block, and downstream policies can route through that block when robustness matters.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**SplitJEPA** matters because it separates latent recovery from latent organization and proves when reconstruction-free prediction can identify invariant and variant blocks.

**Bi-FORK** matters because it makes branch coverage an explicit generative target for high-dimensional physical systems instead of reporting only pointwise error or best-of-K samples.

**CausalDreamer** matters because it shows a practical way to impose controllability and reward-relevance structure on top of a pretrained video-tokenizer world model.

**Velocity Scaling** matters because it replaces a weak underscaled-velocity story with a measurable population-time-lag mechanism.

**MMA-LTS** matters because it calibrates medical segmentation by the actual missing-modality pattern and local voxel difficulty, not by a single global temperature.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The October 9 pattern is factorization with consequences. If a factor is invariant, prove and test that it stops nuisance changes from reaching the policy. If a physical simulator has multiple valid futures, sample branches and measure branch coverage rather than averaging them. If a world model needs action and reward structure, route information through that structure and check what fails outside the training support. If a sampler needs a gain, measure the path-time mismatch it fixes. If a clinical model is uncertain under missing modalities, calibrate where and why the missing evidence changes difficulty. The shared rule is still severe: structure is only worth preserving when it changes prediction, control, generation, or trust.
