# Daily Paper Digest - 2026-08-17

## Theme

The best papers today all attack interface mismatch. **MemPrism** says stored history is not yet usable memory unless it is reorganized for the current decision. **Silent Updates** says a system card is not yet accountability unless it is bound to the artifact actually being served. **DreamWAM** says predicted video is not yet useful future state unless it preserves motion, geometry, and semantics that matter for action. **CliniCARE-Bench** says raw clinical accuracy is not yet trustworthy reasoning unless the investigation is evidence-grounded and shortcut-free. **WorldClaw** says a text prompt is not yet a world unless it is compiled into explicit terrain, region plans, and editable assets.

## Short overview

This Monday, August 17, 2026 run used live arXiv HTML full-text inspection across long-horizon agent memory, post-deployment model governance, world action models, clinical agent evaluation, and 3D world generation. I inspected the arXiv HTML full text for all five ranked papers below.

Four papers earn preserved notes. **WorldClaw** is the strongest adjacent inspiration, but it does not clear the preserve bar because the mechanism is richer than the evidence: the pipeline is interesting, yet the evaluation is still mostly qualitative and heavily dependent on strong closed models. **Most relevant today:** **MemPrism**.

## Ranked papers

### 1. **MemPrism: Task-Conditioned Relational Memory Views for Long-Horizon Agents**

**Verdict:** must read direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the sharpest memory paper in the batch because it names the right failure mode. The problem is not always missing storage or bad retrieval. Sometimes the evidence is there, but it is organized in the wrong form for the current decision. MemPrism stores normalized event history, then builds temporary relational working-memory views on demand. On matched backbones it reaches **40.71%** ALFWorld success versus **38.27%** for LangMem, **12.87%** Mind2Web overall action accuracy versus **8.79%** for full history, and **17.7%** EB-ALFRED average success versus **11.7%** for Mem0, while also cutting prompt tokens by **33.6%** at the 50-step threshold.

### 2. **Silent Updates: Measuring and Closing the Post-Deployment Disclosure Gap**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is one of the better field-deployment papers in recent memory because it turns vague drift anxiety into a concrete chain-of-custody audit. The paper studies **9** first-party API providers and **7** inference hosts and finds that no provider in the sample exposes a verifiable API-to-evaluation round trip, even when they publish substantial safety documentation. It also finds that **6 of 9** providers include terms that may discourage independent evaluation, then proposes a behavioral trigger system for when post-deployment changes should force disclosure or re-evaluation.

### 3. **DreamWAM: Beyond RGB Future Prediction for World Action Models**

**Verdict:** preserve-worthy direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the one robotics or VLA paper that clears the bar today because it makes a precise representational correction instead of hand-waving about "world knowledge." DreamWAM supervises future state through appearance, motion, geometry, and semantics rather than RGB alone, while remaining RGB-only at inference. On matched baselines it improves LIBERO from **98.00%** to **98.90%**, LIBERO-Plus from **69.16%** to **75.47%** with joint inference and from **51.36%** to **63.44%** without rollout, and real-robot perturbation performance from **55.6%** to **74.4%**.

### 4. **CliniCARE-Bench: Clinical Calibrated Audit of Medical Reasoning in EHR**

**Verdict:** strong adjacent direct paper.

Why it matters: This is the non-robotics medical benchmark worth keeping because it evaluates agentic clinical audit the way it should be evaluated: through logged record investigation, evidence grounding, governing-policy use, process adherence, and calibrated abstention rather than answer-only medical QA. The benchmark covers **25** clinician-validated scenarios and **750** patient-specific cases over MIMIC-IV. Across **16** agentic systems, four-way accuracy spans **65.3%** to **76.1%**, but defect-free accuracy is **4.8** to **14.8** points lower and reorders the field. That is exactly the kind of leaderboard humiliation a domain needs.

### 5. **WorldClaw: Agentic 3D Open-World Generation at Scale**

**Verdict:** adjacent inspiration, not yet preserve-worthy.

Why it matters: The mechanism is more interesting than the current evidence. WorldClaw uses a clean global-to-regional pipeline: structured scene planning, semantic terrain generation, region-conditioned object composition, mesh reconstruction, and render-guided refinement into editable instance assets. That is much closer to production-minded 3D world construction than generic image-to-world vibes. The problem is that the main evaluation is qualitative, the pipeline still leans on heavy closed-model components, and the paper has not yet shown a hard quantitative case that the decomposition survives beyond demos.

## Most relevant to cabbageland

**Most relevant today:** **MemPrism**.

It sharpens a design rule that keeps showing up everywhere else in the digest. Storing the right facts is not sufficient. The system also needs the right decision-time interface. MemPrism makes that explicit with normalized event storage plus temporary relational views. **Silent Updates** makes the same move for governance: publishing an evaluation artifact is not enough unless it is bound to the served system. **DreamWAM** makes it for action models: predicting future pixels is not enough unless the future representation exposes action-relevant state. Same lesson across domains: a persistent artifact without the right interface is a polite way to fail.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

**MemPrism** matters because it cleanly separates persistent memory from working memory. The paper's real contribution is not another memory store. It is the claim that post-retrieval representation mismatch is its own failure mode, plus evidence that dynamic relational views beat fixed history even when the underlying facts stay unchanged.

**Silent Updates** matters because it attacks a governance blind spot that most model-card discussion glides past. The useful question is not whether documentation exists, but whether an external party can verify that the served system is the one the documentation evaluated.

**DreamWAM** matters because it reframes the future-prediction target rather than just bolting on a bigger video backbone. The strongest evidence is not the in-distribution gain. It is that matched beyond-RGB supervision substantially improves robustness even when no rollout is used at inference.

**CliniCARE-Bench** matters because it shows raw accuracy can flatter clinically unsafe systems. Defect-free accuracy, process adherence, and calibrated abstention are not cosmetic extras here. They change the ranking.

**WorldClaw** matters as a framing prompt for 3D generation: global terrain and regional editable assets are a much better decomposition than trying to hallucinate an entire world in one homogeneous pass. But it is still more a promising systems sketch than a settled research result.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The August 17 lesson is that the strongest systems do not just preserve information. They compile it into the right operational interface. Good agent memory should expose the relation structure a decision actually needs. Good deployment governance should bind published evaluations to the artifact being served. Good world-action models should preserve motion, geometry, and semantic identity rather than worship RGB futures. Good clinical benchmarks should punish confident shortcutting, not reward it. Good 3D generation should keep global structure explicit while letting local assets remain editable. Same taste everywhere: do not confuse a stored artifact with a usable state.
