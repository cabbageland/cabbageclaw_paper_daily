### 1. **Conditional Diffusion Models for Amortized Bayesian System Identification**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant diffusion, explicit state, uncertainty, and control-oriented system identification. **Verdict:** Highly relevant; preserve. **Most relevant today.**
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.20666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20666), Mike Hänseroth, Niklas Wahlström, Thomas B. Schön, Thomas Seel, and Jan-Hendrik Ewering, November 16, 2026; [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20666). Submitted to IFAC; not an acceptance claim.
- **Mechanism:** Adapt Simformer into a conditional score-based diffusion sampler with separate parameter, hidden-state, measurement, and known-input tokens. A symmetric Markov attention mask connects neighboring states and local observations/inputs, while static parameter tokens aggregate globally. Train with denoising score matching on short simulated windows and random conditioning masks; change the observed variables at inference to switch between joint identification, smoothing, and missing-data reconstruction.
- **Why it matters:** Structural locality and global evidence accumulation are different problems. The model preserves state accuracy when records grow, but cannot keep sharpening parameter uncertainty beyond the evidence lengths it learned. That split is more useful than a blanket claim of length generalization.
- **Evidence:** Four low-dimensional simulated systems; training records have 50 steps, with deployment up to 250. On linear random walk, dense Simformer's state error grows over tenfold, while the structured model's stays nearly constant. Attention-mask/time-encoding ablations attribute the stable state result mainly to the mask, not RoPE. Sampling 1,000 effective samples takes 7 versus 94 seconds for PMMH at length 50, and 43 versus 760 seconds at length 250, on one L40S.
- **Catch:** PMMH is more accurate: even the largest training budget leaves parameter error roughly two to five times higher at length 50. On linear random walk and double well, parameter error roughly doubles as length grows to 250 because the learned posterior fails to contract appropriately. The Lotka–Volterra state result is weaker, with about 50% error growth. Timing excludes amortized offline training; no real-data, high-dimensional, or simulator-misspecification validation. Plotting credible intervals is not a calibration study.
- **Read scope:** Full main text, including objective, dependency mask, study design, ablations, window-width analysis, related work, and conclusion. No code execution. [Detailed note](../paper_notes/conditional_diffusion_bayesian_system_identification.md).

### 2. **The Flip Test: Video World Models Fail to Learn the World State**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant world-model evaluation, physical state, and mechanistic diagnosis. **Verdict:** Highly relevant evaluation recipe; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.19822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19822), Mi Luo and colleagues, November 15, 2026; [project](https://flip-test.github.io/).
- **Mechanism:** Test nearly identical initial images on opposite sides of a physical outcome boundary. Then add equal-sized same-side perturbations to distinguish meaningful sensitivity from indiscriminate flipping, and dense sweeps to locate the predicted boundary. Separate state-reading failures from plausible-video production using direct VLM decisions and measurement-oriented coding agents.
- **Why it matters:** A convincing rollout can be wrong in exactly the way planning cannot tolerate. The transferable benchmark is the hierarchy of paired correctness, crossing-minus-noncrossing sensitivity, and boundary localization—not another undifferentiated realism score.
- **Evidence:** 1,680 initial frames over 10 simulated and 15 nominally real-world tasks; 11 video models. Best overall pair accuracy is 18.3%, where both outcomes must be right and ideal independent binary guessing gives 25%. Video-model selective-sensitivity intervals all include zero; the direct-answer VLM scores +0.46, with 95% interval [0.36, 0.56]. On clearly judged pairs, outputs remain identical across 72% of real and 89% of simulated pairs.
- **Catch:** Only 59 of the 300 real-world frames come from footage with recorded physical outcomes; 241 are edited photographs or staged synthetic stills, with labels fixed by construction. Level-one judgments require agreement of two VLM judges; ambiguity and disagreement count as wrong, so the 25% reference assumes clear binary readouts. Main evaluation uses one rollout per initial frame, not a full predictive-distribution assessment. Threshold-heavy, mostly rigid-object tasks diagnose a real failure without proving that every learned dynamics representation is absent or useless.
- **Read scope:** Main benchmark construction, metrics, model comparison, selectivity/sweep results, diagnostic framing, and appendix limitations. Coding-agent implementation and complete judge audit not reproduced. No detailed note today.

### 3. **Reference Forcing: Long Video Generation with Region-Guided Drift Correction**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant video diffusion/flow, controllability, and structured memory repair. **Verdict:** Useful, mechanism-rich; lighter recommendation.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.21559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21559), Sheng Zhao, Haitian Zheng, Weikai Lin, Junjie Zhao, and Yuhao Zhu, November 16, 2026; [project](https://zhaosheng-thu.github.io/reference-force/).
- **Mechanism:** Keep a separate attention path to the initial video chunk instead of making appearance anchors compete with recent motion context in one softmax. Distill a teacher's segmentation/flow-constrained region matching into query/key LoRA adapters and a learned gate using velocity-prediction MSE. Separately move matched older-cache values toward initial-reference values while keeping their keys unchanged. Do not repair the newest context chunk, where correction causes flicker.
- **Why it matters:** Retaining memory does not help when the retained memory has drifted. Split trusted appearance anchors from recent dynamics, and repair content without silently rewriting the addressing keys. The gate lets newly appearing content avoid irrelevant anchors.
- **Evidence:** On LongLive, adding anchor attention alone improves consistency but reduces tracking-strength motion by 15.5%. Combined with value correction, long-range DINO consistency improves by 0.097 versus 0.044 and 0.034 for the separate components while retaining baseline motion. Comparisons match 12-latent-frame cache capacity and initial noise. At 60 seconds, subject/background consistency is 97.56/96.67 versus LongLive's 97.33/96.49, but imaging quality drops from 68.58 to 68.10.
- **Catch:** Stronger anchoring still trades motion for consistency and can nearly freeze the scene. Late-arriving objects have no initial reference, and similar multiple subjects challenge the single-subject teacher. Seven-participant preference evidence is small. Two-hour project examples are not the same as the controlled 30/60/120-second benchmark. The project currently labels code as coming soon; no implementation claim from the manuscript's availability wording.
- **Read scope:** Main attention/objective/cache method, benchmark and component ablations, gain sweep, limitations, and selected training details. No exhaustive supplement or code audit. No detailed note today.

### 4. **Open-fMIND: A Large-Scale Open-Access fMRI Corpus for Pretraining Brain Foundation Models**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant neuro AI, representation learning, and reusable data infrastructure. **Verdict:** Useful resource; lighter recommendation with access/reproducibility caveats.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.21215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21215), Carlo Alberto Barbano, Giovanni Marraffini, and Demian Wassermann, November 16, 2026; [dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/cbarbano/open-fmind); [pipeline repository](https://github.com/carloalbertobarbano/open-fmind).
- **Mechanism:** Uniform fMRIPrep preprocessing, 52 atlas views from 14 families, both denoised and independently re-denoisable ROI time series, connectivity matrices, motion metadata, and run-level provenance. Indexed tar shards support selective retrieval rather than downloading every representation. Validate pretraining using a frozen Brain-Semantoks self-distillation encoder and matched downstream probes.
- **Why it matters:** Different parcellations can be controlled views of the same underlying signal rather than separate incompatible datasets. The useful contribution is reusable preprocessing with provenance and confounds—not pretending that 52 versions of one scan are 52 independent samples.
- **Evidence:** 322 source studies, 11,379 subjects counted per dataset, and 45,731 runs; 2.38 million run-atlas pairs in quasiraw. On family-aware HCP evaluation, Open-fMIND versus UK-Biobank pretraining gives sex AUROC 0.945 versus 0.945 and cognition correlation 0.415 versus 0.409. ABIDE diagnosis balanced accuracy is 62.0% versus 63.1%; ADHD-200 diagnosis/age shows no clear gain over random initialization.
- **Catch:** One pretraining run per encoder; cross-validation bars do not measure training-seed uncertainty. UK-Biobank comparison is not a perfectly matched input-stream experiment. Dataset-origin classification reaches 60.3% balanced accuracy versus 0.37% chance, so site/task structure remains substantial. No harmonized phenotypes; ROI-only data loses fine spatial information. At inspection, Hugging Face requires login and agreement to share account information before file access, and the linked pipeline GitHub repository is empty despite the paper's code-release statement. Source-specific licenses include noncommercial/share-alike conditions. No bulk files were downloaded or access terms accepted.
- **Read scope:** Corpus construction, preprocessing/provenance, exclusions, site/subject analyses, pretraining setup/results, usage limitations, and licensing; dataset card and code landing page checked. No full dataset or participant-overlap audit. No detailed note today.

### 5. **Ego2Sim2Humanoid: Agentic Real-to-Sim for Learning Loco-Manipulation VLAs without Robot Demonstrations**

- **Classification:** Directly relevant agentic real2sim2real, explicit contacts, and verified training-data construction. **Verdict:** Useful early evidence; robotics slot, not a general solution.
- **Source:** [arXiv:2611.19196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19196), Chen Liu and Nikolaus Correll, November 14, 2026; [project](https://robonana.github.io/ego2sim2humanoid/). The PDF's short title is Agentic Real-to-Sim for Learning Loco-Manipulation VLAs without Robot Demonstrations.
- **Mechanism:** Turn Aria glasses RGB-D/head/visible-hand recordings into collision and appearance assets plus contact bands. Solve robot-specific grasps and collision-free stances; blend visibility transitions; refine with physics-based tracking RL and semantic rewards. Render the robot viewpoint along accepted simulated rollouts, yielding synchronized images, proprioception, and action labels to fine-tune GR00T N1.7.
- **Why it matters:** This earns the real2sim2real label more than scene reconstruction alone: it trains on converted simulated trajectories and actually executes a downstream VLA on hardware. Contact geometry, embodiment adaptation, and observation/action synchronization do identifiable work.
- **Evidence:** Of 219 bottle and 154 hat recordings, 189 and 59 survive the pipeline. Unitree G1 hardware success is 9/20 bottle lifts and 5/20 hat placements. With the same RL stage, contact-band grasp planning reduces median hat penetration from 1.8 to 0.8 cm over 71 recordings. The paper reports where hardware failures occur rather than presenting successful videos alone.
- **Catch:** Two tasks, small hardware sample, low end-to-end success, heavy hat-data rejection, and no real-robot comparative baseline establishing a broad policy advantage. Zero target-task robot demonstrations is not zero robot priors: pretrained GR00T, SONIC, motion generators, and grasp machinery do substantial work. Physical parameters are guessed from priors, not measured system identification. Rigid/parametric reconstruction excludes difficult deformables. Final dataset review is part of filtering; complete autonomous acceptance is not established. The project says videos and code are coming soon.
- **Read scope:** Main reconstruction/adaptation/correction/rendering pipeline, filtering, contact comparison, hardware outcomes, and limitations; optimizer/reward appendices not exhaustively audited. No detailed note today.

## Novelty / framing / baseline impact

- **Diffusion inference:** Simformer already supplies arbitrary conditioning. Attribute novelty to the HMM-specific token/mask/window adaptation, not the invention of amortized diffusion or generic Bayesian inference. Match simulation and optimizer budgets; compare against accurate non-amortized references and count training cost.
- **World models:** Add matched non-flipping controls and boundary sweeps. Separate edited-image tests from physically recorded outcomes, strict paired success from per-image accuracy, and state extraction from rollout rendering.
- **Generative memory:** Compare anchor-only, correction-only, combined, stronger native attention, and consistency-motion frontiers. Trusted memory is an intervention target, not automatically a neutral cache.
- **Neuro infrastructure:** Split by underlying subject/run before creating atlas views. Compare matched pretraining streams and multiple training seeds; account for source identity and motion. Recheck artifact access rather than repeating open-access or code-release language uncritically.
- **Robotics:** Report conversion yield, simulated contact validity, real grasp success, and task completion separately. Keep mass/friction identification distinct from geometric reconstruction, and name the pretrained robot components behind a zero-demonstration claim.

## One-paragraph takeaway

The common mechanism is a representation that accepts a concrete obligation. A Markov mask must preserve local inference when a record grows; a world model must change its answer at the physical boundary rather than merely animate both images; an appearance memory must resist drift without freezing motion; a brain corpus must preserve provenance and independence across views; a converted robot demonstration must survive contact physics and hardware execution. The strongest papers also show where that obligation fails. Preserve those boundaries alongside the clever representation.

## Other screened directions

**Explicit Regularization for Flow Matching** ([arXiv:2611.19587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19587)) received full-PDF acquisition and targeted formulation, theorem-assumption, spline-solver, and parameter-selection inspection. Its important distinction is derivative regularization versus merely shrinking velocity magnitude. Uniform-in-time stability requires derivative order k with 2k greater than ambient dimension D under its stated assumptions; experiments use one- and two-dimensional spline fields with direct solves, not large neural image models. A worthwhile theory follow-up, not evidence that a Hessian penalty fixes high-dimensional generator memorization.

**Foundation Pareto Flow Policy for Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning** ([arXiv:2611.19825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19825)) received full-PDF acquisition and main mechanism, dataset/protocol, and result-table inspection. It combines preference-conditioned flow matching with broad pretraining, curated mid-training, and few-shot adaptation on D4MORL. The paper's aggregate median gain is much larger for sparsity (+29.44%) than hypervolume (+0.98%); do not sell denser Pareto coverage as a dramatic return improvement. Template-generated language preferences and dataset-relative quality filtering limit a foundation-policy interpretation. This was an explicit non-agent RL/control check, not a title-only exclusion. Neither follow-up gets a canonical note today.
