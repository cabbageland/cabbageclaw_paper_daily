Next Forcing, TacForeSight, and LIBERO-Occ are preserved today.

Next Forcing is the strongest direct hit. Its claim is simple and useful: teacher-forced next-chunk denoising lets high-frame-rate video world models lean on appearance copying, so the model needs a denser target that makes dynamics unavoidable. The method adds multi-chunk prediction modules for next1, next2, and next3 chunks, chains them causally, fuses intermediate main-model layers, and optionally keeps the depth-1 module at inference to generate the next video chunk in parallel. The results are strong on RoboTwin, PhyWorld, and a general video pretraining setting, though the training scale and proprietary data mean I trust the mechanism more than any single headline number.

TacForeSight is the best contact-rich manipulation note. It treats wrist force/torque as a high-frequency global cue that can precede local tactile deformation, then predicts future tactile latents and feeds them into a flow-based policy through current-future tactile cross-attention and adaptive visuo-tactile gating. The real-robot results are much stronger than the baselines across five contact tasks and three perturbation settings. The caveat is that the system assumes force and tactile hardware, and the evaluation is specialized, but the mechanism is exactly the kind of explicit physical foresight worth remembering.

LIBERO-Occ is the strongest VLA evaluation/framing note. The benchmark physically instantiates occluders in LIBERO scenes, verifies visibility/validity/executability, and separates manipulated-object, receptacle, and dual occlusion. The main empirical point is blunt: models that look strong on standard LIBERO collapse under scene-induced occlusion. VIM then imagines a complementary viewpoint from the occluded primary view and conditions action prediction on both observed and generated evidence. It improves substantially, but the paper is honest that this is a simulation benchmark and generated views remain bounded by learned priors.

IMPACT is strong adjacent inspiration and may deserve a later control note. It decouples high-level diffusion-policy planning from low-level force compensation by learning an online internal model from joint torque residuals, then applying feedforward wrench compensation. It is not a VLA/world-model paper in the narrow sense, but it is a very clean example of putting hidden physical state into a control interface instead of expecting imitation learning to absorb payload dynamics implicitly.

MV-Actor is useful citation material for multi-view bimanual perception. It aligns semantic tokens across physically corresponding camera regions, injects feed-forward reconstruction features for spatial awareness, and repairs noisy depth. I am not preserving it as a top note because it is mostly a perception-front-end improvement, while today's stronger thread is predictive hidden-state completion for action.

WorldOlympiad and P3D-Bench are good benchmark papers, not today's core preserved mechanisms. WorldOlympiad is useful for evaluating whether video world models preserve physics, 3D consistency, and interaction state. P3D-Bench is useful for checking whether code-generating MLLMs recover parametric 3D structure rather than merely producing plausible shapes. Both may be worth citing when framing evaluation, but they do not change the architecture/control story as directly as today's top three.

Most relevant today: Next Forcing.

The steal-worthy idea is to attack the supervision target, not only the context window or the sampling schedule. If a world model can solve the training task by copying adjacent appearance, the objective is under-specifying dynamics. Multi-chunk prediction makes the model answer "what keeps changing?" across several horizons, which is closer to the representation needed for planning and rollout.

TacForeSight is the physical-sensing companion. It is not enough to concatenate contact sensors and hope the policy sorts it out. If one signal reliably leads another, make that temporal asymmetry explicit and predict the hidden local state the controller will soon need.

LIBERO-Occ is the robustness companion. A VLA that performs well when all task evidence is visible may not be learning a recoverable state representation. Viewpoint imagination is not magic, but it is a clean intermediate object: an explicit guess about missing evidence that can be trained and inspected.

Next Forcing raises the baseline for autoregressive video world models and WAMs. Papers should now say whether their objective creates pressure for multi-horizon dynamics or mostly rewards near-frame reconstruction. It also gives a practical ablation checklist: timestep shift, multi-layer feature fusion, auxiliary horizon depth, and whether the auxiliary heads can accelerate inference.

TacForeSight raises the baseline for contact-rich manipulation policies using force/tactile sensing. Reactive multimodal fusion is a weak baseline if the claimed problem is contact transition. A stronger method should model how global force cues anticipate future local tactile state and show perturbation recovery, not just nominal success.

LIBERO-Occ raises the baseline for VLA robustness. Occlusion should be physically instantiated and task-executability checked, not simulated as cosmetic image corruption. It also sharpens the evaluation question: does the model infer missing task evidence, or only exploit currently visible objects?

WorldOlympiad is useful evaluation pressure for generative world models: video quality is not enough if physics, geometry, and interaction continuity fail separately.

P3D-Bench is useful evaluation pressure for 3D/code-generation systems: executable and visually plausible parametric code can still fail the real structural target, especially dimensions, topology, and part-level decomposition.

The best paper today is Next Forcing because it changes what a causal video world model is forced to learn: multi-horizon dynamics rather than local chunk copying. TacForeSight is the tactile/contact note to remember because it predicts future tactile latents from force/torque, giving the policy physical foresight instead of reactive fusion. LIBERO-Occ is the VLA robustness note because it turns occlusion into a real partial-observability test and uses imagined complementary views as inspectable missing evidence. The common lesson is that hidden state should not be left as a hope inside a giant latent. Make the missing future, contact, or view into an explicit interface.
