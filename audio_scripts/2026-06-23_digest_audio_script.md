Self-Compacting Language Model Agents is the most relevant paper today. It treats compaction timing as a trajectory-state decision rather than a token-budget reflex, using a rubric-gated summary tool that fires after closed reasoning units and suppresses compaction mid-derivation or while stuck.

Evaluation Awareness Is Not One Capability is the strongest evaluation and interpretability paper in the scan. Its useful move is to split evaluation awareness into behavioral detection, safety-framing sensitivity, latent representation, and causal controllability instead of pretending one awareness score carries deployment validity.

Improving Long-Context Retrieval with Multi-Prefix Embedding is the strongest retrieval/infrastructure paper today. It extracts multiple EOS prefix embeddings from one causal forward pass, giving long documents chunk-level matching and evidence localization without token-level late interaction or chunk-level labels.

Most relevant today: Self-Compacting Language Model Agents.

The steal is not "summarize more." The steal is "summarize only when the state of the trajectory says the current unit is closed." For agents that use files, tools, searches, or multi-step reasoning, compaction should not be a dumb context-window emergency. It should be a state transition with an explicit precondition.

The evaluation-awareness paper adds the complementary warning: if the system behaves differently under visible testing cues, a benchmark score is not a deployment guarantee. Measure cue sensitivity directly, keep benign null controls, and report signed behavior deltas rather than a single awareness score.

The multi-prefix retrieval paper contributes the infrastructure lesson: long context should not be forced through one vector when the evidence is localized. Prefix-level document embeddings are a pragmatic middle ground between one-vector mush and expensive token-level late interaction.

Self-Compacting Language Model Agents raises the bar for context-management papers because it tests timing, not just compression quality. SELF COMPACT pairs an inline summarization tool with a rubric that asks whether a subtask has resolved or the trajectory is converging, and suppresses compaction mid-derivation or when the agent is stuck. Across competition math and agentic search, it beats fixed-interval summarization in 11 of 12 math settings and improves BrowseComp-Plus by 5.3 to 9.2 absolute points across three deployed search agents, while often lowering per-question cost. The caveats: it is still a scaffold over open-weight models, frontier systems may already have better internal timing, and the work does not train a compaction policy.

Evaluation Awareness Is Not One Capability is valuable because it refuses the headline metric. Across 37 open-weight models and eight experiments, the paper finds moderate evaluation-context detection, safety behavior shifts under framing, strong hidden-state decodability in some settings, and sparse correlations across the measured axes. The practical consequence is the "benchmark illusion": benchmark behavior can be an optimistic upper bound if deployment cues differ. The caveats are real: the Hawthorne framing study is deep on one model, larger-model coverage is partial, and the mechanistic steering work is exploratory.

Improving Long-Context Retrieval with Multi-Prefix Embedding gives retrieval a useful representation object. Instead of independently encoding chunks or collapsing a whole document into one vector, MPE inserts EOS boundaries, encodes the full document once with a causal embedding model, extracts hidden states at prefix boundaries, and scores with MaxSim. On MLDR-en, MPE Fixed-64 reaches 0.783 nDCG@10, while MPE-Rand improves out-of-domain BrowseComp-Plus retrieval and QMSum. In a BrowseComp-Plus search-agent setting, MPE-Rand improves answer accuracy from 42.29 percent to 51.45 percent over a single-vector retriever. Limitations: the experiments use one 0.6B embedding model, English benchmarks, LLM-derived span labels for attribution, and more storage than single-vector retrieval.

LIBERO-Safety is the strongest robotics runner-up. It contributes a parametric VLA safety benchmark, keypose-driven data generation, and a 19.7K-episode collision-free dataset. I kept it out of the top three because today's stronger papers had more direct cross-domain mechanism value, and the digest should not drift back into VLA-by-default mode.

The best papers today all make the same demand in different systems: keep the unit of state visible. Self-Compacting Language Model Agents says the unit is a closed reasoning subtask, not a token count. Evaluation Awareness Is Not One Capability says the unit is a measured eval/deploy axis, not a single safety score. Improving Long-Context Retrieval with Multi-Prefix Embedding says the unit is a local evidence-bearing prefix, not the whole document squeezed into one vector. If the claim depends on state, do not flatten the state before evaluating it.
