They put pressure on the exact object that is supposed to carry the claim: a diffusion language model's inter-step bottleneck, a soft-routed MoE's aggregate confidence, or a 3D editor's source-to-target box specification.

How Transparent is DiffusionGemma? is the most relevant paper today. It turns the vague worry that text diffusion models may reason opaquely in latent space into a set of auditable measurements: opaque serial depth, bottleneck token ablations, monitorability, and case studies of non-autoregressive reasoning.

Toward Calibrated Mixture-of-Experts Under Distribution Shift is the strongest calibration paper in the scan. Its useful move is the distinction between hard-routing robustness and soft-routing fragility: calibrated experts do not guarantee calibrated aggregate probabilities once routing configurations get reweighted.

Thinking in Boxes is the best 3D/generative media paper today. It uses 3D boxes as an actual edit specification, not a decorative condition. The source and target boxes plus a depth-aligned floor give the generator a compact geometric contract for translation, rotation, scale, disocclusion, and camera change.

Several runner-ups were useful but stayed below the note line. GEN-Guard is a strong healthcare/deployment paper: client-blocked evaluation exposes model-selection failures in federated surgical AI, and disagreement-aware distillation gives a practical post-hoc correction. I kept it as a runner-up because the correction is more deployment hygiene than a broadly reusable modeling primitive. TimeProVe is a sensible propose-then-verify system for long ADL video QA, but it depends heavily on action detectors and heuristic evidence ranking. The Token Is a Group Element has a sharp geometric premise, but the empirical evidence is still toy sequence completion. SoftSkill is directly interesting for skill systems, but its strongest results are single-round QA; the paper itself admits long-horizon agentic compression is not robust yet.

Most relevant today: How Transparent is DiffusionGemma?

The steal is the audit shape. Cabbageland should treat latent reasoning systems the way this paper treats DiffusionGemma: identify the hidden bottleneck, bound how much serial computation bypasses readable states, test whether the bottleneck can be compressed into interpretable symbols, and then check whether those symbols are actually useful to a monitor. The important part is not that DiffusionGemma looks fine today. The important part is that future latent systems now have a template they can fail.

The MoE calibration paper contributes a second reusable lens: do not trust modularity at the component level when the router creates a many-to-one aggregate. The calibration claim has to be tested at the routed aggregate, under shifts that stress the configurations where experts disagree.

Thinking in Boxes contributes the control-interface lesson. A generative model can be powerful and still need a small explicit state object to make user intent unambiguous. The box-and-floor representation is not aesthetically subtle, but it gives the model a job it can actually condition on.

How Transparent is DiffusionGemma? raises the bar for latent-reasoning transparency work. The paper reports that DiffusionGemma's opaque serial depth is 28.6x Gemma 4 26B A4B if the inter-step bottleneck is treated as uninterpretable, but 1.1x if the bottleneck is interpretable. Its top-k and probability-threshold ablations show that k = 8 or p = 0.03 can match baseline capability on the tested benchmarks, and the token-identity analysis suggests most load-bearing intermediate states are guesses for current or neighboring final tokens. The caveat is important: multi-canvas monitorability may hide single-canvas regressions, and "mapped latent vector looks token-like" is not the same as guaranteed faithful reasoning.

Toward Calibrated Mixture-of-Experts Under Distribution Shift is valuable because the theory matches the practical failure mode. Hard routing creates an expert-confidence bottleneck; reweighting routing regions can preserve calibration if each expert-confidence slice remains reliable. Soft routing collapses routing weights and expert outputs into a scalar confidence, so many configurations can share the same confidence while having different outcome frequencies. The proposed Robust MoE and Robust Filtered objectives improve ECE on shifted or ambiguous subsets across CIFAR-10H, PACS, and CivilComments. The limitations are scale and proxy choice: the experiments use compact four-expert MoEs, and high proper loss is only a proxy for routing-induced calibration error.

Thinking in Boxes is useful because the explicit structure is doing the work. Prior box/depth/primitive methods often use 3D cues as approximate conditions or cleanup scaffolds; this paper treats source and target boxes as the edit itself. The strongest baseline impact is on large spatial edits, especially disocclusion and object/camera motion. The caveat is that the representation is only as good as the fitted boxes and floor estimate. When similar-scale boxes are ambiguous, the model can collapse to the identity transformation.

GEN-Guard is the strongest runner-up for healthcare deployment. It identifies performance leakage as a model-selection failure, reports MSFs in 38 of 46 experiments, and improves worst-case F1 in Multi-Cholec and PolypGen. The useful idea is client-blocked evaluation before deployment. The caveat is that CBE only works as well as the blocked validation clients represent the deployment shift.

The best papers today all refuse to let the hidden part stay hand-wavy. How Transparent is DiffusionGemma? asks whether a latent diffusion language model's inter-step state is readable enough to monitor. Toward Calibrated Mixture-of-Experts Under Distribution Shift asks whether the routed aggregate remains calibrated when soft-routing configurations shift. Thinking in Boxes asks whether a 3D editor can be controlled by an explicit geometric specification instead of prompt vibes. Different domains, same standard: find the bottleneck, state what it must preserve, and test the claim at that bottleneck instead of admiring the system around it.
