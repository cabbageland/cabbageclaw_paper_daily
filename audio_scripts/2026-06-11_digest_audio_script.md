Today's useful pattern is the world-action interface is becoming the actual research object. Making Foresight Actionable is the strongest paper because it does not accept "the world model made a plausible video" as evidence that the action head can use the representation. It probes where action attention and causal sensitivity land, then regularizes WAM hidden states toward spatially coherent DINOv2 features so the action decoder reads task-relevant interaction regions. World Pilot is the complementary integration recipe: route a WAM's scene-evolution latent into VLA hidden states and its anticipated trajectory into the action generator as a soft prior. DAM-VLA is the sensor-time version of the same idea: stop forcing vision, language, force, proprioception, and action onto one synchronous clock, and let each modality keep its own buffer and update rhythm.

AGRA, World Pilot, and DAM-VLA are preserved today.

Making Foresight Actionable is the strongest direct hit. The paper diagnoses a concrete failure mode in world-action models: a WAM can generate a plausible future while the action decoder attends to background, static hands, or other task-irrelevant regions. AGRA adds an auxiliary alignment loss between selected video-diffusion hidden states and DINOv2 patch features, which makes the feature field more spatially coherent and action-readable. The paper's best contribution is not the headline success number; it is the attention plus causal-intervention audit that turns "world model features" into something testable.

World Pilot is the best companion integration paper. It keeps the WAM frozen and injects two priors into a VLA: Latent Steering routes the scene-evolution latent through residual cross-attention into VLM hidden states, while Action Steering compresses the WAM's anticipated trajectory into a single prefix token for the flow-matching action generator. The ablations are useful: latent injection beats decoded future images, a single trajectory token beats per-step or raw trajectory conditioning, and even a scene-prediction-only Cosmos model helps before action post-training.

DAM-VLA is the strongest asynchronous multimodal VLA paper. Its argument is clean: a synchronous VLA oversamples slow vision and language, undersamples fast contact signals, and gates action generation on a complete observation bundle. DAM-VLA keeps per-modality latent buffers, refreshes them at their sensor rates, and lets the action head read them continuously through gated cross-attention. The real-robot evidence is small but sharp: seven contact-rich tasks, 15 trials each, and a large gap between the full model and synchronous or concatenation baselines.

PLUME is strong adjacent inspiration and may deserve a later dedicated note. It learns a latent belief over physical parameters and rewards, updates that belief online with a flow-matching world model, and plans with likelihood-penalized rollouts. The hardware screwdriver result is especially relevant for explicit physical state. I am not preserving it today because the top three form a tighter VLA/WAM interface story, not because PLUME is weak.

DIRECT is strong systems framing for embodied planner deployment. The key point is that test-time compute has different capability profiles depending on whether it is reasoning depth, model size, or memory history. The router is useful, but it is more about compute allocation for high-level planners than about state/action representation.

Loop-OWM is useful compositional-reasoning inspiration. It frames ARC as object-centric transition learning over structured visual-symbolic states. I am not promoting it above today's robotics papers because the internal recurrent steps are not yet semantically stable, and the result is more a controlled reasoning lens than an embodied mechanism.

World Model Self-Distillation, VICX, VLGA, ATM, SG-PVR, ISAP-3D, and CP4D are all citation material for narrower purposes: data-free video-world-model task transfer, video-plan-to-trajectory grounding, dense driving geometry supervision, latent-world-model diagnostics, structured video reward reasoning, identity-slot 3D parts, and physics-aware 4D composition. None displaced the three preserved notes today.

Most relevant today: Making Foresight Actionable.

The steal-worthy idea is to audit the interface, not just the rollout. If a world model's hidden states are supposed to guide action, ask whether perturbing task-critical interaction tokens changes the action more than perturbing background tokens. Ask whether action-head attention lands on the contact site. If the answer is no, the system has a world-model branding problem, not a working action interface.

World Pilot then shows how to route priors when the interface is usable: scene-evolution latents belong near perception tokens, while coarse motion priors belong near the action generator. DAM-VLA adds the timing lesson: a modality is not just another input field. It has a natural update rate, retention horizon, and failure mode.

Making Foresight Actionable raises the baseline for WAM papers. A plausible generated future is no longer enough. Papers should show whether the action decoder uses the future representation in task-relevant regions, and whether background perturbations causally disturb actions.

World Pilot raises the baseline for WAM-to-VLA fusion. Future images are a weak interface if the latent already contains the useful dynamics and the decoded image adds artifacts. The ablation that a single trajectory-level token beats per-step trajectory conditioning is also useful: priors should steer, not shackle, the action generator.

DAM-VLA raises the baseline for multimodal VLAs with force, tactile, or proprioceptive streams. Synchronous concatenation is now a suspect baseline for contact-rich tasks. A stronger comparison needs native-rate buffers, sparse visual re-encoding, and modality-specific gates.

PLUME is worth citing when the claim is about physical parameter uncertainty. If a task's correct strategy depends on friction, object shape, or resistance, a parameter-agnostic policy is a weak baseline.

DIRECT is useful when arguing that "more thinking" or "more memory" should be routed by task demand rather than paid on every step.

The best paper today is Making Foresight Actionable because it makes the world-action interface inspectable: a useful future representation should focus the action head on interaction regions and be causally robust to irrelevant background changes. World Pilot is the matching integration recipe, routing WAM latents and trajectory priors into different parts of a VLA. DAM-VLA generalizes the same taste to multimodal timing, with per-modality buffers and gated updates instead of synchronous token mush. The common lesson is blunt: a "world model" only matters when its hidden state reaches the control computation in the right form, at the right layer, and at the right time.
