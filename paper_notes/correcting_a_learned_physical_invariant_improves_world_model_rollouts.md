## One-paragraph overview

The paper studies a frozen DreamerV3 world model trained only on pendulum video and asks whether its latent transition learns an approximately conserved scalar analogous to physical energy. The author searches a degree-4 polynomial family over projected latent coordinates for candidates that change little under the model's own one-step transition, then checks whether the recovered scalar is reproducible across trained conservative models, disappears under matched damped dynamics, and actually matters for rollout quality. The key result is yes on all three fronts: conservative models recover a reproducible energy-like invariant, damped models do not, and projecting latent states back toward the invariant level set reduces rollout error on conservative systems while matched random constraints usually make things worse.

## Model definition

### Inputs
Latent states from a frozen DreamerV3 world model trained on 64 x 64 pendulum video, together with one-step latent transitions generated by that model during autonomous rollout.

### Outputs
A scalar candidate invariant defined as a degree-4 polynomial over projected latent coordinates, plus corrected latent rollout states produced by projecting the latent back toward the invariant level set.

### Training objective (loss)
The paper does not introduce a new trainable neural architecture. The underlying DreamerV3 world model is pretrained and frozen. The paper's main optimization is a label-free search over a degree-4 polynomial family that minimizes change under the model's own latent transition, followed by selection and validation against held-out trajectories and energy correlation.

### Architecture / parameterization
Frozen DreamerV3 world model without actor or critic, trained only on pendulum video. The invariant search operates on projected latent coordinates using a degree-4 polynomial family.

## Key questions this summary must address

### 1. What problem is the paper trying to solve?
It is trying to distinguish a dynamically meaningful latent invariant from a mere post hoc correlate that happens to line up with a physical variable when decoded from latent space.

### 2. What is the method?
Recover a candidate scalar from the latent state by searching a restricted polynomial family for functions that are approximately preserved under the model's own one-step transition, compare the result across conservative and damped training regimes, and then intervene during autonomous rollout by projecting latent states back toward the recovered level set.

### 3. What is the method motivation?
A world model can predict video well enough to look physically competent while still letting important dynamical quantities drift during imagination. If that drift is real, correcting it should improve rollout quality. If the quantity is a fake correlate, the correction should not help and may hurt.

### 4. What data does it use?
Gymnasium pendulum video in two regimes: conservative dynamics and matched damped dynamics. The core experiments use three trained conservative seeds, three matched damped seeds, and six random initializations for sanity checks.

### 5. How is it evaluated?
By measuring latent invariance statistics, comparing conservative and damped systems, and testing whether enforcing the recovered invariant changes 50-step rollout error relative to no edit, matched random polynomial constraints, and damped-model constraints.

### 6. What are the main results?
Randomly initialized DreamerV3 models can correlate with true energy up to 0.908, which is a useful warning that correlation alone is weak evidence. The conservative models recover candidates with held-out invariance ratios of 0.0027-0.0121, while damped models jump to 0.983-0.994 and vary about 2400x more within trajectories. When the recovered invariant is enforced during rollout, error drops by 2.9%, 3.3%, and 3.5% across the three conservative seeds. The same edit on damped models changes error by +0.5%, and matched random constraints usually increase error.

### 7. What is actually novel?
The novelty is the intervention logic. Many probing papers stop at decodability. This one asks whether the model's own transition preserves the quantity and whether making the latent state respect it improves imagined futures.

### 8. What are the strengths?
It is small but clean. The conservative-versus-damped comparison is the right negative control, and the rollout-correction experiment is much stronger evidence than a regression from latents to energy. The paper also explicitly shows that high energy correlation can appear in random untrained models, which is the kind of sanity check too many interpretability papers skip.

### 9. What are the weaknesses, limitations, or red flags?
This is still a pendulum paper. The system is tiny, the invariant family is hand-restricted, and the rollout correction is evaluated on the same small regime rather than on a broad distribution of complex environments. The paper also acknowledges that the intervention evidence is in-sample relative to the chosen trajectories, so one should not pretend this already solves invariant discovery for general world models.

### 10. What challenges or open problems remain?
Finding meaningful invariants in larger latent spaces with multiple interacting objects, partial observability, and nonconservative dynamics where the relevant structure is not a scalar energy-like quantity.

### 11. What future work naturally follows?
Invariant-discovery pipelines for richer world models, rollout-correction schemes that work in higher-dimensional physical systems, and diagnostics that test whether latent constraints are merely decodable, transition-preserved, or actually causally useful for planning.

### 12. Why does this matter for cabbageland?
Because cabbageland cares about explicit state and about world models that behave mechanistically rather than only visually. This paper provides a crisp recipe for checking whether a latent quantity is part of the model's operative dynamics or merely sitting there as a readable byproduct.

### 13. What ideas are steal-worthy?
Use matched dynamical counterexamples, not just decoding metrics. Evaluate latent quantities by transition preservation and rollout intervention. Treat "correlates with a physical variable" as weak evidence unless the quantity also improves the model when enforced.

### 14. Final decision
Keep as a preserved note. It is a narrow paper, but the question it asks is exactly the right one.

## 6. Mandatory critical angles

The paper is strongest on mechanism, failure-mode diagnosis, and explicit dynamical structure. It earns the world-model label because it probes the model's own transition, not just a static representation. The biggest limitation is transferability: pendulum results are useful as a standard, not as proof that the method scales.
