## One-paragraph overview

Standard trajectory SFT for language agents trains on action tokens and masks environment observations. ActObs changes only the label mask: it also trains the model to predict the observation tokens already present in the same trajectories. The deployed policy still emits actions, not observations, but the extra objective forces the shared representation to model action consequences. After the same GRPO procedure, ActObs initializations retain more entropy, move less from their SFT checkpoint, solve more tasks at larger sampling budgets, and transfer better to code-editing tasks.

## Model definition

### Inputs

The model receives multi-turn terminal-agent trajectories: a task prompt, assistant action turns containing reasoning and commands, and environment observations such as terminal output.

### Outputs

At deployment the model outputs action tokens. During ActObs SFT, the loss also targets observation tokens in the trajectory, so the model is trained to predict both what the agent did and what the environment returned.

### Training objective (loss)

For action token indices A and observation token indices O, ActObs minimizes language-modeling loss over both sets:

* action loss plus lambda-weighted observation loss, divided by the weighted token count
* lambda = 0 recovers action-only SFT
* the default ActObs uses lambda = 1

The downstream RL step is standard GRPO unless otherwise noted.

### Architecture / parameterization

Experiments fine-tune Qwen3-4B and Qwen3-8B. SFT uses 50k multi-turn terminal trajectories from the synthetic portion of Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus. GRPO uses 2,392 containerized Endless Terminals tasks.

## Key questions this summary must address

### 1. What problem is the paper trying to solve?

Agent SFT uses observations as context but does not learn to predict them. The paper asks whether that convention is a bad initialization for later reinforcement learning.

### 2. What is the method?

ActObs unmasks observation tokens already present in trajectories and includes them in the SFT loss. It adds no data, parameters, sequence tokens, forward passes, or RL changes.

### 3. What is the method motivation?

Predicting observations trains the model on action consequences. That should preserve environment modeling and prevent SFT from collapsing too hard onto imitation of the teacher's next command.

### 4. What data does it use?

SFT uses 50k terminal trajectories, about 0.71B tokens, generated by DeepSeek-V3.2 in Nemotron-Terminal-Corpus. GRPO trains on Endless Terminals tasks. Evaluation uses Terminal-Bench 2.0 and aider-polyglot.

### 5. How is it evaluated?

The paper reports pass@k on Terminal-Bench 2.0 with 89 tasks and 16 attempts per task, plus aider-polyglot with 225 code-editing tasks and four attempts per task. It also tracks entropy, policy movement, observation-prediction gradients, and controls such as Obs-to-Act and ECHO.

### 6. What are the main results?

After GRPO at Qwen3-4B, ActObs reaches 7.2 pass@1, 12.5 pass@4, 15.5 pass@8, and 19.1 pass@16 on Terminal-Bench 2.0, versus 5.6, 11.1, 14.0, and 18.0 for action-only SFT. On aider-polyglot at 4B, ActObs reaches 13.9 pass@1 and 25.3 pass@4, versus 9.7 and 20.4. At 8B, ActObs trades away pass@1 on Terminal-Bench but gains at larger k, reaching 27.0 pass@16 versus 23.6 for action-only.

### 7. What is actually novel?

The novelty is showing that observation tokens in trajectories are not just context; they are free consequence-supervision targets whose inclusion changes downstream RL behavior.

### 8. What are the strengths?

The intervention is minimal, the controls are sensible, and the paper analyzes mechanism instead of stopping at pass@k. The entropy and policy-movement results make the claim more interesting than a leaderboard bump.

### 9. What are the weaknesses, limitations, or red flags?

The task family is terminal/coding-heavy, so the transfer to embodied agents or non-text environments is not established. The result also shifts performance toward pass@k exploration, and at 8B it can reduce pass@1 reliability.

### 10. What challenges or open problems remain?

The core open question is how to tune observation supervision for a desired exploration/reliability tradeoff. It is also unclear how much the method depends on observation text being directly predictable by a language model.

### 11. What future work naturally follows?

Apply ActObs-style masking to multimodal and robotics trajectories, combine it with learned world-model heads, and test whether observation supervision improves calibration or planning under longer horizons.

### 12. Why does this matter for cabbageland?

Cabbageland cares about agents that preserve causal consequences, not just command imitation. ActObs is a small, stealable loss-mask pattern for keeping transition information alive before RL.

### 13. What ideas are steal-worthy?

Do not discard observation tokens as mere context. Treat expert trajectories as both policy demonstrations and transition data. Measure whether pre-RL objectives leave useful entropy for exploration.

### 14. Final decision

Preserve. The paper is agent-heavy, but the mechanism is concrete enough to keep.
