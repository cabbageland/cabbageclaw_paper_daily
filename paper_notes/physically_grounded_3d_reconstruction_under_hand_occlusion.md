# Physically Grounded 3D Generative Reconstruction under Hand Occlusion using Proprioception and Multi-Contact Touch

## Basic info

* Authors: Gabriele Mario Caddeo, Pasquale Marra, Lorenzo Natale
* Year: 2026
* Venue / source: arXiv
* Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09100
* Date surfaced: 2026-04-13
* Why selected in one sentence: It uses proprioception and tactile contact as explicit geometric constraints inside a generative 3D reconstruction pipeline instead of asking vision-only priors to hallucinate through severe occlusion.

## Quick verdict

**Highly relevant**

This is one of the better recent examples of adding real structure where reconstruction is actually underdetermined. The useful move is not merely multimodality; it is the decision to represent object geometry in a hand-aligned SDF space and enforce contact and non-interpenetration during generation. I inspected the abstract and substantial HTML introduction/method text, but I did not read the full PDF appendix, so some experimental and optimization details remain uncertain.

## One-paragraph overview

The paper tackles in-hand object reconstruction when the hand blocks much of the object from view. Instead of relying only on RGB and hoping a learned prior fills in the rest, it uses known hand pose from proprioception and observed contact locations from tactile sensing as extra evidence about where the object surface can and cannot be. Geometry is represented as a camera-aligned, pose-aware SDF in the same 3D domain as the hand, encoded into a latent space by a Structure-VAE, and then generated by a conditional flow-matching model. Physics-based contact-consistency and non-interpenetration terms are used during finetuning and inference so the reconstructions are not just visually plausible but physically compatible with the interaction.

## Model definition

### Inputs
Visible RGB observation of the object, hand and visibility masks, a latent representation of the posed hand derived from proprioception, and a tactile or contact representation encoding observed touch points. The model is trained first on vision-only object images, then finetuned on occluded manipulation scenes with these multimodal inputs.

### Outputs
A latent representation that decodes into a pose-aware, camera-aligned signed distance field for the object, effectively yielding metric-scale amodal geometry and implicit pose in the hand-centered 3D domain. The paper also describes downstream refinement and texturing modules that can use this structure estimate afterward.

### Training objective (loss)
From the accessible text, the generator is trained in latent space with a conditional flow-matching objective over Structure-VAE latents. On top of that, the method adds physics-derived objectives: a contact-consistency loss that pulls the reconstructed surface toward observed contacts, and a non-interpenetration loss that pushes the object outside the hand volume. Those same terms are also used as decoder-guidance during inference. I did not inspect the full formula details in the PDF.

### Architecture / parameterization
A hybrid multimodal generative stack: a Structure-VAE for compact latent SDF representation, a conditional flow-matching diffusion-style model operating in latent space, and decoder-side physics guidance. The core scene representation is a pose-aware, camera-aligned SDF aligned with the hand geometry.

## Key questions this summary must address

### 1. What problem is the paper trying to solve?
Single-view in-hand reconstruction under severe hand occlusion is fundamentally ambiguous. Vision-only methods can output shapes that look reasonable but violate scale, contact, or free-space constraints, which makes them weak for manipulation. The paper aims to reconstruct object geometry and pose in a way that stays physically consistent with the hand-object interaction.

### 2. What is the method?
- Represent object geometry as a camera-aligned, pose-aware SDF in the same metric 3D space as the hand.
- Learn a compact latent space for these SDFs with a Structure-VAE.
- Train a conditional flow-matching model to infer the latent geometry from RGB, masks, hand pose, and touch.
- Add a contact-consistency loss so the generated object agrees with observed tactile contacts.
- Add a non-interpenetration loss so the object does not unrealistically occupy the hand interior.
- Reuse these physics terms as guidance during sampling, not just during training.
- Hand the resulting physically grounded structure to a downstream refinement/texturing stage if needed.

### 3. What is the method motivation?
The motivation is simple and correct: the moments when geometry matters most for manipulation are the moments when vision is least reliable. Contact and known hand kinematics contain exactly the missing evidence vision lacks. If the reconstruction is supposed to be useful for control rather than just rendering, those constraints should shape the generation process directly.

### 4. What data does it use?
From the accessible text, the model is pretrained on vision-only object images to learn a shape prior, then finetuned on occluded manipulation scenes in simulation using RGB, hand pose, and tactile observations. The paper also reports transfer to a real humanoid robot with a different end-effector from training. I did not inspect the full dataset construction details.

### 5. How is it evaluated?
It is evaluated mainly on simulated in-hand reconstruction under severe occlusion, comparing multimodal variants against vision-only baselines on reconstruction quality and physical plausibility. The paper also reports real-robot transfer to test whether the learned structure survives a change in embodiment.

### 6. What are the main results?
The headline result is that adding proprioception and touch improves amodal completion under heavy occlusion and yields reconstructions that better respect physical feasibility and real-world scale than vision-only baselines. The real-robot deployment result also matters: the method appears to transfer to a humanoid platform with a different end-effector, which is a decent sanity check against overfitting to one hand geometry. I did not verify every metric table from the appendix.

### 7. What is actually novel?
The real novelty is not “multimodal reconstruction” in the abstract. It is the specific decision to fuse proprioception and multi-contact touch as explicit geometric constraints inside a latent generative SDF pipeline, then use those same physical constraints again at inference time for decoder guidance. That is a tighter integration of interaction evidence than just concatenating more sensors and hoping for the best.

### 8. What are the strengths?
- The extra modalities are structurally justified, not thrown in for buzzword coverage.
- The SDF representation gives the physics constraints somewhere meaningful to act.
- Contact and non-interpenetration are the right kinds of constraints for manipulation-facing reconstruction.
- The method targets a genuinely hard and practically relevant failure mode of monocular 3D inference.
- Real-robot transfer, even if limited, is much better than a simulation-only story.

### 9. What are the weaknesses, limitations, or red flags?
- The method still depends on tactile sensing and reasonably accurate hand pose, which narrows deployment convenience.
- The accessible text does not make it fully clear how robust the method is when contacts are sparse, noisy, or partially wrong.
- The representation is specialized to hand-object interaction geometry; it is not a generic world model.
- Since I did not inspect the full appendix, I cannot yet judge how strong the baselines and ablations are in detail.
- There is some pipeline complexity here: VAE latent space, flow matching, multimodal conditioning, and downstream refinement.

### 10. What challenges or open problems remain?
The big open problem is how to scale this kind of physically grounded reconstruction beyond carefully instrumented hand-object setups. Another challenge is active perception: deciding where to touch next or how to move the hand to reduce uncertainty rather than only reconstructing from passively observed contacts.

### 11. What future work naturally follows?
- Extend the framework to active tactile exploration rather than fixed-contact reconstruction.
- Learn uncertainty estimates over the reconstructed geometry so downstream control knows what is still ambiguous.
- Generalize from single-object in-hand scenes to cluttered contact-rich scenes.
- Replace some hand-specific assumptions with more reusable contact-centric scene representations.

### 12. Why does this matter for cabbageland?
Because it is a clean example of replacing priors-as-hallucination with priors-plus-evidence. The paper adds explicit state exactly where the problem is underconstrained, and it uses physically meaningful losses rather than aesthetic plausibility. That is the kind of mechanism we should keep stealing from.

### 13. What ideas are steal-worthy?
- Treat touch and hand pose as geometric constraints, not just extra encoder channels.
- Use a representation whose gradients support physical guidance directly, such as an SDF.
- Apply consistency constraints during inference, not only during training.
- Align the generated structure to the manipulator’s coordinate frame so pose estimation becomes partly implicit.

### 14. Final decision
**Preserve it.** This is not a universal solution, but it makes a strong local argument for physically constrained generative inference under occlusion, and the argument is useful well beyond this exact setup.
